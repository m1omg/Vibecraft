**Conventions (same as the siblings):**
- Durations are ms. HUD sizes are px on the **1600 × 900 reference canvas** (architecture §11), scaled by the HUD content scale. World distances are metres; `d` is the current camera distance.
- Colours are sRGB hex. The UI never uses HDR. Only 3D VFX bloom.
- Easings are the Penner functions in `Juice` (combat FND-1): `out_quad`, `in_quad`, `out_cubic`, `in_cubic`, `in_out_sine`, `out_back(c1)`, `in_back`, `out_bounce`. **This doc adds `in_out_cubic`, `out_quart` and `out_quint` to `Juice`.** Named curves POP_IN, PUNCH, SHAKE, RISE_FADE, TIMBER_SNAP, FELT_BOB and BRONZE_RING are the economy doc's `JuiceCurves` (§2.4). Springs are FND-1's closed-form `Juice.spring`, evaluated from a start state (so a step and an impulse use the same function), and `Juice.spring_track`. Peaks of 60 ms or less are **latched** (combat §2): they start at their peak value, so a 30 fps frame always shows them.
- "Own" means the local player.
- **★** marks a twist WC3 did not have.
- Faction ids `kingdom` (Brennmark) and `clans` (the Tormai; the UI never shows "Clans"). The HUD skin, cursor, UI sounds, **UI motion preset (§3.1)** and advisor always follow the **local player's** faction.

## 1. Feel goals

The HUD, the camera and the mixer are the player's **hands and ears**. They should feel like a well-made tool on a craftsman's bench: instant, weighty and quiet while nothing matters, and impossible to miss when something does.

- **Answer in the same frame.** Every click is answered on the input frame by the button, the cursor and a voice or a tick. Then the feedback gets out of the way.
- **A steady hand.** The camera leaves at speed, stops firmly and never drifts. It only shakes when the ground itself heaves in front of you.
- **A curated mix.** One voice at a time, ranked by importance, muffled when off-screen. Alarms sound like nothing else. The music breathes the way WC3's did, with silence between songs.
- **One rhythm for the whole HUD.** Pulses share three locked tempos, and at most two things pulse at once, so the periphery never shimmers.

WC3 got the bones right: talking portraits, the unit voices, the horn and the minimap ping, the Space jump, the sun/moon orb and the score screen. We keep every one of those conventions. Our juice goes into **timing curves, honesty and material**. The tactile layer is oak, brass and burgundy leather for Brennmark, and felt, bronze and saffron stitching for the Tormai, and the material is in the **motion**, not only the colour: a Brennish button latches back with a crisp brass snap, a Tormai button gives like felt and recovers without a rebound. Never neon, glass or glitter.

**Pillars**
1. **Same-frame answer.** Input feedback fires on the input event, not on the next sim tick. The sim catches up 1–3 ticks later, and the UI never waits for it. Anything that must answer input on the same frame is drawn by the OS cursor, by a Control on the input frame, or played as **2D** audio (a 3D player only starts on the next physics tick, §2).
2. **The camera moves only for a reason.** It moves when the player asked (pan, zoom, jump) or when a whitelisted ground-heaving event happens in view (§5.4). Never on its own initiative, never for routine hits.
3. **Calm by default, loud by exception.** Attention is a budget:
   - Routine feedback is short, small and low in the mix.
   - Alarms are rare, throttled per rules §17.3, shape-coded and sound like nothing positive.
   - Anything visible on screen does not also get shouted.
   - At most two HUD elements pulse at once (UCA-F5).
4. **Honest.** Counters and bars are exact; only trailing "ghosts" lag. No event is announced before the sim says it happened. Nothing reveals what the local player cannot see: no shake, ping, sound, hover, cursor change or preview from fogged or invisible enemies (UCA-F4).
5. **Crafted, not generic.** Each row has its own curve tied to a physical idea (a latch, a stamp, a bell, felt, ink). **The two factions share time budgets and differ in material, and material includes motion** (§3.1). Overshoot is a spice used by three families only (§2).
6. **Refresh-rate proof.** Everything is closed-form in elapsed time or exact exponential (§2). 30, 60, 144 and 240 Hz give identical results.

## 2. Ground rules (bind every row)

**Time**
- Camera, HUD, cursor and audio scheduling run in `_process(delta)`, never in `_physics_process`, and read time from **`Clock`**, a new autoload with `process_priority = −1000` and `process_mode = ALWAYS` that runs before every other node each frame. It publishes:
  - `Clock.ui_now` / `Clock.ui_dt`: real, unscaled seconds from `Time.get_ticks_usec()` deltas (each frame's dt clamped to 0.1 s), which keep running while paused and ignore game speed and `Engine.time_scale`;
  - `Clock.fx_now`: visual game time (stops while paused, scales with game speed and with `visual_rate` after the match, SEQ-02).
  `Vfx.ui_now` and `Vfx.now` (the siblings' `ui_time` / `fx_time`) become read-only forwards of these, so every reader in a frame sees the same timestamp.
- HUD Tweens are created on the control they animate (`create_tween()`), with `set_ignore_time_scale(true)`. The HUD's `process_mode` is ALWAYS.
- Smoothing uses `Juice.damp` or the closed forms below, never `lerp(a, b, k)` per frame.
- **Velocity approach** (used for camera pan). It is exact for any dt, given a goal velocity that is constant within the frame:
  `x' = x + v_g·dt + (v − v_g)(1 − e^(−k·dt))/k`, `v' = v_g + (v − v_g)·e^(−k·dt)`.
- **Exponential approach** (zoom, height follow): `x' = x_g + (x − x_g)·e^(−k·dt)`.
- **Springs** use the closed form `Juice.spring` (combat FND-1).
- **Mouse-driven motion** reads `Input.get_last_mouse_velocity()` (smoothed by the engine over ≈ 0.1 s), never per-frame position deltas, which quantise differently at 60 and 240 Hz.
- **3D audio starts on the next physics tick** (Godot 4.7.2 starts `AudioStreamPlayer3D` playback and updates its panning in `NOTIFICATION_INTERNAL_PHYSICS_PROCESS`, i.e. at 30 Hz). Acks, order ticks, UI sounds, the placement stamp and alarms are therefore 2D.
- Juice reads the sim and never writes it:
  - Camera position, HUD state and audio never feed the sim, except through the player's own commands (unchanged).
  - No row touches `World.rng` or sim timing.
  - **`Engine.time_scale` is 1.0 for the whole match.** The only exception is SEQ-02's held breath, which sets it to `visual_rate` after `match_ended` has fired **and** World stepping has halted through World's pause flag (so `interpolation_fraction()` is frozen at 1). It returns to 1.0 when the Chronicle opens, by which time 3D rendering is off (SEQ-02). A test asserts 1.0 on every tick before `match_ended` (§8).

**Readability**
- HUD animations never reflow the layout. Every juiced control sets `offset_transform_visual_only = true` and animates the `offset_transform_*` properties, with the pivot at the centre (bottom edge for the felt press, §3.1).
- The central 50 % of the playfield never receives HUD overlays. The exceptions are the cursor, the drag box, the error line (bottom-centre, small) and the end banner. This matches the ULT-01 rule that the centre is never coloured.
- Text over the world is at least 13 px, with a 2 px ink outline (#14110D). World-anchored text (the hover nameplate, group numerals) is drawn as 2D Labels on a **WorldLabels** CanvasLayer (layer 1, after the screen-FX node), placed with `camera.unproject_position()` and hidden when the point is behind the camera: pixel-exact, in the HUD font and outline pipeline.
- Every state difference is **shape-coded** as well as colour-coded:
  - notched rings mean enemy or alert;
  - dashed rings mean hover;
  - hatching means unaffordable;
  - the lock glyph means a missing requirement;
  - notched strips mean low HP.
- **Overshoot budget.** Overshoot (`out_back`, POP_IN, or an underdamped spring with a visible peak) is allowed in **three HUD families only**, each with a physical idea:
  1. **latch:** a button's release and the cooldown-ready ring-out (a mechanism lets go);
  2. **stamp:** something is pressed into place: group assign, glyph stamps, count chips ("×2", "+k"), the selection-ring pop, the banner word, the Chronicle's ribbon tab, ECO-12's count bounce;
  3. **unroll:** cloth that unrolls: the level-up ribbon and the clans feed tab.
  Everything else arrives and leaves **without** overshoot (`out_quart`, `out_cubic`, `in_quad`, or a spring with ζ ≥ 0.8). Overshoot is specified as a **visible peak** (for example "scale 1.04 at 60 ms"), never as `out_back` on a sub-pixel delta.

**Honesty and information**
- **Fog gate:** shake, 3D sound, hover states, cursor states, previews, compass arrows and pings are produced only for things the local player can currently see, or for the player's own and allied entities. One predicate decides this for every UI system: `ScreenQuery.is_pickable(u)` (UCA-F4).
- Alerts are about the player's own entities only. The UI never pings an enemy position.
- A **ghost** (trailing HP chunk, missing-amount label) may lag. The **value** never does.

`reduce_motion` forces the following:
- `camera_jumps` = Instant and shake 0;
- pops become 120 ms brightness blinks, and shakes become 200 ms red blinks;
- no pulses (HudPulse holds every element steady), no tassel motion, swoop, dolly or crest spin.

`reduce_flash` (combat doc, applied here):
- the cooldown-ready white overlay α 0.6 → 0.2 (the ring stays);
- red low-HP pulses and the compass pulse become steady (α at the pulse's 75 % point);
- minimap frame glints off (pings stay);
- the release brightness flash ×1.18 → ×1.06; the tooltip affordability flash off (colour change only);
- lantern gutter flickers off.

**Window focus and minimise**
- On `NOTIFICATION_APPLICATION_FOCUS_OUT`: edge pan off, cursor confinement released, and the arbiter applies `unfocused_audio` on Master (duck key `unfocused`, attack 300 ms, release 300 ms on focus in).
- While minimised (`DisplayServer.window_get_mode() == WINDOW_MODE_MINIMIZED`): the portrait SubViewports go `UPDATE_DISABLED`, the global shader parameter `ui_paused` = 1 freezes the HUD shaders, and the lit-frame and HudPulse updates stop. Restored on the first unminimised frame.

```gdscript
# src/input/camera_juice.gd — visual only. Results are written to members (no allocation).
var x := Vector3.ZERO
var v := Vector3.ZERO

func approach(v_goal: Vector3, k: float, dt: float) -> void:
	var e := exp(-k * dt)
	x += v_goal * dt + (v - v_goal) * (1.0 - e) / k
	v = v_goal + (v - v_goal) * e

# Trauma shaker (CAM-05). t = Clock.ui_now. Trauma decays linearly (exact), and the noise is
# sampled by time, so the result is identical at any refresh rate.
# _noise: FastNoiseLite, TYPE_SIMPLEX, fractal_type = FRACTAL_NONE (the 4.7.2 default is FBM with
# 5 octaves, which puts content far above Nyquist), frequency = 1.0, fixed seed.
func shake_offsets(t: float, distance: float) -> Vector3:   # x, y in metres; z = roll in degrees
	var s := trauma * trauma * Settings.shake_scale()
	var k := distance / 28.0                                   # constant on-screen size at any zoom
	var f := freq_hz                                           # 14 gameplay, 7 cinematic
	var n := Vector3(_noise.get_noise_2d(t * f, 0.0), _noise.get_noise_2d(t * f, 37.0),
		_noise.get_noise_2d(t * f, 91.0))
	n = (n / 0.7).clamp(Vector3(-1, -1, -1), Vector3(1, 1, 1))   # single-octave simplex reaches ±0.999
	return Vector3(s * 0.9 * k * n.x + kick.x, s * 0.9 * k * n.y + kick.y, s * 1.2 * n.z)
```

Buses are processed from the highest index down, so Voice already holds the Acks and Alerts sends when the Music and Ambience compressors read it, and Alerts has been processed when the Combat compressor reads it. Every muffled bus sits to the right of its parent, so off-screen sounds keep their category's gain, duck and reverb. Godot auto-disables silent buses, so unused ones cost nothing. **Names, not indices, are the contract** (the combat doc's "PortraitVoice at 12" predates the muffle buses). Renaming a bus silently falls back to Master (Godot), so a unit test asserts that every `AudioBuses` name resolves and that every muffled bus sends to its parent.

## 6. Our own twists (what WC3 did not have)

Each twist reports a real fact, protects the player's attention, or gives the faction a hand-made personality. **D** marks twists a player sees at the Default juice level; **0** marks those kept at level 0 Competitive too.

1. **Material you can feel (§3.1) [D].** A Brennish button latches back with a visible brass snap; a Tormai button gives like felt and recovers without a rebound. Cooldowns ring out like a hand-bell or a gong; feed lines are a parchment slip pinned by a tack or a felt tab that unrolls. The timing is identical, so nothing competitive changes.
2. **Live box preview and the truncation badge (SEL-03) [0].** You see exactly who will be selected before you let go, and "12 / 17" tells you the selection cap cut five units, instead of a silent drop.
3. **Shortfall ghost and the affordability wipe (HUD-06, HUD-05) [0].** "−35" under the gold counter says how short you are, and an unaffordable button's hatching wipes away the moment you can afford it.
4. **The return crumb (CAM-04) [0].** Shift+Space glides you back to where you were before the last jump, so checking an alert never costs you your macro view.
5. **Alert compass, breadcrumbs and a directional alarm (NTF-01, CAM-04) [0].** Off-screen alerts point at themselves from the screen edge, are clickable, Space walks back through the last five places, and the alarm is panned toward the trouble, so the ear says "left" before the eye finds the arrow.
6. **Fights you started stay quiet (NTF-01) [0].** Creeping with your army never rings the base alarm; a raid on your workers always does.
7. **Alarms that sound like nothing good (§5.3) [0].** Clashing tritone bells or a falling slit-drum, never the bells and gongs of your victories.
8. **One rhythm for the HUD (UCA-F5) [0].** Every pulse runs on three octave-locked tempos, and at most two pulse at once.
9. **Teach, then leave (NTF-01) [0].** The [Space] hint shows on the first three alerts until you have used Space once, then never again.
10. **Order ticks and punchline protection (AUD-03) [0].** Every click is acknowledged without voice spam, and a joke line is never cut off mid-punchline.
11. **Selection that paints in like ink (SEL-01) [D].** Rings spread from where your drag began, or from a group's centre.
12. **A cursor with secondary motion and zero lag (CUR-01, CUR-02) [D].** The faction tassel or ribbon swings with your hand from baked hardware frames, and targeting reticles lock onto valid targets on the same frame.
13. **The portrait is a character, not a video (POR-02) [D].** It flinches at real blows, pants when low, glances at the minimap when the alarm sounds, is lit by a lantern at night, and its jokes get subtitles.
14. **An autocast you can watch work (HUD-04) [D].** A stitched comet races one lap each time the autocast actually fires; between firings only the corner dots blink.
15. **A HUD that lives in the world's light (HUD-07) [D].** The brass and oak catch golden hour, moonlight and passing cloud; lanterns breathe at night; the clock notches each night you survive.
16. **Group badges with health strips and in-world numerals (GRP-01) [0].** You see which army is bleeding without selecting it, and who belongs to group 3.
17. **The queue pops toward the rally point (HUD-09) [D].** A finished unit's icon leaps out of the queue in the direction it will walk, and the slot warms in its last 10 %.
18. **The cripple candle (HUD-10) [D].** Losing your last hall lights a watch candle or strings a cord of beads; you feel the 120 s burning down.
19. **Pause as a sketch (HUD-11) [D].** Time stops and the world turns into the same pencil-and-paper look as remembered terrain.
20. **A precise minimap (NTF-02) [0].** A dashed preview shows where a click will put the camera; relation colours with shapes help colour-blind players.
21. **Off-screen muffle and the low end (AUD-04, AUD-02) [0].** Muffled means "not on your screen", and heavy moments get one deliberate sub-bass thump instead of a shake.
22. **The music goes to night, and heroes have motifs (MUS-01) [D].** At dusk the current track is filtered into night instead of being cut, and you hear an enemy hero's motif the first time you see them.
23. **Advisor fatigue (NTF-03) [D].** The third "not enough gold" in a minute comes out exasperated, the fifth curt.
24. **The crest egg and rare delights (HUD-12) [D].** Poke the Ember Wheel six times and it jams; once in a while, an automaton bows at midnight or a bird crosses the mirror at dawn.
25. **The march bed and the rally stem (AUD-05, MUS-01) [D].** You hear your army moving as one cadence, and the percussion swells only when your visible army fights.
26. **The Last Tableau and the unsung hero (SEQ-02, SEQ-03) [D].** The battlefield holds its breath, then becomes a painting of the decisive moment, and the Chronicle names the regular soldier who did the most.
27. **A clickable feed (NTF-03) [0].** Every completion or alert line is a link to its place.

## 7. Anti-patterns (rejected in review)

1. **Routine screen shake.** Shake on hits, crits, deaths, level-ups, alerts or UI errors. Any shake from an event the player cannot see. The whitelist in §5.4 is exhaustive.
2. **A floaty or mushy camera.** Pan stops longer than 150 ms; key inertia; a zoom that starts slowly (less than 15 % of a notch on the first frame) or settles over 300 ms; the camera drifting, breathing or bobbing while idle; soft bounds that crawl into the map edge.
3. **A camera with a mind of its own.** Auto-follow, auto-zoom on events, or forced cinematic cuts during play. The camera moves only on input, on an explicit jump, or for §5.4 events.
4. **Unskippable or input-eating sequences.** A start swoop that blocks input or resolves a click on a moving view; an end sequence you cannot skip. The HUD is live from frame 0.
5. **Laggy or jumpy cursors.** Anything at the hotspot drawn by a software overlay (it trails the hand by 2–3 frames); hotspots that move between states; giant cursors that cover targets; re-registering the OS cursor image for routine state changes; a cursor that moves on its own.
6. **Clumsy tooltips.** Tooltips that follow the cursor and cover the command card; a delay over 400 ms; no warm-up chaining; static cost text that never turns affordable; rebuilding rich text every poll.
7. **Feedback on release, or input gated by animation.** Command buttons that act on mouse-up; hotkeys with no visual echo on the card; a sub-card sweep that delays or drops a hotkey chord.
8. **Alarm spam.** Voice for events already on screen; an alert per hit; a base alarm for a fight you started; per-grid-cell alarms that ring three times for one raid; queued advisor lines that play seconds late (stale information is worse than none); stacking identical error lines.
9. **Voice spam and overlap.** Every right-click playing a voice line; a tick on top of every voice line; two acks at once; annoyed lines cut mid-punchline; acks ducked under combat.
10. **Adaptive bombast.** Battle-music swings, hard mid-track switches at dusk, music that never rests, or music ducking under every click.
11. **A flat or muddy mix.** Everything at 0 dB; normalising every asset; no caps; several systems writing bus volumes or filter cutoffs; stacked ducks cratering the mix (Music never more than 8 dB down in play); low end from every hit; muffled sounds that skip their category bus and play louder off-screen than on.
12. **Generic UI juice ("AI slop").** The same `out_back` bounce on everything (overshoot is limited to the latch, stamp and unroll families, §2); overshoot on sub-pixel deltas nobody can see; glow on everything; neon gradients, glass panels, gloss sweeps, rainbow sheens or particle bursts on HUD buttons; UI tweens over 300 ms on frequent actions; a "pop" without a physical idea behind it (latch, stamp, stitch, bell); a default dark rounded-rect toast.
13. **Skin-swap factions.** Two factions that differ only in colour and samples while sharing every curve. Material includes motion (§3.1).
14. **Animations that reflow the layout.** Scaling a control inside a container without `offset_transform_visual_only`, so its neighbours jitter.
15. **HUD that lies.** Counters, bars or timers lagging the sim to match an animation; a clock "sweeping" to the start time; a ready chime before the sim says ready; a stalled queue bar that keeps moving.
16. **Frame-rate-dependent behaviour.** `lerp(a, b, 0.1)` per frame, Euler-integrated springs, counting frames, per-frame mouse deltas, multi-octave noise sampled per frame, or rendering a portrait at the display rate.
17. **Time tricks during play.** `Engine.time_scale` changes, sim pauses for UI flourishes, or visual slow motion before `match_ended`. The single exception is SEQ-02's held breath, after the sim has stopped.
18. **Colour-only coding.** Red/green order or alert states without shapes; HP strips that rely on hue alone.
19. **Overlays on the action.** Notifications, banners, waves or tints in the central 50 % of the playfield during play; tooltips over the card; feed lines stacking past 4.
20. **Pulse soup.** Several unrelated pulse rates in the periphery at once, or more than two HUD elements pulsing together (UCA-F5).
21. **An alarm that sounds like good news.** Sharing a timbre or rhythm between an alarm and a tier-up, completion, revive or ready cue (§5.3 valence rule).
22. **GPU stalls for UI.** Viewport readbacks (`get_image`) during play for portraits, icons or effects. The only readback is the post-match tableau capture.
23. **Per-unit scans for picking.** Projecting every unit on every mouse move. Picking goes through the spatial hash (UCA-F4).
24. **UI that leaks fog.** Hover rims, cursor tips, nameplates, previews, badges, pings, sounds or shake from units the local player cannot see.
25. **Lens fakery.** Tilt-shift, depth of field, chromatic aberration or film grain to sell the "diorama". The diorama is in the art and the light, not a blurred lens (world doc anti-pattern 6).
26. **Copying WC3 expression.** Gauntlet-hand cursors, the gold-and-stone console frame, WC3's orb art, its under-attack wording ("Your forces are under attack"-style lines; our feed says "struck", "harried", "engaged"), its level-up or error sounds, or a re-voiced "Victory!" dialog layout. Genre conventions are fine: bottom console, 4×3 card, minimap pings, Space jump, a day/night orb, a score screen. Our shapes, materials, words and sounds are original (ip-safety §2).
