## 1. Feel goals

The HUD, the camera and the mixer are the player's **hands and ears**. They should feel like a well-made tool on a craftsman's bench: instant, weighty and quiet while nothing matters, and impossible to miss when something does.

- **Answer in the same frame.** Every click is answered on the input frame by the button, the cursor, the ring and a voice or a tick. Then the feedback gets out of the way.
- **A steady hand.** The camera accelerates crisply, stops firmly and never drifts. It only shakes when the ground itself heaves in front of you.
- **A curated mix.** The mix is curated, not piled up: one voice at a time, ranked by importance, muffled when off-screen. The music breathes the way WC3's did, with silence between songs.

WC3 got the bones right: talking portraits, the unit voices, the horn and the minimap ping, the Space jump, the sun/moon orb and the score screen. We keep every one of those conventions. Our juice goes into **timing curves, honesty and material**. The tactile layer is wood, brass and burgundy leather for Brennmark, and felt, bronze and saffron stitching for the Tormai. Never neon, glass or glitter.

**Pillars**
1. **Same-frame answer.** Input feedback fires on the input event, not on the next sim tick. The sim catches up 1–3 ticks later, and the UI never waits for it.
2. **The camera moves only for a reason.** It moves when the player asked (pan, zoom, jump) or when a whitelisted ground-heaving event happens in view (§5.4). Never on its own initiative, never for routine hits.
3. **Calm by default, loud by exception.** Attention is a budget:
   - Routine feedback is short, small and low in the mix.
   - Alarms are rare, de-duplicated and shape-coded.
   - Anything visible on screen does not also get shouted.
4. **Honest.** Counters and bars are exact; only trailing "ghosts" lag. No event is announced before the sim says it happened. Nothing reveals what the local player cannot see: no shake, ping, sound or hover from fogged events.
5. **Crafted, not generic.** Each row has its own curve tied to a physical idea (a latch, a stamp, a bell, felt, ink). The two factions share timing and differ in material.
6. **Refresh-rate proof.** Everything is closed-form in elapsed time or exact exponential (§2). 30, 60, 144 and 240 Hz give identical results.

## 2. Ground rules (bind every row)

**Time**
- Camera, HUD, cursor and audio scheduling run in `_process(delta)` with **real** delta, never in `_physics_process`.
- HUD and camera time is `Vfx.ui_now`: real, unscaled seconds that keep running while paused (combat §2). Game speed never speeds up the UI.
- HUD Tweens are created on the control they animate (`create_tween()`), with `set_ignore_time_scale(true)`. The HUD's `process_mode` is ALWAYS.
- Smoothing uses `Juice.damp` or the closed forms below, never `lerp(a, b, k)` per frame.
- **Velocity approach** (used for camera pan). It is exact for any dt, given a goal velocity that is constant within the frame:
  `x' = x + v_g·dt + (v − v_g)(1 − e^(−k·dt))/k`, `v' = v_g + (v − v_g)·e^(−k·dt)`.
- **Springs** use the closed form `Juice.spring` (combat FND-1).
- Juice reads the sim and never writes it:
  - Camera position, HUD state and audio never feed the sim, except through the player's own commands (unchanged).
  - No row touches `Engine.time_scale`, `World.rng` or sim timing.
  - The end-sequence slow-down applies only after the sim has stopped (SEQ-02).

**Readability**
- HUD animations never reflow the layout. Every juiced control sets `offset_transform_visual_only = true` and animates the `offset_transform_*` properties, with the pivot at the centre.
- The central 50 % of the playfield never receives HUD overlays. The exceptions are the cursor, the drag box, the error line (bottom-centre, small) and the end banner. This matches the ULT-01 rule that the centre is never coloured.
- Text over the world is at least 13 px, with a 2 px ink outline (#14110D).
- Every state difference is **shape-coded** as well as colour-coded:
  - notched rings mean enemy or alert;
  - dashed rings mean hover;
  - hatching means unaffordable;
  - the lock glyph means a missing requirement;
  - notched strips mean low HP.

**Honesty and information**
- **Fog gate:** shake, 3D sound, hover states, compass arrows and pings are produced only for things the local player can currently see, or for the player's own entities.
- Alerts are about the player's own entities only. The UI never pings an enemy position.
- A **ghost** (trailing HP chunk, missing-amount label) may lag. The **value** never does.

**UCA-F2 sketch (load-bearing; exact for any dt):**

```gdscript
# src/input/camera_juice.gd — visual only.
static func approach(x: Vector3, v: Vector3, v_goal: Vector3, k: float, dt: float) -> Array:
	var e := exp(-k * dt)
	return [x + v_goal * dt + (v - v_goal) * (1.0 - e) / k, v_goal + (v - v_goal) * e]

# Trauma shaker (CAM-05). t = Vfx.ui_now. Trauma decays linearly (exact), and the noise is
# sampled by time, so the result is identical at any refresh rate.
func shake_offsets(t: float, distance: float) -> Vector3:   # x, y in metres; z = roll in degrees
	var s := trauma * trauma * Settings.shake_scale()
	var k := distance / 28.0                                   # constant on-screen size at any zoom
	var f := freq_hz                                           # 14 gameplay, 7 cinematic
	var n := Vector3(_noise.get_noise_2d(t * f, 0.0), _noise.get_noise_2d(t * f, 37.0),
		_noise.get_noise_2d(t * f, 91.0)) / 0.7                # normalise simplex to about ±1
	return Vector3(s * 0.9 * k * n.x + kick.x, s * 0.9 * k * n.y + kick.y, s * 1.2 * n.z)
```

## 6. Our own twists (what WC3 did not have)

Each twist reports a real fact or protects the player's attention. None is decoration.

1. **Live box preview and the truncation badge (SEL-03).** You see exactly who will be selected before you let go, and "12 / 17" tells you the selection cap cut five units, instead of a silent drop.
2. **Selection that paints in like ink (SEL-01).** Rings spread from where your drag began, or from a group's centre, so even selecting feels hand-made.
3. **Directional, fog-honest shake (CAM-05).** The rare shake also says *where* the blast was, and it can never leak an unseen enemy.
4. **The ink veil (CAM-04).** Long glides get a painterly page-turn at the screen edges, so you keep the speed of a jump without the strobing of a fast pan.
5. **Alert compass and breadcrumbs (NTF-01, CAM-04).** Off-screen alerts point at themselves from the screen edge, are clickable, and Space walks back through the last five instead of only the latest.
6. **Teach, then leave (NTF-01).** The [Space] hint shows on the first three alerts until you have used Space once, then never again.
7. **Order ticks and punchline protection (AUD-03).** Every click is acknowledged without voice spam, and a joke line is never cut off mid-punchline.
8. **A cursor with secondary motion (CUR-01).** The pointer's faction tassel or ribbon swings with your hand, and targeting reticles visibly lock onto valid targets (CUR-02).
9. **The portrait is a character, not a video (POR-02).** It flinches when hit, pants when low, glances at the minimap when an alarm sounds, follows your cursor, and is lit by a lantern at night. Annoyed lines get a subtitle bubble, because the jokes are the content.
10. **Shortfall ghost and the affordability wipe (HUD-06, HUD-05).** "−35" under the gold counter says how short you are, and an unaffordable button's hatching wipes away the moment you can afford it.
11. **An autocast you can watch work (HUD-04).** The stitched comet flares and races a lap each time the autocast actually fires.
12. **A HUD that lives in the world's light (HUD-07).** The brass and oak of the frame catch golden hour, moonlight and passing cloud shadows. The clock is a Brennish astronomical dial or a Tormai bronze mirror.
13. **Group badges with health strips and in-world numerals (GRP-01).** You see which army is bleeding without selecting it, and who belongs to group 3.
14. **Off-screen muffle (AUD-04).** Muffled means "not on your screen", a sense WC3's flat mix never gave.
15. **The music goes to night (MUS-01).** At dusk the current track is filtered into night instead of being cut, and night picks come from sparser arrangements.
16. **The march bed (AUD-05).** You hear your army moving as one cadence, and you hear an enemy column arrive on screen.
17. **Rally stem (MUS-01).** The percussion swells only when your visible army is fighting, in sync with the track, without battle-music swings.
18. **The Last Tableau (SEQ-02).** The battlefield holds its breath at quarter speed, then becomes a painting of the decisive moment, which heads your Chronicle.
19. **A clickable feed (NTF-03).** Every completion or alert line is a link to its place.

## 7. Anti-patterns (rejected in review)

1. **Routine screen shake.** Shake on hits, crits, deaths, level-ups, alerts or UI errors. Any shake from an event the player cannot see. The whitelist in §5.4 is exhaustive.
2. **A floaty camera.** Pan stops longer than 150 ms; key inertia; zoom smoothing over 300 ms; the camera drifting, breathing or bobbing while idle.
3. **A camera with a mind of its own.** Auto-follow, auto-zoom on events, or forced cinematic cuts during play. The camera moves only on input, on an explicit jump, or for §5.4 events.
4. **Unskippable sequences.** A start swoop that blocks input, or an end sequence you cannot skip. Every sequence cancels on any input, and the HUD is live from frame 0.
5. **Laggy or jumpy cursors.** A software-only pointer (one frame of lag); hotspots that move between states; giant cursors that cover targets; swapping the OS cursor image every frame.
6. **Clumsy tooltips.** Tooltips that follow the cursor and cover the command card; a delay over 400 ms; no warm-up chaining; static cost text that never turns affordable.
7. **Feedback on release.** Command buttons that act on mouse-up, or hotkeys that trigger with no visual echo on the card.
8. **Alarm spam.** Voice for events already on screen; an alert per hit; queued advisor lines that play seconds late (stale information is worse than none); stacking identical error lines.
9. **Voice spam and overlap.** Every right-click playing a voice line; two acks at once; annoyed lines cut mid-punchline; acks ducked under combat.
10. **Adaptive bombast.** Battle-music swings, hard mid-track switches at dusk, music that never rests, or music ducking under every click.
11. **A flat mix.** Everything at 0 dB; normalising every asset; no caps; several systems writing bus volumes; stacked ducks cratering the mix. One arbiter, the deepest duck wins.
12. **Generic UI juice ("AI slop").** The same `out_back` bounce on everything; glow on everything; neon gradients, glass panels, rainbow sheens or particle bursts on HUD buttons; UI tweens over 300 ms on frequent actions; a "pop" without a physical idea behind it (latch, stamp, stitch, bell).
13. **Animations that reflow the layout.** Scaling a control inside a container without `offset_transform_visual_only`, so its neighbours jitter.
14. **HUD that lies.** Counters, bars or timers lagging the sim to match an animation; a clock "sweeping" to the start time; a ready chime before the sim says ready.
15. **Frame-rate-dependent smoothing.** `lerp(a, b, 0.1)` per frame, Euler-integrated springs, counting frames. Use `damp`, the closed forms and timestamps.
16. **Time tricks during play.** `Engine.time_scale` slow motion, sim pauses for UI flourishes, or visual slow motion before `match_ended`.
17. **Colour-only coding.** Red/green order or alert states without shapes; HP strips that rely on hue alone.
18. **Overlays on the action.** Notifications, banners or tints in the central 50 % of the playfield during play; tooltips over the card; feed lines stacking past 4.
19. **Lens fakery.** Tilt-shift, depth of field, chromatic aberration or film grain to sell the "diorama". The diorama is in the art and the light, not a blurred lens (world doc anti-pattern 6).
20. **Copying WC3 expression.** Gauntlet-hand cursors, the gold-and-stone console frame, WC3's orb art, its under-attack wording ("Your forces are under attack"-style lines), its level-up or error sounds, or a re-voiced "Victory!" dialog layout. Genre conventions are fine: bottom console, 4×3 card, minimap pings, Space jump, a day/night orb, a score screen. Our shapes, materials, words and sounds are original (ip-safety §2).
