## 1. Feel goals

Every unit is a **painted tin soldier that answers when spoken to, hits with weight, and falls with dignity**. A one-second clip of a 60-unit melee must show four things at a glance: **where blows land, who is winning, who is in trouble, and who just died.** It must feel *thwacky, not sparkly*. Motion follows a fixed grammar:

- **Anticipation:** a pull-back whose depth and length depend on the weapon, then a snap that lands exactly on the sim's damage tick.
- **Contact:** a 40–80 ms freeze on the attacker, a rim flash on the victim, and debris that matches the material struck (iron rings, pavise wood splinters, straw, soot, stone chips).
- **Reaction:** a mass-scaled spring: small creeps flinch fast, giants absorb the blow. Never a ragdoll, never jelly.
- **Aftermath:** bodies stay, their team colour drains away, and the ground keeps a scar.

**The factions move differently, not just look differently.** The grammar above is shared; the personality is not:

- **Brennmark (kingdom)** are drilled, deadpan tradesfolk. Their motion is *crisp*: short holds, straight lines, `out_back` snaps, salutes. A Pavisier plants his painted pavise like a shop sign; a Hookgunner leans in to hear you.
- **The Tormai (clans)** are big, slow and courteous. Their motion is *rounded*: deep wind-ups, `in_out_sine` holds, and a bow before violence ("they apologise before hitting you").
- **Creeps** each have a body language of their own: Sootcoats hunch and cough, Hollow Harvest scarecrows move **on twos like stop-motion puppets**, Cairnwakes grind and settle slab by slab.

WC3 reads well because its effects are **short, chunky and sparse**, and we keep that restraint. Our "juice" goes into **timing curves, secondary motion, per-unit personality and readable state tells**, not into particle count.

Pillars:

1. **Every tell is a fact.** Each feedback element reports a real sim event: contact, ownership, order state, a toggle, a cooldown, danger or death. Nothing flashes "for flavour", and nothing lies. There are no fake blocks or parries, and no resource counts that lag behind the sim.
2. **The click is answered in the same frame.** Markers, ring pulses and head-glances fire on input in real time. The sim then catches up within 1–3 ticks.
3. **Toy-diorama physics.** Squash and stretch, springs, hops and swinging props. All motion is additive offsets on the visual `body` and its props, never on the sim-owned `Unit` transform.
4. **Readability beats spectacle.** Every semantic tell moves the silhouette by at least 4 px at default zoom (§2.2). Effects never cover team colour, rings or health bars for more than 80 ms. Density drops automatically in big fights, evenly for both sides (FND-3). Colour is never the only code.
5. **Frame-rate proof.** Everything is evaluated from timestamps, closed-form springs, `exp()` damping or stateless hashes, so 30, 60, 144 and 240 Hz look the same (§2.1 states exactly what "the same" means).

## 2. Ground rules (bind every row below)

### 2.1 Time and determinism

- The sim is 30 Hz fixed and authoritative. **Juice only reads sim state.** It never calls `World.rng`, never writes `Unit` fields, never changes `Engine.time_scale` and never delays a sim event.
- A "hit-stop" is a hold on the attacker's *visual* pose only (CMB-16). The sim damage tick is unchanged.
- **Event queue.** Sim signal handlers run inside the physics step, so they only **append a compact record** to `Vfx`'s ring buffer (512 records, packed arrays). `Vfx._process` drains it (FND-4). No sprite, flash, sound or label is created inside `_physics_process`.
- **Clocks** live in `Vfx`:
  - `Vfx.now`: real `delta` × game speed, frozen while paused. World effects use it. Published once per frame as the global shader uniform **`fx_time`** (the name the sibling docs already use).
  - `Vfx.ui_now`: real, unscaled `delta`. Input feedback (markers, ripples, acks, portrait) uses it. Published as **`ui_time`**.
  - Both are set with one `RenderingServer.global_shader_parameter_set` call each per frame and declared in `project.godot [shader_globals]`. `fx_time` resets at match start (float precision stays below 0.25 ms for 68 minutes).
  - **`TIME` is banned in juice shaders**; it ignores pause and game speed.
- **Timestamps** are stored only in full-float slots: the instance transform basis or an instance uniform. Never in `INSTANCE_COLOR` or `INSTANCE_CUSTOM`, which are half floats on Compatibility (0.5 s resolution at t = 1000 s).
- Transient effects are stored as **start timestamp + duration** and evaluated lazily. An off-screen unit therefore costs nothing and is correct when it comes back. Springs snap to rest while a unit is off-screen.
- **Smoothing** uses `Juice.damp` (`lerp(a, b, 1 − exp(−rate·dt))`), never `lerp(a, b, k)`. Springs use the closed forms in FND-1, never Euler steps. A spring that **tracks a moving target** uses `Juice.spring_track` (first-order hold), which stays within 1 % of a 1 kHz reference at 30 Hz.
- **Thresholds** (whoosh start, footstep plants, ratchet clicks, crossing a stride phase) fire on a **crossing test**: `prev < thr ≤ cur`, evaluated on the frame's start and end values, possibly several per frame, with a per-swing "fired" flag. Never "is the value close to thr this frame".
- **Periodic spawns** (trails, smoke, fizz) use a **time accumulator**: emit `floor(acc / interval)` spawns per frame, with their positions interpolated along the path between the previous and current frame and their `t0` back-dated accordingly. Trail density is then identical at 30 and 144 Hz.
- **No visual oscillation faster than 12 Hz** (Nyquist at 30 fps is 15 Hz). Anything faster becomes a single damped squash, a motion-blur sprite, or value noise in 20 Hz buckets.
- **Latched peaks.** Every curve whose peak lasts 60 ms or less starts *at* its peak (age 0 is the peak value), and `t0` is stamped at drain time in the frame that renders it. The first rendered frame after an event therefore always shows the peak, even at 30 fps.
- **Visual randomness is stateless:** `Juice.hash01(unit.id, event_seq, salt)` at event time, and `Juice.vnoise(unit.id, t, 20.0)` (value noise in 20 Hz buckets, linearly interpolated) for anything continuous. Never `randf()`, never `World.rng`, never a per-frame draw from a stateful RNG.
- **Juice never reads node global transforms.** Under physics interpolation `global_transform` is the last tick's, not what is drawn. Anchors are rebuilt from `unit.display_pos()`, `unit.display_facing()` and the composer's own offsets; bone anchors use `Skeleton3D.get_bone_global_pose()`, read once per owner per frame. Where a node transform is unavoidable, use `get_global_transform_interpolated()`.

### 2.2 Readability and legibility

**Legibility rule (L1).** Every *semantic* tell (one that reports an event or a state) moves a silhouette extreme (head top, hat brim, weapon tip, shield edge, shoulder) or a ring or bar edge by **≥ 4 px at default zoom at 1080p**. The default view is 28 m away, vertical FOV 45°, pitch −56° (heroes §2.6), which gives:

| direction of motion | px per metre | 4 px needs |
|---|---|---|
| sideways on screen | ≈ 46 | 0.09 m |
| along the ground, toward or away from the camera | ≈ 39 | 0.10 m |
| straight up (foreshortened) | ≈ 26 | 0.16 m |

Rules of thumb for a 1.6 m unit: whole-body pitch or roll **≥ 6°** (moves the head 0.17 m); lift **≥ 0.16 m** or a squash **sy ≤ 0.90**; held poses of 8–14°. `tests/playtest/brawl_juice.gd` measures every tell in a "tell gallery" pass (§7).

**Distinct channels (L2).** Two tells that mean different things must differ in *channel*, not only in amplitude: e.g. annoyed = turn to camera + stamp; kill-confirm = weapon raised and held; chest-out = roll sway + weapon on the shoulder.

**Texture layers (L3).** Breathing, tremble, cloth flutter and dust are texture: they are exempt from L1 but are never the only carrier of an event.

**Occlusion.** Effects cover team colour, rings or health bars for **at most 80 ms** (one number, everywhere). Smears start outside the body hull (CMB-20). Smoke stays above head height or at α ≤ 0.35.

**Selection rings and health bars** stay on the sim position (`display_pos()`). Body offsets move only the body, by at most **0.20 m** (wheeled siege recoil: 0.30 m, explicitly exempt because its ring, bar and pick volume stay on the sim position). Picking stays honest.

**Render priority** (explicit on every juice material; transparent materials sort by priority first, so map-sized AABBs no longer sort wrongly):

| layer | render_priority | blend / depth |
|---|---|---|
| water | 0 | world doc |
| contact blobs | 1 | mix, depth test, no write |
| rings and order markers | 2 | mix, depth test, clip-space bias toward the camera (`POSITION.z += 0.0005·POSITION.w`; Godot ≥ 4.3 uses reversed-Z on all renderers, verify the sign in the render test) |
| FxSprites **cutout** (chips, splinters, droplets, clods, pebbles, leaves, straw, shards, feathers, coins) | opaque pass | alpha scissor 0.5, depth write on, no sorting needed |
| FxSprites **soft** (puffs, smoke, dust, haze) | 4 | mix, depth test, no write |
| smears | 5 | mix |
| FxSprites **additive** (sparks, flares, motes, glints, embers) | 6 | add, HDR |
| health bars | 10 | `no_depth_test` |
| combat text and glyphs | 20 | `no_depth_test` |

- HDR above 1.0 is allowed only for VFX: sparks, flares, motes, glints, embers, muzzle flashes and spell light. **Unit flash peak linear luminance ≤ 0.95 at noon** (CMB-17), so units never bloom.
- **Fog of war is honoured.** No sprite, sound, label, look-at or glance is produced at, or toward, a position the local player cannot currently see, unless the source is the local player's own unit *and* the target is visible. Invisible units emit nothing visible. FxSprites, smears and markers also multiply by `fog_tex` as a second guard.
- Colour-blind safety: markers are shape-coded, low HP is hatched, the enemy target flash has notches, effectiveness is carried by size and sound as well as colour.
- `cast_shadow = OFF` on every fx geometry.

### 2.5 Notation

- Durations are milliseconds at 1× game speed. Distances are metres; `h` = the unit's height (infantry 1.75 m).
- Colours are sRGB hex. "HDR n" means emission multiplier n.
- **Angles:** pitch > 0 leans **forward** (toward the facing), roll > 0 leans to the unit's right, yaw > 0 turns left seen from above. The composer converts to Godot (`rotation.x = −pitch` for a −Z-facing model).
- Easings are Penner functions in `Juice`: `out_quad`, `in_quad`, `in_cubic`, `out_cubic`, `in_out_sine`, `out_back(c1)` (c1 1.3 overshoots ≈ 6 %, 1.5 ≈ 8 %, 1.70158 ≈ 10 %), `in_back`.
- `mass` is `fx.mass` (FND-4). Tiers (Calm / Busy / Brawl) and importance are defined in FND-3. "Weapon pose w" is defined in FND-6.

**FND-1 sketch (load-bearing; the impulse factors and rebounds in §4.8 were checked numerically against a 1 kHz integration):**

```gdscript
# src/fx/juice.gd — visual only. x = offset from rest, v = velocity. Exact for any dt.
static func spring(x: float, v: float, omega: float, zeta: float, dt: float) -> Vector2:
	if zeta < 0.995:
		var wd := omega * sqrt(1.0 - zeta * zeta)
		var e := exp(-zeta * omega * dt)
		var c := cos(wd * dt)
		var s := sin(wd * dt)
		return Vector2(e * (x * c + (v + zeta * omega * x) / wd * s),
			e * (v * c - (omega * omega * x + zeta * omega * v) / wd * s))
	if zeta <= 1.005:                                   # critical branch (avoids wd -> 0)
		var e1 := exp(-omega * dt)
		var b := v + omega * x
		return Vector2((x + b * dt) * e1, (v - omega * b * dt) * e1)
	var q := omega * sqrt(zeta * zeta - 1.0)            # overdamped branch
	var r1 := -zeta * omega + q
	var r2 := -zeta * omega - q
	var c1 := (v - r2 * x) / (r1 - r2)
	var c2 := x - c1
	var e1 := exp(r1 * dt)
	var e2 := exp(r2 * dt)
	return Vector2(c1 * e1 + c2 * e2, c1 * r1 * e1 + c2 * r2 * e2)

# Target moves linearly a0 -> a1 during dt (first-order hold): exact for ramps.
static func spring_track(x: float, v: float, a0: float, a1: float, omega: float, zeta: float, dt: float) -> Vector2:
	var u := (a1 - a0) / dt
	var bias := 2.0 * zeta * u / omega
	var r := spring(x - a0 + bias, v - u, omega, zeta, dt)
	return Vector2(r.x + a1 - bias, r.y + u)

static func damp(a: float, b: float, rate: float, dt: float) -> float:
	return lerpf(a, b, 1.0 - exp(-rate * dt))
```

**CMB-16 pose sketch (pure function, unit-tested):**

```gdscript
# split, pull, c1 from the §4.8 weapon timing table (or the unit's fx.motion override).
static func melee_pose(s: float, dp: float, h: float, split: float, pull: float, c1: float) -> float:
	if s < dp:
		var u := s / dp
		if u < split:
			return pull * Juice.out_quad(u / split)                                   # load, settling into the pose
		return lerpf(pull, 1.0, Juice.in_cubic((u - split) / (1.0 - split)))      # snap, accelerating into contact
	if s < dp + h:
		return 1.0                                                                # visual hit-stop (sim unaffected)
	return 1.0 - Juice.out_back((s - dp - h) / maxf(1.0 - dp - h, 0.001), c1)     # recover with overshoot
```

## 5. Our own twists (things WC3 did not have)

Each twist reports a real game fact, so it adds clarity rather than noise.

1. **Faction body language (§4.9, CMB-38).** Brennmark salutes crisply; the Tormai bow, and bow again before their first blow on a new target. Two armies with the same rules timing still *move* like two peoples.
2. **Order ripple (CMB-10).** WC3 confirms with one voice and one ground blip. We send a visible wave of ring pulses and nods through the selection, 28 ms per unit from the one nearest the click: you *see* all 12 units hear you, and late path starts no longer look like ignored orders.
3. **Honest stance and toggle tells (CMB-07, §4.9).** Hold crouches; Plant Pavise *stabs* the painted pavise into the turf; Braced Shot plants the forked rest; a Cairnling settles its slabs; a Fieldkeeper goes rigid. Each is a real armour or damage state you can read without selecting anything.
4. **Cooldown made visible (CMB-37).** Ramrod strokes, a javelin drawn from the bundle and a ratcheting crank end exactly when the weapon is ready: you can time a charge into a gun line by eye.
5. **Proc telegraphs (CMB-46).** The Grappler's reach before a Shove, the Walker's lifted slab before a stun, the Collier's glowing rake before it ignites. Deterministic procs become readable, so micro beats them.
6. **Effectiveness tell (CMB-19).** Javelins *dink* off a fortified wall; a falchion *cracks* into medium armour. Players learn the attack/armour chart by feel, with no tooltip.
7. **Painted smears that follow the weapon (CMB-20).** Dry-brush strokes timed so the stroke head arrives on the contact frame; the flail's smear is a ribbon along its real, swinging head.
8. **Javelin pincushion and straw pass-through (CMB-25).** You can read who is being shot in a blob, and a javelin punching through a scarecrow and out the far side *shows* the −25 % pierce rule.
9. **Kill styles (CMB-35, §4.9).** The Grappler hip-throws the victim over a shoulder; the Javelineer pumps a fist at a long throw; the Hookgunner leans in: "Eh? Did it drop?" Even in a blob you can tell who landed the blow.
10. **Team-colour ebb, rolling hats and faction remains (CMB-30, 31).** The team colour drains from feet to head like a flag being lowered; a kettle hat rolls away on its rim and stays with a dented, faction-specific heap. A corpse never reads as a live unit.
11. **The fight has a start and an end (CMB-36, CMB-43).** A first-contact clash announces a battle; when it is over, the survivors lower their weapons in a wave and one wipes its brow.
12. **Battlefield memory (CMB-32).** Stains and scorches in a scar map persist for minutes, so a choke that was fought over all game looks scarred, with zero extra draw calls.
13. **Siege you can read from above (CMB-27).** A shrinking ground shadow shows the shell's height, the whistle peaks on impact, the Bombard rolls back and is shoved forward by its deafened crew, and the Sling-Cart ratchets and thwumps without a single flash.
14. **Stop-motion scarecrows (§4.9).** Hollow Harvest poses are sampled on twos, so they move like puppets in a harvest-festival nightmare, identically at every refresh rate.
15. **The Gustcaller tests the real wind (§4.9).** The caster's fidget licks a finger and turns toward the actual `atm_wind` direction the grass is bending in.
16. **Creep notice and guard ring (CMB-06, CMB-05).** Camps lift their heads at 1.5× aggro range; a parked squad watches its perimeter.
17. **A portrait that talks and blinks (CMB-14).** Visemes and blinks driven by the real audio envelope; Skymirror, whose eyes are hidden by his visor mirror, glints on his syllables instead.
18. **Eyes-first order glance (CMB-06) and the deny fizzle (CMB-41).** Heads turn in the click frame, hiding tick and path latency; a deny snuffs the would-be bounty in public.

## 6. Anti-patterns (rejected in review)

1. **Real hit-stop or slow motion.** No `Engine.time_scale`, no sim pause and no delayed damage. Hit-stop is a hold on the attacker's visual pose only.
2. **Frame-rate-dependent visuals.** `lerp(a, b, 0.1)` per frame, Euler-integrated springs, counting frames, `TIME` in shaders, half-float timestamps, per-frame RNG draws, oscillations above 12 Hz, thresholds tested with "close to" instead of a crossing, periodic spawns without an accumulator.
3. **Free-running attack clips.** A blade that visibly lands 200 ms before or after the HP drops. Attack and death clips are always seeked from sim phase (CMB-16, CMB-30), and a shoot clip's hit marker is never guessed from hand speed (FND-4).
4. **Faction-blind motion.** One wind-up, one spring, one smear, one death and one heap of remains for every unit of every race: the "generic AI clone" look. Every slice unit has a motion sheet (§4.9).
5. **Moonwalking.** Stride, clip rate or wheel spin driven by time instead of distance travelled (FND-7).
6. **Sub-pixel tells.** A 4° nod or a 2 cm hop that nobody can see from a −56° camera at 28 m. Semantic tells move ≥ 4 px (L1).
7. **Jelly.** Low-damped springs on every hit so a melee blob wobbles continuously; big units ringing at the same frequency as small ones (CMB-21).
8. **Damage confetti.** A number on every hit is MMO noise. The default is Minimal (CMB-28).
9. **Screen shake for routine combat.** Only nearby siege impacts (rate-limited) and the hero events in the camera whitelist may shake.
10. **Fake blocks or parries.** A shield "blocking" a hit that still deals damage lies to the player. The trade clang (CMB-40) shows both blows landing.
11. **Lying poses.** A body turned 60° away from its sim facing; a Hold that looks like a planted pavise; a glance toward an attacker the player cannot see; a cooldown tell that finishes before the weapon is ready.
12. **Resource counters that lag behind the sim** to sync with a coin animation. The display must never lie.
13. **Synchronised idling and lockstep reactions.** A whole army breathing, fidgeting, nodding or glancing in the same frame (the "chorus line"), including on spam clicks. Use hashed phases, the fidget token bucket, the ripple order and the rate limits (CMB-06, 08, 10, 13).
14. **Bloom on units, or flashes too short to see.** Team colour or flash light above 0.95 linear; peaks that a 30 fps frame can skip (§2.1 latched peaks).
15. **A node per effect per unit, or two systems for one job.** 300 `GPUParticles3D`, an `OmniLight3D` per projectile, a mesh allocated per click, two particle pools, two governors, two rally flags. Pool everything; one owner per system (§8).
16. **Tweens fighting over `body`.** Several `create_tween()` calls on `body.scale` and `rotation`. One composer, one write.
17. **Writing visual offsets to the sim-owned `Unit` transform,** or reading node global transforms for effect anchors. Offsets go on `body`; anchors come from display state (§2.1).
18. **Effects that hide information.** Big puffs over health bars, red gore washing out a red team colour, smears over a unit's own tabard for more than 80 ms.
19. **Thinning one side harder than the other** in big fights, so the side with more importance looks stronger (FND-3).
20. **Fog and invisibility leaks.** Sparks, dust, sounds, glances or a creep "notice" revealing a unit the local player cannot see.
21. **Popping corpses.** A body that blinks out of existence. Always ebb, dissolve or sink.
22. **Effects that outlive their information.** Sparks over 400 ms, numbers over 1.3 s, stains with colour.
23. **`Decal` nodes and z-fighting ground quads.** Use the scar map, ground-aligned sprites and clip-space biased rings.
24. **Copying WC3's specific VFX.** No reproduction of WC3's own missile, blood or death-effect art or timing as a signature. Generic conventions (a red crit number with "!", a green move blip, corpses decaying to remains) are fine. Our shapes, textures and curves are original.
