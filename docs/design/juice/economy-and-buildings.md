*Domain doc of the Vibecraft juice bible. Date: 2026-10-17. Status: design spec, **revision 3.1** (revision 3 answered four critiques in two rounds; 3.1 is the completion pass that re-checked every item against the doc's own numbers, the sibling docs and `tools/ip/`; see the Critique log at the end).*

**Sibling docs own, and this doc reuses:** combat FND-1 (closed-form springs, the body composer, overload shedding), FND-2 (FxSprites and its packing), FND-3 (density tiers, `Vfx.allow`, the per-frame spawn budget), FND-4 (event queue, `hit_landed`, `fx` def block), FND-6 (weapon props) and CMB-28 (bounty text and coin flight); heroes HVF-2 (`terrain_height`, GroundZones) and HVF-4 (unit-shader slots); world WLD-F4 (lantern map, LightBudget), WLD-05 (`lit`), WLD-23 (startled flocks), WLD-28/29 (smoke wind, cloth), WLD-31 (world memory ledger) and `terrain.gdshader`; UI/audio UCA-F3, AUD-02 (mixer, pools, priorities), MUS-01 (music director), NTF-02/03/05 (pings, feed, idle-worker button). Where this doc touches their systems it names the hook and leaves the system to them. **This doc owns structure hit flash and structure chips** (combat CMB-18 skips structures; §10).

**Conventions**
- Durations are ms at 1× game speed. Distances are metres. Easings are Godot names (`TRANS_QUAD/EASE_OUT` is written QUAD/OUT) or the material tokens of §2.4.
- Colours are sRGB hex. "HDR x" is an emission multiplier; only values above 1.0 bloom, and only VFX may exceed 1.0.
- "Own" means the local player, or the observed player in observer mode.
- **Factions use their ids.** The slice is `kingdom` (Brennmark) vs `clans` (the Tormai). Construction verbs come from rules §4.7: kingdom *scaffold-raised*, clans *raised felt* ("Raising", clans §2.4). `sylvan` (*woven*) and `deathless` (*unearthed*) are later and live in the §4.2 appendix.
- **Names are data ids.** Every glyph, prop, stream region and sound named here is seeded into `data/` and must pass `tools/ip/check_banned_terms.py` (for example, no `hearth` or `wisp`; clans §1.1: "never Hearth in any name"). Building names follow the faction tables (kingdom §7.2, clans §7).
- **Footprints are in 0.5 m cells** (architecture; kingdom §7.2, clans §7): Croft, Lookout, Gun Tower, Home Yurt, Bow-Mast 4 × 4 cells = **2 m**; Oath Hall, Foundry, Curio Cabinet, Casting Pit, Naming Stones, Tea Tent 6 × 6 = **3 m**; Drill Yard, Timber Yard, Collegium, Wainwright Yard, Grapple Yard, Mirror Yurt 8 × 8 = **4 m**; halls 10 × 10 = **5 m**. **r_h** is the footprint half-diagonal: 1.41 / 2.12 / 2.83 / 3.54 m. Every footprint-scaled effect is written in r_h, never in cells.
- **Size classes:** *small* r_h ≤ 1.5 m; *medium* 1.5 < r_h < 3.5 m; *hall* = main halls.
- **Apron:** walls stand 0.25 m inside the footprint edge on a 0.2 m high apron (kingdom slab, clans stone ring). Coins, chips and the worker's contact point use it (asset contract, §10).
- **Radii are edge distances.** The sim measures every range with `Unit.edge_distance` (unit edge to the footprint rectangle; architecture §5). A truthful ring around a hall is therefore the footprint dilated by R, not a circle (§2.3 terrain rings).
- Readability numbers use the combat doc's default view: 28 m camera distance, FOV 45°, pitch −56°. At 1080p that is ≈ 46 px per metre sideways, ≈ 39 along the ground, ≈ 26 straight up. Max zoom-out is 46 m (× 0.61).

## 1. Feel goals

A base should feel like a **busy workshop you can read at a glance and by ear**:
- Every worker trip ends in a small, crisp payoff that you hear more than see: the Hodman tips his hod into the counting hatch on the beat; the Yurtwright bows and sets the bag on the felt flap.
- Every building is a short ceremony: surveyed, staked, laid course by course, its crown hoisted, topped out, and flagged.
- **Every hammer strike visibly lays something.** Cause and effect are never separated.
- Damage writes itself onto **the wall that was hit**, in the building's own material: plaster drops out of a Brennish frame where the ram struck; Tormai felt tears where the stones landed.
- The economy has a **rhythm in the key of the music**: mallets, hod landings and deposit notes lock to the track's grid and scale, and the ledger sounds brighter the closer your mines run to saturation. An experienced player can tell how healthy the base is without looking at it.

Most of this layer is **information in costume**. Each flourish answers a question: Is that worker carrying anything, and how much? Is this mine saturated? How far along is that barracks, is anyone still building it, and when will it finish? Is my forge working? How much is upkeep costing me? Am I floating gold? Which wall is about to give?

The art is chunky and hand-painted. Coins are fat discs, chips tumble, smoke is painted cut-out cotton (never photographic fog), fire is stylised flame tongues, and only VFX bloom.

**Eight rules for this domain:**
1. **Ceremony scales with rarity.** Deposits (about one per second per mine) land ≤ 0.5 s after the touch, their popup rises for 1 s, repeats habituate, and their resting coins are gone within 2.2 s. A building completes about once a minute: ≤ 1 s. A hall tiers up once or twice a game: 1.8 s. Destruction scales with size: 1.4 s small, 2.2 s medium, 3.4 s hall, and a second kill of the same def within 20 s plays the small variant. Repeats get shorter, never longer.
2. **Never wait for the juice.** The sim never pauses, delays or re-times anything. Flourishes run alongside gameplay and can be cut off at any frame. Anticipation is built from **predictions** of sim events and degrades gracefully when a prediction misses.
3. **Readable in a fight.** Economy effects shrink first when combat is busy (§2.8). Nothing covers units, team colour, selection rings or health bars for more than 80 ms, and emission avoids standing units (§2.8 rule 6).
4. **Every material has its own motion and sound** (§2.4 tokens, §2.5). Timber snaps with a small overshoot, stone thuds and never squashes, felt bobs twice, bronze rings three times in time with its sound, cloth flutters. No "jelly stone", no generic sparkle: glints only on the §2.3 glint whitelist (exposed gold, polished bronze or brass, and the piece an upgrade just improved).
5. **Strikes do the work.** Construction progress is revealed **by** builder strikes (BLD-06), completion is **anticipated** (BLD-24), and a building **caves in under its own weight**: the roof sags, the walls fall out, then the debris clears inside the dust (BLD-16).
6. **Pitch from the `MusicClock`; tempo only where the sim allows** (§2.7). Hammers, hod landings, bows, chimney chuffs and work loops lock to the grid; chops, mine exits and hits keep the sim's timing and take only their pitch from the scale.
7. **Fog-honest.** An effect plays only if the local player could see or hear its source, including looped effects that outlive the moment of sight (§2.1).
8. **Rings mean reach.** A ground ring is always a true radius of effect (levy, gong, auras, spells), drawn exactly on the sim's metric. Ceremonies never draw rings.

## 2. Shared foundations (read before implementing any row)

### 2.1 Hard rules

**Visual-only and refresh-rate independent**
- All juice lives in `_process(delta)` or in shaders. Nothing in juice writes `Unit` sim fields, `PlayerState`, `Pathing` or `World.rng`, or emits gameplay events.
- Motion is a **closed-form function of the effect's age**, built from `JuiceCurves` (§2.4), or the combat doc's closed-form springs (FND-1 `Juice.spring`, `Juice.spring_track`). **No Euler integration anywhere.** 30, 60, 144 and 240 Hz show identical poses at the same game time.
- **Springs kicked by sim changes are back-dated** to the tick's display time: a velocity change detected in `_process` kicks at t_kick = `fx_time` − f · `World.DT`, f = `Engine.get_physics_interpolation_fraction()` at detection, and the closed-form spring is evaluated from t_kick. Kick times then agree within 1 ms between 30 and 240 fps.
- **No periodic visual motion above 12 Hz** (Nyquist at 30 fps is 15 Hz). Shudders chirp **down** from ≤ 12 Hz. `JuiceCurves` declares the frequency of every periodic function, and a test enforces the cap (§9).
- **No frame-counted durations.** The shortest timed element is 50 ms. Curves whose peak lasts ≤ 60 ms start **at** the peak (combat "latched peaks"), so the first rendered frame always shows it.
- **Periodic spawns** (smoke, sawdust, embers) use a time accumulator with back-dated `t0`, never "one per frame". **Large bursts are staggered** into ≤ 4 groups 40–60 ms apart, each with its own back-dated `t0` (§2.3).

**Clocks** (owned by combat §2.1, restated)
- `fx_time` = `Vfx.now`: real delta × game speed, frozen while paused, reset at match start. World juice uses it.
- `ui_time` = `Vfx.ui_now`: real, unscaled. Input feedback (the placement ghost, HUD counters, the pitched-audio scheduler, `MusicClock`) uses it.
- Both are **declared in `project.godot [shader_globals]`** (`fx_time={"type":"float","value":0.0}`, same for `ui_time`, the world doc's `atm_*`, HVF-2's `terrain_height` and this doc's `eco_rings_a`, `eco_rings_b` as `mat4`). `Vfx` only calls `global_shader_parameter_set`. A material that references an undeclared global logs an engine error before registration, which fails `tests/run_tests`.
- `TIME` is banned in juice shaders.

**Timestamps and precision**
- Timestamps live only in full-float slots: the instance transform, or an instance uniform. `INSTANCE_CUSTOM` and `COLOR` are half floats on Compatibility (verified on 4.7.2: 1000.25 reads back as 1000.0).
- **The one sanctioned exception** is a *split* timestamp for real transforms that cannot carry data (trees, ECO-09): `r = floor(t) mod 2048` (an integer, exact in half), `g = fract(t)` (≈ 0.5 ms resolution). Combat's request to move it into a basis slot is answered in §10: tree transforms have no spare slot.

**Event queue and spawn budget** (combat FND-3, FND-4)
- Sim signal handlers run inside `World.step()`. They only append a compact record (kind, entity ids, position captured from `unit.pos`, values, **the tick**) to `Vfx`'s 512-record ring buffer.
- `economy_fx.gd` and `building_fx.gd` drain it at the top of `_process`. Spawns are stamped with that frame's `fx_time`; timelines that must match sim state (levy arming, ECO-21) start from the record's tick display time instead. **No fx work runs inside the 30 Hz tick.**
- **Every economy spawn calls `Vfx.allow(kind, pos, side, importance)`** (notifier visibility, distance, fog, tier rule, cap, and the 64-sprite per-frame budget). There is no economy-side governor or overflow rule.

**Randomness:** `Juice.hash01(entity id, event seq, salt)` and `Juice.vnoise` only. Never `World.rng`, never `randf()`. A burning barracks always burns from the same windows.

**Sim reads:** `display_pos()`, `display_build_progress()`, `display_upgrade_progress()` (prev→cur lerped by the physics interpolation fraction). Never raw tick values for motion. Juice never reads node global transforms (combat §2.1).

**Body writes:** juice never writes `body.transform` directly. It adds **composer layers** to combat FND-1's composer: `work`, `cargo`, `fidget`, `spawn`, `levy`, `admire` for workers, and a structure composer (`settle`, `receive`, `hit`, `lean`) for buildings and mines. The composer writes `body.transform` once per frame. Worker cargo and hod poses go through the `Cargo` bone (§2.3), never through a node transform.

**Fog gate**
- World-space effects and 3D sounds for an entity play only when its cell is visible to the local player (`Fog` value 2) and the entity is not invisible. Exception: own entities' HUD-level feedback (advisor lines, feed lines, the HUD itself).
- **Looped effects re-check.** `building_fx` re-tests every looped emitter on an enemy or neutral structure at the fog upload rate (10 Hz). On fog-over: set `t_stop` on its streams, release its loop voice, freeze its `dmg`, `build_a` and window uniforms at their last-seen values (the ghost of rules §8), and **replace its fire splats in the lantern map with one dim, steady ghost splat** (#FF8A3A, r 4 m, 0.3, no flicker), so a remembered fire glows without moving flames (WLD-F4 honesty). On reveal: restore splats and restart from the current state.
- **Dependency:** `src/world/fog.gd` does not exist yet. Until it lands, `FxBudget.visible_at()` returns true and the fog tests are skipped with a logged reason.

**Renderer**
- No `Decal` nodes. Ground effects use GroundZones (HVF-2) or the terrain ring list (§2.3).
- `OmniLight3D` only through the shared LightBudget (world WLD-F4), Forward+ only.
- `cast_shadow = SHADOW_CASTING_SETTING_OFF` on **every** fx geometry, prop and pool in this doc (`shadows_disabled` only stops *receiving*). **One exception:** the 2 falling trees nearest the focus copy `cast_shadow` from the Trees `MultiMeshInstance3D` (ECO-11), so a felled tree's shadow does not vanish on the first frame of the topple.
- At startup, `RenderingServer.get_current_rendering_method()` selects the Compatibility fallbacks of §2.10.

**FxSprites: FND-2's packing, unchanged, and three sign-bit extensions**
- Packing (FND-2): origin = p0; basis x = v0; basis y = (t0, life, size0); basis z = (size1, gravity, drag or restitution). `INSTANCE_CUSTOM` r = atlas cell (0–63) + 64 × flags (5 bits: motion mode BALLISTIC 0 / BOUNCE 1 / FLUTTER 2 / ORBIT 3, GROUND, facing BILLBOARD / STREAK / GROUND_ALIGNED), g = ground_y, b = spin, a = seed. `COLOR` = start colour.
- Economy needs three more behaviours and FND-2 has no spare flag bits, so they are **signs of full-float basis slots** (exact on both renderers; the shader reads `abs()`):
  - **STREAM: life < 0** (basis.y.y). |life| = the loop period P. basis.x is repurposed as (rise_speed m/s, **t_stop** in `fx_time`, wind_gain); `custom.g` = phase ∈ [0, 1) (streams never clamp to ground); motion bits BALLISTIC. Loop index n = floor((now − t0)/P − phase); age = (now − t0) − (n + phase)·P. **Visible only if n ≥ 0 and t0 + (n + phase)·P ≤ t_stop**, so a column never appears fully formed and stops with no CPU work. A column of N slots uses phases k/N and emits every P/N. Drift is world WLD-28's single wind (wind · age^1.4 · 0.6 m + gust). A rate change never re-times a column: doubling adds a second column offset by half an interval.
  - **SETTLE: restitution < 0** (basis.z.z, BOUNCE only). The bounce scales the horizontal velocity by e as well as the vertical one, so a coin does not skate. At the second ground contact (closed form, like FND-2's first) the sprite **freezes at that point**: x, z and ground_y are held, it lies flat (GROUND_ALIGNED from then on) and runs COIN_SETTLE (§2.4, coin preset) with T = 150 + 100 · seed ms.
  - **ERODE: size1 < 0** (basis.z.x). Over the last 25 % of life the alpha-scissor threshold rises 0 → 0.85 (painted erosion) instead of FND-2's cutout shrink.
- `g = max(g, 1e-3)` guards the bounce division. Free-fall gravity is `JUICE_G` (§2.4) except for deposit flights (flight solver).

**Glint whitelist and budget.** GLINT sprites and prop-shader glints exist only on:
- exposed gold: coins in the kingdom hod, ore heaps, the strongbox heap;
- polished bronze or brass: gongs, bells, the Mirror Yurt's sun-mirrors, the Lookout's brass spyglass tube, clans bronze weapons;
- **the piece an upgrade just improved**, during the BLD-14 ripple only (a honed blade, a new armour plate). There the glint carries the information "this unit got it".

Never on felt, linen, wood, stone, chalk, paper, cards or doors. A world glint costs one token from a bucket on `ui_time` (3 tokens, refilled at 3 per second, so ≤ 3 world glints per second on screen) and fires ≤ 1 per source per 4 s. UI glint sweeps (card buttons, the hero slot) sit outside the budget.

**Terrain ring list** (the levy, gong and any radius over 11 m; jointly owned with the world doc's `terrain.gdshader`)
- Two `mat4` shader globals: `eco_rings_a`, column i = (centre x, centre z, footprint half-width, footprint half-depth) of ring i's hall; `eco_rings_b`, column i = (R, t0, style, alpha). Four rings; R = 0 disables a column; a uniform-coherent branch skips the code when all four are off.
- **Shape = the footprint rectangle dilated by R** (a rounded square, corner radius R), because the sim tests `edge_distance(hall) ≤ R`. Per terrain fragment: q = abs(p.xz − c) − half; d_fp = length(max(q, 0)) + min(max(q.x, q.y), 0); s = d_fp − r(t).
- **Motion:** the front grows r(t) = R · CUBIC/OUT(min(age / T_front, 1)) from the footprint edge (T_front: levy 700 ms, gong 900 ms), **holds at exactly R for 400 ms**, then fades over 500 ms. The line runs from s = 0 to s = +0.15 m (outside), so **its inner edge is the predicate boundary: a unit is affected iff its body circle reaches inside the inner edge.** Notches 0.6 m long at 6 (levy, burgundy #7B1E2E on ivory #EDE2C8) or 8 (gong, GONG bronze #D9A55E on #3A2E22) equal angles about the centre.
- Exact on ramps and 1.75 m cliffs (it is evaluated where the terrain is drawn), identical on Compatibility, ≈ 12 ALU per live ring per terrain fragment.
- **Fog:** the CPU writes `alpha` = 1 for the owner and allies, and for other players only while the hall is visible (fog 2).
- **Eviction** (more than 4 calls inside one ring's 1.6 s life, possible with 8–12 players): a new ring takes a free column, else replaces the oldest ring belonging to another player, else the oldest own ring. The local player's newest call is therefore always drawn.

**Flight solver** (deposit flights only; the one documented exception to `JUICE_G`). Given the release point p0, the target p1, a clearance H above the higher endpoint and a flight time T:
- h_a = H + max(0, y1 − y0); h_b = H + max(0, y0 − y1);
- g_t = 2(√h_a + √h_b)² / T²; v0.y = √(2 · g_t · h_a); v0.xz = (p1 − p0).xz / T;
- T25 = the flight time at g = 25. **T may be chosen anywhere in [0.79 · T25, 1.34 · T25]** (g_t from 40 down to 14 m/s²: a flick, never a float). That window is 0.55 · T25 wide, ≥ 180 ms whenever T25 ≥ 0.33 s, which every slice flight meets (kingdom gold H 0.35 m: T25 0.335–0.367 s; lumber H 0.15 m: 0.44 s). ECO-04 uses the freedom to land on the music grid.

### 2.7 `MusicClock` and pitched juice (rule 6)

`MusicClock` (`src/audio/music_clock.gd`, owned by the music director MUS-01; read-only for juice):
- `bpm`, `tonic` (0–11), `mode` (`ionian`, `mixolydian`, `lydian`, `dorian`, `aeolian`, `phrygian`), `is_silent`.
- **Measured phase** (Godot's "sync the gameplay with audio" recipe), sampled once per frame while a track plays: b_meas = (pos + `AudioServer.get_time_since_last_mix()` − `AudioServer.get_output_latency()` − `first_beat_s`) × bpm / 60, pos = the stream's playback position.
- **Published beat: a monotonic phase-locked loop on `ui_time`.** b advances at r = (bpm / 60) · (1 + c), c = clamp(0.5 · (b_meas − b), −0.02, +0.02) per frame. It never steps and never runs backwards (r ≥ 0.98 · bpm/60). **Track start:** for the first 2 beats the slew limit is ±0.25, so it re-locks within 1–2 beats without a jump. During the 20–40 s gaps it **free-runs** at the last bpm. On pause it holds; on unpause it continues from the held b (the track resumes at the same position).
- `beat_at(t_ui)` = b + r · (t_ui − now), for scheduling up to 0.5 s ahead.
- **Strike detection is per consumer:** each consumer (a site, a work loop) keeps `last_index`; a strike fires when floor((b − offset)/m) > `last_index`, which then updates. Every grid point fires exactly once at any refresh rate, and a long frame fires it late (≤ 1 frame), never twice.
- **Grid multiplier:** a consumer with a target interval picks m ∈ {0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.5, 2} beats whose interval is nearest its target (hammers: nearest 650 ms, which keeps every interval within 520–780 ms from 40 to 200 BPM and gives m = 1 from 81 to 115 BPM, so every slice track strikes on whole beats). Re-picked only on track change.
- `scale() -> PackedInt32Array` and `note(idx) -> int` (semitones above the tonic; idx ≥ 5 adds octaves).
- `data/music.json` gains per-track `bpm`, `tonic`, `mode`, `first_beat_s`. Before the first track: kingdom G mixolydian 88 BPM; clans D dorian 92 BPM (clans §3.4 prompt). One 16th note is 156–179 ms between 84 and 96 BPM.

**Scale choice (never clash with the track):**

| Track mode | Pentatonic used by all pitched juice |
|---|---|
| ionian, mixolydian, lydian | major pentatonic {0, 2, 4, 7, 9} |
| dorian, aeolian, phrygian | minor pentatonic {0, 3, 5, 7, 10} |

Faction identity comes from **timbre and motif contour**, not from a fixed scale: kingdom pitched sounds are a soft coin-bell and a woodblock; clans are a small bronze singing-bowl tap and a felt-mallet log drum. Samples are recorded at C5; `pitch_scale` = 2^(semitones/12), with the tonic placed in the octave that keeps `pitch_scale` in [0.75, 1.5].

**Users:** grid-locked (tempo and pitch): BLD-08 strikes and work song, ECO-04 hod landings and bows, ECO-25 ledger phrases, BLD-11 work loops and chuffs, BLD-14 ripple ticks, ECO-15 upkeep notes. Pitch only (sim tempo): ECO-08/10 chops, ECO-01 mine exits.

## 6. Our own twist (what WC3 did not have)

Each item makes a hidden number readable or gives the base character. None changes an outcome.

1. **Course-laid construction (BLD-06, 08).** Every hammer strike or hod delivery visibly lays the next strip of wall, in that course's own material and sound. A full Hod Chain *looks* twice as busy as a lone builder, and the reveal can never drift from the work.
2. **Sites never play in unison (BLD-08).** Each site takes its own subdivision of the beat and each builder its own humanisation, so a busy base sounds like a workshop, not a metronome; long sites get a hummed work song or a Yurtwright counting its strikes.
3. **Completion you can feel coming (BLD-24).** An accelerando, a crown staged on the ground from the start and hoisted on a rope, a topping-out sprig or a hoisted gong, and a shared wind-up whose final blow lands exactly on the completion tick.
4. **The Hodman tips Agnes into the counting hatch, on the beat (ECO-03, 04).** His hod is his silhouette; gold is heaped in it, billets lie across it in rows (the lumber upgrade is visible), and every deposit is a flick into a hatch that lands on a 16th note. The Yurtwright bows and sets the bag on the felt flap instead.
5. **Coins settle like real coins (ECO-04).** They spill outward off the sill, bounce once, wobble faster and faster to rest, and lie still before they erode, with a tiny accelerating tinkle.
6. **The economy plays in the key of the music, and brightness means saturation (§2.7, ECO-25).** Deposits play composed phrases on the grid; the ledger sounds full when your mines run at capacity and calm when they do not; floating gold sounds dull.
7. **Saturation lanterns, the impatient queue and a mine that breathes (ECO-01, 02, 17).** "Five per mine" is shown on the mine, the sixth worker taps its foot, and the door glows just before each worker steps out.
8. **Tax bite on every trip and every bounty (ECO-05).** Upkeep is something the player watches peel off their income, then a notched coin that stays on every popup.
9. **Truthful rings drawn on the sim's own metric (ECO-21, 22, §2.3).** The levy ring stops at exactly 22 m and the gong's at 30 m from the hall's footprint, drawn by the terrain shader so it drapes over cliffs; no ceremony ever draws a ring, so a ring always means reach.
10. **Kept cargo you can see (ECO-21, 22).** A levy's load stays in the hod on his back; a garrisoned Yurtwright's bag hangs on the yurt's lattice hook, visible through the rolled skirt.
11. **Trees tell you they are about to fall (ECO-10, 19, 20).** The chop steps down the scale, a notch deepens, the trunk leans, the chopper looks up, and the tree never falls toward the camera.
12. **The yard eats your lumber (ECO-31).** Deliveries stack up at the Timber Yard and Casting Pit until the sawyers cut them or the kiln swallows them.
13. **Surveyor's sketch ghost (BLD-01).** Placement looks like boiling ink on parchment with a real inked outline, hatching where the sun would shade, red cross-hatch when blocked, and an ink blot when stamped.
14. **Silhouette preview over the site (BLD-06), and a site that looks abandoned when it is (BLD-25).** Anyone can see what is being built and how far along it is; an unattended site drops its tools, loses its glow and asks for a builder.
15. **Every production building works in its own way (BLD-11).** The dummy is thwacked, the bellows breathe, the wheel turns, the wrestlers circle, the mirror tracks the sun; idle buildings park still.
16. **Research leaves marks (BLD-14).** Blade notches and quilted patches on the Foundry's sign, felt ribbons on a clans mast: a base's tech is readable from its walls, and the upgrade ripple starts from the new mark.
17. **Damage lands where it was dealt (BLD-15).** The wall the ram pounds opens first; siege shots leave bites; every burning building visibly burns, whatever the sprite budget; repaired patches stay fresh for 20 s.
18. **Buildings cave in under their own weight (BLD-16).** The roof sags, the ridge cracks, the walls fall out, and the debris clears inside the dust; a hall makes the world hold its breath first. Tormai felt tears, the lattice folds, and the gong falls last and wobbles like a coin.
19. **Last legs and the last sack (BLD-23, ECO-28).** A building at 10 % leans toward its broken wall and creaks; the last worker out of a dying mine looks back at it.
20. **Faction idle beacons and personality tics (ECO-12, 26).** Idle Hodmen light pipes and pat Agnes; idle Yurtwrights brew tea on three stones; a Hodman bills you for rapid clicks with a notch on his tally stick.
21. **The harass alarm points to the answer (ECO-27).** Workers flinch and look at the raider, and the Levy Horn or the gong button lights up while your worker line is bleeding.
22. **Income tachometer (ECO-30).** The HUD coin spins faster as income rises and stands still when it stops.
23. **Floating-bank strongbox (ECO-23).** Unspent gold piles up beside your hall, the deposits sound dull, and at 2,000 the lid jams and coins roll off.
24. **Admire beat (BLD-26).** The builder steps back and looks up at what it built; at a tier-up a wave of raised tools passes through the base.
25. **Rare hashed delights (ECO-29).** One tree in forty hides a nest; the Croft's stork leaves when it burns and comes home when it is mended.
26. **Donut plumes, unit-aware emission and painted cut-out smoke (§2.3, §2.8, BLD-16).** Set pieces never hide the fight in the ruins, dust skips the spots where units stand, and smoke reads hand-painted.
27. **Stateless GPU particles and stateless audio phrasing.** Coins, chips, debris and streams cost zero CPU per frame and are refresh-rate independent by construction.

## 7. Anti-patterns (do not ship these)

**Feel**
- **Mobile coin showers and slot-machine counters.** No HUD coins on every deposit; routine deposits never punch the counter (ECO-13). A small burst and one popup is the ceiling. (CMB-28's bounty coin flight is the one sanctioned exception.)
- **Number soup.** Merge (350 ms), cap, lanes, and own on-screen deposits only.
- **Disconnected work.** A continuous scanline that rises while builders hammer at nothing. Every strike must lay something (BLD-06).
- **One overshoot for everything.** BACK with s = 1.70158 on world props, or the same bounce on stone, felt and timber. Use the material tokens (§2.4).
- **Props from thin air.** POP_IN (scale from 0) on crowns, sacks, tools, crews or heaps. Physical things are hoisted, dragged, drawn from a belt, set down or climb into view.
- **Generic sparkle.** Glints on linen, felt, chalk or cards; "star glints" at doors; undefined "N motes". Glints only on the §2.3 whitelist (exposed gold, polished bronze or brass, the piece an upgrade just improved) within the budget; every particle is a named kind (§2.3).
- **Decorative ground rings.** Shockwaves on completions and tier-ups dilute the rings that carry information (rule 8).
- **A metronome base.** Every builder striking on the same grid point; one mallet sound for stone, timber and felt.
- **Identical production buildings.** The same chimney, sparks and door glow everywhere with "bellows animate" as a checkbox. Each building gets a specified work loop (BLD-11).
- **The generic worker.** A Hodman with a linen sack instead of his hod; clans workers who deposit exactly like the kingdom.
- **Destruction as a wipe.** A clip plane sweeping down the building with debris at the edge, a dissolve, or a sink into the ground. Buildings cave in (BLD-16); the clip only clears the debris inside the dust.
- **Rewarding bad play.** A richer, fuller sound for floating gold (ECO-23 must sound worse).
- **Screen shake for economy or building events.** Use world-space motion, material response and HUD micro-shakes.
- **Long ceremonies on frequent events.** Repeats get short variants; destruction scales with size.
- **Off-key and off-tempo juice.** Pitched sounds that ignore `MusicClock`; chromatic steps; continuous pitch drifts; endless looping arpeggios.
- **Fanfare without information.** If an effect answers no question and is not rare, cut it or move it to level 2.

**Gameplay safety**
- **The sim waiting on the juice.** No delayed deposits, spawns or availability. Anticipation uses predictions that may miss.
- **Juice writing sim state**, or writing `body.transform` outside the composer.
- **Juice that lies about a mechanic.** Gonged workers dropping cargo the rules keep; a levy hat flying 25 m back to its owner; rings that do not match the sim's distance test.
- **Fog leaks:** blocker rings on unseen units, chalk lines to unexplored mines, smoke, fire or flickering fire light on a fogged enemy building, enemy saturation shown.

**Engine**
- **Frame-counted visuals, periodic motion above 12 Hz, Euler springs, `TIME` in shaders, impulses stamped at frame time.**
- **Timestamps in half-float slots** (`INSTANCE_CUSTOM`, `COLOR`), except the split encoding.
- **More than 16 instance uniforms in a shader**, or scalar uniforms that should be packed vec4s (§2.3 contract).
- **A second particle encoding** next to FND-2's, or new flag bits FND-2 does not have; economy extensions are sign bits.
- **Two features sharing `material_overlay`**; toggling overlays per hit; **auto-indexed instance uniforms** in shaders that share one instance (override + overlay), which alias each other's slots.
- **Prop transforms read before the animation pass** (one-frame lag); per-worker prop MultiMesh writes; glyphs or markers on moving units rewritten by the CPU every frame (use UnitPosTex).
- **Node churn:** no `Node.new()` or `create_tween()` per particle or per deposit; no Label3D per popup.
- **Per-entity draws:** props, popups or ground patches as separate nodes; any fx node casting shadows (except the two falling trees).
- **A second particle system, governor, spring library or light pool.** Extend the shared ones; every spawn goes through `Vfx.allow`.
- **Per-building `OmniLight3D`s, `Decal` nodes, physics bodies for debris.**
- **Ground patches coarser than the terrain** (a 65 m ring on a 32 × 32 patch; 100 m dash strips in one instance) and 1-pixel quads on tiny zones.
- **Per-source frustum queries** (`get_frustum()` allocates); cache once per frame.
- **Alpha-blended smoke columns in one MultiMesh** (they sort inside-out). Use painted cut-outs.

**Readability**
- **Strobing buildings** (> 2.5 Hz whole-building flashes), buildings that bloom when hit, and the same channel for "selected" and "hit".
- **Healthy sites that look on fire** (raw HP / max HP on a site); a fire budget that hides the Burning state.
- **Effects sized in cells as if they were metres;** dust clouds that swallow the builder or the units fighting next to a ruin.
- **"Nearest N" without hysteresis:** fires, overlays and voices that pop while the camera pans.
- **Smoke or fire covering units**; emitters outside footprint × 0.8; dense scaffolds hiding the reveal.
- **Red/green-only signals.** Always add hatching, glyphs or notches.
- **Infinite accumulation.** Rubble, logs, leaf litter and stumps have caps and lifetimes (WLD-31).
- **Literal render priorities** or per-doc numbering. Every fx material reads `src/fx/render_order.gd` (the shared ×10 table).

**IP and culture**
- **Copying WC3 specifics:** lines, select sounds, level-up or completion motifs, gold-mine or scaffold model designs, "+N" font styling. **Faction cliché:** no hide tarps, bone poles, war horns or drums of the WC3 Orc look for the clans (clans §2.5); no blue roofs or gold trim for the kingdom (kingdom §2.5).
- **Banned names in ids** (`hearth`, `wisp` and the rest of `tools/ip/banned_terms.txt`): glyph, prop, stream-region and sound ids are data and must pass the checker.
- **Real sacred items and banned clans imagery** (clans §1.4, §2.3, §2.5): carved faces on standing stones or anything hung on the Naming Stones; a living beast carrying a gong, or a living tortoise as the hall; mirror-hung robes, ribbon fringes on robes, milk libations, horse-hair standards. Clans VO, gag and select text passes the scoped culture lint (§10): "face", "faces", "carved face", "living tortoise", "tortoise head", "mirror robe", "libation", "horse-hair".
