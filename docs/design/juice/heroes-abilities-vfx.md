**Conventions (same as the siblings):**
- Durations are ms at 1× game speed. Distances are metres; `h` is the unit's height.
- Colours are sRGB hex. "HDR n" is an emission multiplier; only n > 1.0 blooms.
- Easings are the Penner functions in `Juice` (combat FND-1): `out_quad`, `in_quad`, `out_cubic`, `in_cubic`, `in_out_sine`, `out_back(c1)`, `in_back`. When no c1 is given, `out_back` uses c1 = 1.70158. **This doc adds two** (request to FND-1, §12): `out_expo(t)` = 1 at t = 1, otherwise 1 − 2^(−10t) (Penner), and `out_bounce(t)` = Penner's four-segment bounce (n1 = 7.5625, d1 = 2.75).
- Named curves POP_IN, RISE_FADE, FLARE and SHAKE are the economy doc's `JuiceCurves` (§2.4), used **unchanged** (SHAKE HUD = 6 px, 100 ms period, 300 ms).
- Gravity for item, coin and prop arcs is the economy's `JUICE_G` = 25 m/s².
- "Own" means the local player, or the observed player in observer mode.
- **★** marks a twist WC3 did not have.

## 1. Feel goals

Heroes are the protagonists of every match, so this layer is about **attention and truth**. The targets:
- You can find your hero in a 60-unit melee in under half a second (a playtest measures it, §10).
- You can read what a spell will do *before* it lands and exactly *where* it lands.
- Every milestone (a level, an item, a cleared camp, the first hero walking out of the altar) feels like a small party that never takes a frame of control away from you.
- Every hero is a *person*: they cast with their own tools **and their own body timing**, level up with their own emblem and gesture, wear their progress, and die in their own silhouette.

Spells speak **one visual language**. There are eight schools, each with its own palette, **shape** and **rhythm**. Every spell follows the same three beats:
1. an anticipation, made from the caster's **own props**, that exactly fills the sim's cast point;
2. a release on the effect tick, with a caster hold and an impact frame sized to the spell's weight;
3. a linger that shows what is still in effect.

We keep WC3's conventions (hero glow, level-up flash, sleeping creeps, glinting loot) and make each one **carry information**:
- the hero ring becomes a character sheet, and the level-up is that emblem standing up and ratcheting a notch;
- timers live on the battlefield instead of in bars;
- every area effect is drawn exactly as big as it is, and nothing cosmetic pretends to be an area.

The texture is the diorama's: painted petals, felt, bronze glints, gunpowder smoke, packed hail and chalk lines. It is never neon, never glitter, never "magic sparkles" in general, and never a glowing orb in a hand. **Every particle is a named painted thing** (a spoke-chip, a bronze fleck, a wax-seal chip, a chalk dash, a petal); there is no anonymous glowing dot anywhere in this doc (HVF-5).

**Eight rules for this domain:**

1. **Truthful.** The visual footprint equals the gameplay footprint (±2 %). Release visuals land on the sim's effect tick, and **a travel visual (petal streak, braid, fuse cord) arrives on that frame, never after it**: the cause is always drawn before or with its effect. Every timer is drawn from a view reader or `sim_time` (§2.1 rule 1), never from a local clock. An expiry that does nothing never looks like a payoff.
2. **Telegraph, then pay off.** Each school has its own wind-up rhythm, and that rhythm finishes exactly at release. An opponent can learn when to dodge, and a caster feels the snap.
3. **Ceremony scales with rarity.** Approximate frequencies and lengths:

   | Event | Happens about | Ceremony length |
   |---|---|---|
   | XP gain | every few seconds | ≤ 0.75 s (tested) |
   | Potion or tea | a few times per game | ≤ 0.6 s |
   | Item pickup, give, drop | a few times per game | ≤ 0.7 s |
   | Purchase | a few times per game | ≤ 0.8 s |
   | Level-up | 9 times per game | 1.5 s (line at 1.15 s) |
   | Camp cleared | 5–7 times per game | 1.6 s |
   | Hero summoned at the altar | 1–3 times per game | 1.2 s (first hero: 2.0 s) |
   | Ultimate | 2–5 times per game | a 7–25 s set piece (Grand Bombard is the 25 s one) |

   Nothing frequent is long.
4. **Readable in the brawl.** Danger is never throttled: zone edges, CC glyphs and timers always draw at full strength. Cosmetic lingers are the first thing cut. Units, team colour, health bars and selection rings are never covered for more than 80 ms (combat §2.2).
5. **Fair.** Juice never leaks information the game hides:
   - fogged or invisible units;
   - enemy cooldowns, XP progress, ultimate readiness and the skill an enemy just learned;
   - your altar's location, your hall's bearing, how many of your heroes are dead and when they come back;
   - which creep carries the item.

   It also never shows anything that did not happen.
6. **Spectacle goes up, not out.** A ground ring with no gameplay behind it has **no edge line**, a soft gradient only, **r ≤ 1.6 m and α ≤ 0.35**. Anything larger goes vertical (pillars, columns, standing emblems, rising dust). A crisp ground ring therefore always means "this area does something **now**". An effect that captures its units at cast time (the Wagon Ring) draws its ring only while it captures, then **hands the truth to the units** it captured (ULT-02's roll call): a ring never keeps promising protection to whoever walks in.
7. **Props, not orbs.** Anticipation comes from the hero's own tools (flask fuse, braid weight, staff mirror, wheel-shield hub) and from the hero's sigil reconfiguring into the school glyph. There is no default hand orb and no second magic circle (ABL-01).
8. **Bodies, not only props.** Each release class has its own anticipation pose and hold (ABL-01); each hero has its own level-up gesture (HER-04), wears its levels (HER-04 "wear"), and dies in its own silhouette (HER-06). Crowd-controlled victims act out their state (STS-08). Identical timing across characters is the slop signature; the motion dialects of §2.1 rule 14 keep two factions from moving alike.

## 2. Shared foundations (read before implementing any row)

### 2.1 Ground rules

**These inherit everything in combat §2 and economy §2.1:**
- The sim is authoritative at 30 Hz. Juice only reads sim state.
- No `World.rng`, no `Engine.time_scale`, no sim delay. Visual randomness is `Juice.hash01` / `Juice.vnoise`.
- Motion is a closed-form function of age, or uses the closed-form spring.
- No `Decal` nodes. No `TIME` in juice shaders (`fx_time`, `ui_time` and `sim_time` only).
- Pool everything. Sim signal handlers only enqueue (combat FND-4); `Vfx._process` drains.
- Fog is honoured: `Vfx.allow(kind, pos, side, importance)` gates every spawn.

**Additions for this domain:**

1. **Three clocks, one for each kind of truth.**
   - `fx_time` (`Vfx.now`, accumulated real delta): cosmetic world motion only (flutter, flicker, loop phases, spring ages).
   - `ui_time` (`Vfx.ui_now`): HUD feedback.
   - **`sim_time` = (`World.tick_count` + `World.interpolation_fraction()`) × `World.DT`**, published once per frame by UnitPosTex (`RenderingServer.global_shader_parameter_set`, declared in `project.godot [shader_globals]`). It freezes on pause (the fraction is held at 1), follows game speed, and follows Godot's `max_physics_steps_per_frame` clamp (8 in 4.7.2) **by construction**, because it is made of ticks. `fx_time` does not: on any frame longer than 8 ticks (267 ms: hitches, and every frame of the 2–3 fps llvmpipe/lavapipe test runs) the sim falls behind wall time.
   - **Every sim-bound start or end is stored as `tick × DT` in sim seconds** (buff ends, zone ends, summon lives, fuse runs, cast starts) and compared with `sim_time` in the shader. There is no re-anchoring loop. Cosmetic motion inside a sim-bound life (a flame tongue's lick, a petal's sway) still uses `fx_time`.
   - Spell anticipation reads **view readers** (`FxRead`, §2.2), which lead the display by one tick so that they reach 1.0 exactly on the frame that processes the release tick and never before it. View readers are never called from sim or AI code (lint test, §10).
2. **Cast sync.** The release visual spawns in the frame that processes `ability_released`, which is the tick the sim applies the effect. On that frame `FxRead.cast_progress()` returns exactly 1.0 (§2.2). As a guard only, any anticipation still below 1.0 on that frame snaps to 1.0. Anticipation never releases early.
3. **Truthful radii.** Every reticle, zone, splash ring, line, cone and aura ring uses the radius, length, width or angle in the ability def, measured to the outer edge of the edge line. Only vertical elements (pillars, smoke columns, curtains) may extend past the radius, and only upward. A unit test enforces this for **every** gameplay shape the doc draws, read from the defs rather than from a hand-kept list (§10).
4. **Info honesty (in addition to fog):**
   - Invisible-to-local units are treated as fogged: no sprites, labels, sounds or XP particles, and no level-up.
   - Enemy XP progress, enemy ultimate readiness and the learn "taste" (HER-05) are never drawn for enemies.
   - Altar dressing (dead-hero count, revive progress) is own and allied only; enemies see the altar idle (HER-07).
   - Enemy soul sparks never fly toward their altar; recall bearings are drawn for the owner only.
   - Creep item carriers are never marked.
   - Previews query exactly what the sim will query, through `Abilities.preview()` (§2.2).
5. **Physics interpolation.** Sigils, glyphs, motes and rigs are `physics_interpolation_mode = OFF` and positioned from `display_pos()`, either through UnitPosTex (HVF-7) or the CPU cache it fills. Teleports use `reset_physics_interpolation()`; `Unit.teleport()` already does this.
6. **Heroes have importance 3** (combat FND-3). Their sprites spawn into a **reserved FxSprites sub-range** (§2.3) that only importance ≥ 3 may use, so "never throttled" is true even when a brawl saturates the shared layers.
7. **Compatibility packing.** On Compatibility, `INSTANCE_CUSTOM` and `INSTANCE_COLOR` are half floats (11-bit significand). Therefore:
   - angles in custom data are **wrapped on the CPU** before writing (θ mod 2π/6 for the 6-fold wheel spokes, mod π for 2-fold patterns, mod 2π otherwise; the gong's twin winds only sway);
   - **no absolute time** (t0, t_end, beat start) is ever stored in custom or colour data; times live in the instance transform's basis rows (32-bit, combat FND-2), in sim seconds for sim-bound times and `fx_time` seconds for cosmetic ones, or are written as relative values (progress 0..1, a phase ∈ [0, 1) against the global `fx_time`);
   - integers in custom data (unit slot, atlas frame + flags) stay ≤ 2047;
   - `tests/render/test_fx_packing.gd` is extended to the sigil, GroundZones, StatusFx and AttachFx layouts on `opengl3` (§10).
8. **No aliasing.** Any periodic visual has a **pattern frequency ≤ 12 Hz** (hard cap), where pattern frequency = rotation rev/s × the pattern's symmetry order (or the oscillation frequency). **Patterns that must read as motion target ≤ 7.5 Hz** (fps/4 at a 30 fps display; 12 Hz is only 2.5 frames per cycle there and strobes). Any spinning or rolling element whose pattern would exceed 7.5 Hz crossfades over 100 ms to its radially blurred painted **spin-disc frame** and back; this applies to **props and rigs too** (the rolling sigil, shield spins, wagon wheels, kegs), not only to school glyphs. Flicker and jitter use `Juice.vnoise(id, t, hz)` with hz ≤ 12 (keys at a fixed rate, interpolated), never a per-frame random. The same spell therefore looks identical at 30, 60 and 144 Hz.
9. **Back-dated periodic spawns.** Trails, embers and smoke curls use combat §2.1's accumulator. Each sub-frame spawn gets t0 = its exact emission time and p0 = the emitter's position at that time (a lerp between last and current display positions, or the projectile's closed-form arc), so 30 Hz shows the same ribbon as 240 Hz, not beads (tested to 1 cm, §10).
10. **Material variants, never `discard` in the live unit shader.** Combat's `unit.gdshader` stays discard-free (early-z for 300 units). Dissolves and dithers swap in a variant material **per surface** only while active (HVF-4: `set_surface_override_material`, never `material_override`, which would replace every surface's textures). Extra surface passes (shimmer, sketch outline, hero outline) go in `GeometryInstance3D.material_overlay` on the affected units only.
11. **One wind, one sun.** Every smoke puff, petal, dust puff and linger drifts with the world wind: `+ atm_wind.xy × atm_wind.z × 0.6 m/s` (world WLD-F1, the same factor as CMB-24 smoke). Mirror glints, sunbeams and petal value pops use the key light direction `atm_sun.xyz` (WLD-01: the sun by day, the moon by night).
12. **Rank is readable.** Ability ranks are public, so they show: particle counts ×1.0 / 1.25 / 1.5 at ranks 1 / 2 / 3 (before tier multipliers), core HDR +0.2 per rank above 1, and a third sound layer (the school tail sweetener, −14 dB) at rank 3. Radii change with rank only where the def says so.
13. **The hero feet budget is three layers** (§2.9). The sigil hosts every other hero-feet element as a mode of its own band.
14. **Motion dialects (no default `out_back` pop-in).** Nothing enters as "scale 0→1 with `out_back`" unless its row says why. Each owner moves in its own curve family:

    | Dialect | Entrances and changes | Settles | Typical beats |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | **Kingdom: stepped clockwork** | a snap (`in_cubic`, 40–60 ms), then a hold | a 3–4° (or 3–4 %) recoil (`out_back(2.0)`, 40–50 ms) | ratchets, clicks, pawl clacks; waves step 20 ms per element |
    | **Tormai: wind and breath** | a swell (`in_out_sine`, 200–420 ms) | a low-damped sway spring (ω 6–9, ζ 0.25–0.35) | gusts, ripples, sways; waves breathe 35 ms per element |
    | **Neutral and creeps** | a stop-motion step (2–3 held poses at 12 Hz) or a plain `out_cubic` | none, or one `out_quad` bounce | loot, camps, shops |

    **Timing variety:** a row's durations come from its dialect and its physics (fall heights, roll lengths), not from a house constant; reviewers reject rows that reuse 120 / 300 ms without a reason.
15. **`no_rotation` heroes.** A hero def may set `"no_rotation": true` (the Uncut: clans §2.5, "nothing about her spins", an IP squint rule). **Every shared system checks it:** the sigil (its winds lean by shear, never by turning; its cast glyph is drawn as straight strokes, ABL-01), XP and power-up particles (straight lines, no arcs that curl), prop flourishes and gags, level-up emblems (they rise facing the camera instead of tilting up), kill styles (combat §4.9, request §12) and deaths. Pendular body motion (her braid chain, the sabre) stays within ±45° of rest and never completes a turn. A test asserts zero spin on every fx element attached to her (§10).
16. **Render hygiene.** Every fx `GeometryInstance3D` (sprites, zones, strips, sigils, emblems, status layers, AttachFx, Beams, XP particles, ground items, afterimages, wagon wheels, the grass ring) sets `cast_shadow = SHADOW_CASTING_SETTING_OFF`, and every fx shader is `render_mode unshaded`, so Compatibility never adds a pass per omni or spot light and no cascade draws them. **Exceptions** (deliberate props that cast shadows and are lit): the Bombard crate, the kegs and the bombard itself, the death props (rolling shield, chimney-hat), and loot parcels.
17. **Travel arrives on the release frame.** A visual that travels from caster to target (a petal streak, the braid, a fuse cord, hail chips flying to spawn points) is **pre-launched**: it leaves at the crossing `FxRead.cast_progress() ≥ 1 − travel_s / cast_point` (the same crossing test as the risers, ABL-01) and arrives on the release frame, so the effect never shows before its cause. If `travel_s` > `cast_point`, the travel is compressed to the cast point; for an instant ability (cast point 0) it is drawn as a 60 ms ribbon from caster to target that is complete on the release frame and fades over 90 ms. Tested (§10).
18. **Chest-anchored one-shots move with their hero.** An FxSprites one-shot spawned at a moving hero (a glint, a seal chip, a soot puff) that lives longer than 150 ms takes the hero's velocity (UnitPosTex texel C, HVF-7) as part of its `v0`; BALLISTIC motion is linear in v0, so this is free. Anything that must stay glued to the body longer uses AttachFx's unit-local space.

### 2.2 Sim → view contract (what `abilities.gd`, `heroes.gd`, `creeps.gd`, items and shops must expose)

None of these systems exist yet. Juice needs the hooks below. They are cheap and deterministic, and the HUD, the AI and replays need most of them anyway. **The sim exposes only tick-exact values**; anything that depends on frame timing (the interpolation fraction) lives in the view-only `FxRead` layer under `src/fx/`, so no sim, AI or replay code can ever read a machine-dependent number.

**Contract rule:** a change to `Events` or `Unit` also updates `docs/architecture.md` §5 and §10 **in the same commit**.

### 2.8 Cost model and monitors (GDScript, container Xeon 2.1 GHz, measured per combat FND-1 methodology)

### 2.9 Hero feet budget

Inside about 1.1 m (≈ 52 px) a selected hero casting in an aura used to draw six concentric things. Now a hero draws at most **three feet layers**:

1. **The selection ring** (0.75 m, SelectionCircles), when selected.
2. **The sigil** (HER-01). It hosts every other hero-feet element as a **mode of its own band**: the cast glyph (on the inner disc), the channel arc, the aura pip, the padlock and iron tint (Wagon Ring), the will-travel dash (recall), the XP arc (outer edge), the level segments (the band itself), the HP cracks and the ult gem.
3. **One transient** (level-up dust, revive ring, lock flash, landing dust).

The contact blob (FND-7, r 0.9 m) is the hero's shadow, not a layer. **Mode priority** when several want the band: channel > cast > lethal lock > XP arc > will-travel > aura pip. Non-hero units keep their own budget: ring + one feet glyph (+ a life ring for summons).

**Language rules**

1. **Squint pairs are split by motion.** Blossom and Ember share warm hues: Blossom **falls** (petals) while Ember **rises** (tongues and embers), and Blossom is pale while Ember is saturated with dark smoke. Rime and Gale share cool whites: Rime is **static and angular** while Gale is **fast and curved**. Gloam is the only school that **darkens**.
2. **Value structure:** cores are ≥ 85 % value, and edges and fills are ≤ 50 %. Gloam inverts this, with a dark core and a light rim. HDR above 1 is allowed only on cores, sparks and flashes, never on fills or edges.
3. **Everything must read without bloom.** Bloom is a bonus; shape and value carry the meaning. (HDR 3.0 survives on `opengl3`, per combat's packing test; Compatibility adds halo sprites, combat §2.4.)
4. **Faction dialects** decorate any school a hero casts. Kingdom: bronze sparks, gunpowder smoke and folk-painted chunkiness. Clans: six-point mirror glints, ochre dust, silver smoke, felt fluff and packed hail. Clans magic stays warm glints, dust, hail and silver smoke (clans §2.2: no blue lightning, no fel green, no teal glow, no ghost glow). Kingdom has no glowing light-from-above and no glowing hands (kingdom §2.3, §6.3); its "holy" is Blossom.
5. **Day/night can change a spell's school ★.** Sunglint is Blossom by day and Moonglint is Gloam by night, so the clock change is visible in the spell itself.
6. **Size:** release bursts match the effect radius. A single-target burst stays within 1.5× the target's ring radius. Spectacle goes **up** (pillars, columns, curtains, discs, standing emblems), not out (§1 rule 6).
7. **IP:** do not reproduce WC3's signature spell looks: a golden beam from the sky onto a heal target, a swirling blue portal vortex, falling ice shards over an area, blue zigzag chain lightning, a pillar of fire from the sky, a spinning thrown hammer that stuns, a dust-ring stomp that slows, fel-green fire, a purple-black blight carpet, **a translucent fade with wind streaks as a stealth vanish**, **a vertical light-beam teleport**, or **a hawk summon with feathers and a cry**. Generic conventions (stun stars, sleep glyphs, a level-up flash, an "!") are fine, drawn with our shapes, timing and sounds.

## 8. Our own twist (what WC3 did not have)

Each twist makes a hidden number readable or gives a moment character. None of them changes an outcome.

**The hero sheet on the ground**
1. **The sigil is a character sheet you can read (HER-01).** The band itself is ten segments that light one per level and fuse into a solid ring at level 10; a 3 px arc shows XP (own heroes only), a 7 px gem shows the ultimate is ready (own and allied only), and the same band hosts the cast glyph, channels, the lethal lock and the recall dash. Every element is clamped to stay legible at max zoom.
2. **The sigil is clockwork that walks with you (HER-01).** The kingdom wheel ratchets one notch per footstep, never a skating turntable; the Tormai gong ripples on the first step and every fourth.
3. **The sigil answers your clicks and cracks with HP (HER-01).** Select a hero and the wheel clicks a notch or the gong ripples; order it and the sigil leans toward the order. Below 35 % and 15 % HP it cracks, and on death it shatters along those cracks.

**Levels and death**
4. **The level-up is the emblem standing to face you (HER-04).** A 70 ms wind-up, then the hero's own emblem rises to face the RTS camera (a true circle, not a flattened ellipse) and ratchets one clacking notch per level, or the gong is struck and rings like real bronze. Each hero adds a gesture: the Marshal raps her shield, the Firewright tips his hat, the Uncut tosses her braid back, Skymirror buffs his mirror.
5. **The bell climbs the music (HER-04).** The level bell climbs the current track's pentatonic, level by level, with brass at 6 and a full chord at 10; it never clashes with the score, and a multi-level gain plays as an arpeggio.
6. **Heroes wear their levels (HER-04).** A brass stud on the shield rim, a soot stripe on the hat, a bronze ring on the braid, a polish ring on the mirror, one per level: a veteran looks like one.
7. **XP in the hero's own material, before its level-up (HER-03).** Ivory spoke-chips click into the kingdom wheel; bronze flecks ting the gong. A level-crossing kill sends them in 180 ms, so cause comes before effect; the arrival tick climbs the scale as the next level nears; at level 5+ creep kills fizzle three times and then stop.
8. **Hero kills pay in shards (HER-06).** The dead hero's six sigil shards fly to the heroes who earned the XP.
9. **Four deaths, four silhouettes (HER-06).** The Marshal sits back against her planted shield, which rolls away once she is gone; the Firewright topples like a plank and coughs soot as his hat pops off; the Uncut kneels upright on her planted sabre after her braid weight drops ("Ilaya's braid is cut."); Skymirror lies back with his mirror throwing one last glint at the sky. They are painted out stroke by stroke, not dissolved in noise.
10. **The soul token goes home, and only then does the altar mourn (HER-06, HER-07).** A hub ember lands in the Oath Hall wheel and only then does its flame gutter; a bronze fleck strikes the name stone and only then does the name go dark. On your side only.
11. **The altar is the progress bar, for you only (HER-07).** The Oath Hall floor wheel speeds up and the Naming Stones relight; enemies see a quiet altar.
12. **The oath and the name (HER-08).** A new hero kneels while the iron floor wheel turns one spoke, or while the gong frame rings and the hero's mark is carved into a stone; then the sigil rolls (kingdom) or skims on a gust (Tormai) out of the altar to the hero's feet.

**Spells**
13. **Props and bodies, not orbs (ABL-01).** Every spell is wound up with the hero's own tools and the release class's own body shape: a throw winds back, a lash coils, a raise dips then reaches, a slam rises on its toes then sinks. The kingdom sigil clicks three times into the school glyph; the Uncut's slashes three straight strokes.
14. **Cause before effect, always (ABL-01).** Petals, braids, fuse cords and hail chips are pre-launched so they arrive on the frame the spell lands.
15. **Spells have weight (ABL-01).** A flick releases instantly; a throw holds 60 ms; a raise 80; a slam 90 and squashes; an ultimate holds 110 ms with an impact frame, a Combat-bus hole before it and the hero's shout on it; ordinary casts carry an effort breath.
16. **Diegetic timers instead of cast bars (ABL-07).** A fuse cord that can be defused; stun stars that fall one per half-second; root coils that unwind; a rack of powder kegs emptying into the bombard; a hail-swift that drips as it melts; a shield rim that cools; lock rims that drain; an eclipse corona and a grass ring that thin. Every duration reads on the battlefield, on the sim's clock.
17. **Truthful radii, allegiance edges, honest cosmetics (ABL-06, §1 rule 6).** Every area is drawn exactly as big as it is, checked by a test for every shape; edges are smooth, notched or dashed by allegiance; a cosmetic ground ring is always small, soft and edgeless.
18. **The Wagon Ring's roll call (ULT-02).** The wheels rise in a wave from her facing, race once round the ring calling each captured unit by name with an iron lock rim, then collapse onto those units: the protection rides on the soldiers, not on the ground, and the end stamps "saved ×N".
19. **Fire that reacts to its victims (ABL-06).** Naphtha flames pulse on each damage tick and lean toward the units they burn; they flare when someone walks in.
20. **The spells notice each other (ABL-08).** Hail over fire hisses into steam; a Gale streak carves a lane through smoke; petals curl into embers in fire; wheels rolling through fire catch alight.
21. **Outcome previews you can feel (ABL-04).** Heal preview, the sim's real Braid Lash destination, Sunglint's exact victims, Fuse Mark's blast ring, the bombard's rings, the recall travellers; each newly captured enemy ticks as you sweep.
22. **Blossom, not holy; sun-lightning, not blue (§5).** Our holy magic is warmth and falling blossom; storm magic is yellow-white with ochre dust.
23. **Day/night changes a spell's school (§5).** Sunglint by day, Moonglint by night; Swallowed Sun swallows the sun by day and the moon by night, and fire burns ×1.3 inside your own dome.

**Statuses, summons, items and camps**
24. **Auras that keep time (STS-02).** Measured Cadence pops its glyphs on the beat and soldiers stamp or tap on every second beat; its tick plays only when it matters.

## 9. Anti-patterns (rejected in review)

**Feel**
1. **The magic circle and the glowing hand orb on every spell.** The AI-clone template. Anticipation comes from props and the sigil's own cast mode (ABL-01).
2. **The generic gold pillar as a level-up.** The level-up belongs to the hero's emblem; the thin fresnel pillar is only a far-zoom marker, and it belongs to level-up and revive only. Heals and buffs use their school shapes.
3. **Cosmetic ground rings** larger than 1.6 m, or with an edge line. They read as areas. Go vertical instead (§1 rule 6).
4. **The same death for every hero.** Each hero gets a signature beat before the shared dissolve.
5. **Glow rings under everything.** Only heroes get a sigil, and the sigil hosts every other hero-feet element (§2.9). Buffs live at the feet as small glyphs.
6. **Rainbow and glitter magic.** Each school has one palette, one shape family and one rhythm. Do not mix schools inside one spell except for the documented pairs: the Wagon Ring is Blossom + Stone; Burning Lens is Orrery + Ember; Hail-Swifts are Rime with a Gale wind dialect.
7. **Buff icon stacks over heads** (MMO clutter). One head glyph at most; buffs go to the feet; the complete list lives in the HUD.
8. **Cast bars over units.** Use diegetic timers and the sigil's channel arc.
9. **XP number spam.** WC3 never printed "+25 XP" on every kill, and neither do we. Spoke-chips, bronze flecks and the bar carry XP; numbers appear only for tomes, the diary and the camp stamp.
10. **Long ceremonies on frequent events.** Potions ≤ 0.6 s, pickups ≤ 0.7 s, purchases ≤ 0.8 s, XP ≤ 0.75 s.
11. **Screen shake for spells.** Only the whitelisted trauma for own slam ultimates near the focus (UI §5.4).
12. **Full-screen flashes, white-outs, or tints that cover the centre of the playfield.** The ultimate vignette stays outside 55 % of the screen radius and under α 0.14.
13. **Releases with no weight.** A slam that lands like a flick; an ultimate without a hold, an impact frame or a shout.
14. **Interchangeable bodies.** The same lean, hold, squash or kneel for every hero and class. Each release class has its own pose and hold (ABL-01), each hero its own level gesture (HER-04) and death silhouette (HER-06).
15. **Default pop-in and house timings.** Everything scaling in from 0 with `out_back`, and durations clustered on one or two constants. Use the faction motion dialects (§2.1 rule 14).
16. **Anonymous glowing dots.** "Motes" and "sparkles" with no named painted sprite or dialect (HVF-5). The parcel is opened, not sparkled; XP is spoke-chips and bronze flecks.
17. **Stock shader looks on the big moments.** A hash-noise dissolve with a glowing rim; a Bayer screen-door for stealth. Heroes are painted out stroke by stroke and sketched in as an under-drawing (HVF-4).
18. **Permanent metronomes.** A sound or ring that repeats forever on a unit you keep selected (Measured Cadence ticks and rings only when it matters, STS-02).
19. **Checkbox juice.** Values below perception: α changes under 0.1, scale changes under 2 %, offsets under 2 px, data under 3 px, flourishes behind triggers that never fire.

**Truth and fairness**
20. **Spell visuals bigger than their hitbox,** or fills that bleed past the edge. A test enforces this.
21. **Releasing early.** A flash or sound before the sim applies the effect; an anticipation that ends before the cast point does; a lagging reader that snaps the last 11 % on release.
22. **Local-clock timers** that drift from the sim (stars, fuses, kegs, rings). Always read sim start and end ticks through `FxRead` or `sim_time`; never `fx_time` for a sim duration (it drifts whenever the sim clamps to 8 steps a frame).
23. **Timers that promise a payoff that never comes.** A fuse ring on a harmless expiry; an expiring bombard that looks like it exploded.
24. **Information leaks:**
    - motes, sparks or sounds from fogged or invisible units;
    - XP arcs, ult gems or learn tastes on enemy heroes;
    - altar dressing that shows the enemy how many heroes are dead and when they return;
    - soul sparks or recall streaks pointing at enemy altars or halls;
    - marking which creep carries the item;
    - previews that highlight hidden units.
25. **Lying effects.** A summon that looks untargetable during its arrival; a revived hero that looks absent while already targetable; leashing creeps that shimmer as if invulnerable when they are not; a level-up flare that fires before the XP that caused it arrives.
26. **Dither for anything but invisibility.** Summons decay in their own material; leashing creeps get a regen rim and footprints.
27. **Effects before causes.** A heal number before its petals arrive, a yank before the braid touches, a debuff before its cord loops. Travel visuals are pre-launched to arrive on the release frame (§2.1 rule 17).
28. **Rings that outlive their truth.** A crisp area kept on screen for an effect that was decided at cast; it promises protection to whoever walks in (the Wagon Ring hands its truth to the units, ULT-02).
29. **Random pitch on musical or timed cues.** Ladder bells, fuse ticks, cadence and XP ticks, and risers are `pitch_locked` (§7).
30. **Circular motion on the Uncut through a shared system.** Sigil spins, spiralling particles, orbiting kill styles and tilting emblems all check `no_rotation` (§2.1 rule 15).

**Readability**
31. **Effects over units, team colour, rings or health bars** for more than 80 ms. Fills stay at α ≤ 0.35 (GLOAM_DARK excepted), tubes are fresnel-weighted, near-side rigs fade, emitters sit on donuts.
32. **Colour-only coding.** Allegiance, rarity, rank, buff/debuff borders and validity always have a shape or hatching code.
33. **Throttling danger.** Cutting zone edges, CC glyphs, timers or hero events in a big fight. Cut cosmetic lingers instead, and reserve sprite slots for heroes.
34. **Bloom on units or sigils.** Unit and sigil emission stays ≤ 1.0, except inside the level-up emblem; a render test fails any other sigil instance above 1.0 (the gem's sparkle lives on the HUD).
35. **Sky rigs parked over a fight.** An eclipse disc, a window pane or a far-side wheel covering units for seconds. They fade under the footprint rule (ULT-01).
36. **Sub-pixel data.** Pips, hairlines and gems of 1–4 px. Every sigil element is clamped (§2.6).

**Engine**
37. **Per-effect nodes:** a `GPUParticles3D` per spell, an `OmniLight3D` per hero, a `Tween` per particle, `Node.new()` per cast, a Label3D per number. Pool everything; transients are slot writes.
38. **Per-frame CPU writes for unit-attached instances.** Positions come from UnitPosTex; instances are written on events.
39. **`discard` in the shared unit shader, or `next_pass` on the shared material.** Use the DISSOLVE and DITHER variants per surface (`set_surface_override_material`, never `material_override`, which drops each surface's textures) and `material_overlay` on the affected units only.
40. **Ring buffers that evict live gameplay visuals.** Protected records and ultimate rigs are never evicted.
41. **High-frequency spins, flickers and tremors** that alias by refresh rate (> 12 Hz pattern frequency, per-frame random jitter).
42. **Absolute times or unwrapped angles in half-float instance data** on Compatibility.
43. **`Decal` nodes** for reticles or zones. Unsupported on Compatibility; use GroundZones.
44. **Frame-counted visuals,** `lerp(a, b, k)` per frame, or trails spawned per frame at the current position. Use age-based curves, `damp` and back-dated accumulators.
45. **Juice writing sim state,** reading `World.rng`, or emitting gameplay signals from fx code.
46. **First-use shader compiles at the payoff moment.** Warm up every material at map load, in the real scene with real lights, fog and shadows (a hidden SubViewport compiles the wrong Compatibility variants) (HVF-6).
47. **Frame-dependent readers in sim or AI code.** The interpolation fraction lives only in `FxRead`; a lint test keeps it out of `src/systems`, `unit.gd` and the AI (§2.2).
48. **High-water `visible_instance_count`.** Partitioned slot ranges that draw degenerate grids above the live ones. Compact with swap-remove (§2.4).
49. **Filtered height textures across cliff walls.** They tent zones and tip sigils at every cliff; sample the exact per-tile corners (HVF-2).
50. **Shadows and lit passes on fx geometry.** Every fx node is unshaded and casts no shadow, except the listed props (§2.1 rule 16).
51. **`depth_prepass_alpha` as a sort for translucent rigs.** Its prepass discards α < 0.99; sort the few instances on the CPU (§2.7).

**IP**
52. **Copying WC3 specifics:** see §5 rule 7. Also no WC3 level-up model or sound, no "has fallen" banner wording, no WC3 item-chest model or icons, and no superseded names (the clans review renamed several; `check_banned_terms.py` runs on all data this doc seeds).
