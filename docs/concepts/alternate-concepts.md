# Alternate game concepts (shelved)

These four original world/faction concepts were pitched by a creative panel on 2026-10-16 for Vibecraft. The user decided Vibecraft itself should stay as close to Warcraft III as legally possible, so these are **saved for possible future games**. Each was scored by three judges (WC3 fan, art director, producer); scores and notes are at the end of each concept.


---

## Vibecraft: The Toll and the Hush

*Pitch angle: resonance*

**Logline.** The loudest kingdom ever cast in bronze marches into the twilight forest of the moth-folk who drink sound. It's a heroic, chunky, hand-painted RTS where every spell is a note, every army is a choir, and silence is the deadliest weapon.

### World
The world of Tessitura was sung into being by the First Chord. When that Chord faded, its notes fell to earth and hardened into Chordstone, a crystal that still hums and powers every spell, forge and war machine. Where its echoes soaked into the soil, Tonewood forests grew: hollow-trunked trees that whistle in the wind and make the finest drums, bows and beams. Everyone in Tessitura agrees that sound is power. They disagree about what to do with it.

The Bronze Carillon is a boisterous bell-founding kingdom on the highland moors. They believe that a bell loud enough (the Great Peal, a bell the size of a mountain) can ring the First Chord back into the world and make it whole. Their guilds cast bells, blow horns and march to brass bands, and nearly all of them are a bit deaf. The Velvet Hush are nocturnal moth-folk of the twilight forests. They tend the Undersong, the last faint whisper of the original Chord, which can only be heard in silence. Every new Carillon bell drowns it a little more, and if it goes silent the Chord is lost forever. Neither side is evil: one wants to shout the world back to life, the other wants to listen while it heals. The war starts in Chimewood Hollow, where a Carillon survey expedition comes to mine the Singing Veins for the Great Peal and wakes the Hush. The tone is a heroic Saturday-morning epic: saturated, warm and funny. Half-deaf shouting bell-ringers fight politely whispering moths who keep getting distracted by lamps, and every battle sounds like a concert.

### Why it isn't generic
1) The core conflict is LOUD vs QUIET instead of humans vs orcs. It plays out in territory (golden Peal rings fighting violet Hush mist on the ground), in mechanics (the Carillon's Toll reveals; the Hush's silence hides and cancels auras) and in the game's own audio. 2) Neither faction is a stock fantasy race. One is bronze bell-folk who wear bells as armor and fire trumpet-guns. The other is velvet moth-folk with wing-cloaks, eye-spot markings and lanterns. There are no plate knights, no tusked green brutes, no elves and no undead. 3) Every system ties to sound. Gold is humming Chordstone, lumber is whistling Tonewood, food is 'Voices' in your army's choir, and upkeep is 'Dissonance', so the soundtrack slightly detunes as upkeep rises. Detection is a bell toll, stealth is silence, and the Hush ultimate cuts the music to near-silence before an explosive echo. 4) The audio is part of the gameplay. Hit sounds are pitch-snapped to the score's current chord and lightly quantized in time, so fights literally play as percussion. The Toll lands on the music's downbeat, and casting on the beat empowers abilities. The camera muffles sound inside Hush zones. 5) The charm is original. Shouty, mishearing brass-band workers face whispering ASMR moths who apologize and then stab you. 6) It still delivers the full WC3 loop: leveling heroes with ultimates, creep camps with item drops, a neutral shop, upkeep tiers, small armies of 20-40 units, day/night and a 3/4 camera.

### Resources
GOLD analog, CHORDSTONE: humming crystal shards from Singing Veins, outcrops of tuning-fork-shaped crystal that glow teal-gold and hum louder as they deplete. Up to 5 workers per vein, mining in plain view so the activity reads from the camera. Carillon Bellhands tap it with mallets in rhythm; Hushlings hum it to sleep until shards drop. LUMBER analog, TONEWOOD: resonant timber from Echo Trees, hollow trunks with natural flute-holes that whistle in the wind (ambient sound comes from the forest itself). Carillon saw it; the Hush peel bark strips. FOOD analog, VOICES: every unit is a voice in your army's choir. The town hall gives 15 Voices, each supply building (Bellhouse or Silk Cradle) gives 10, and the cap is 80. Unit costs are 1-2 Voices for line units, 4 for siege and 5 for heroes. UPKEEP, DISSONANCE: 0-40 Voices is In Tune (100% Chordstone income), 41-60 is Off-Key (-30%) and 61-80 is Cacophony (-60%). The UI shows a tuning-fork meter whose needle drifts off-center, and as a fun audio cue the faction music detunes by a few cents and picks up a wobbly kazoo layer at Cacophony, so you HEAR your economy straining. FACTION MINI-RESOURCE: the Hush also bank ECHOES (max 10 or 20) harvested from deaths. The Carillon instead harvest TIME, through the Toll rhythm.

### Faction: The Bronze Carillon

**Fantasy.** A cheerful, industrial, brass-band kingdom of bell-founders. Its armies march to fanfares, wear bells as armor and fire sonic rings from giant horns. The fantasy is being the loudest, proudest army on the field: your war machine is literally a marching band, and the beat makes you stronger.

**Unique mechanic.** PEAL & TOLL. The Great Carillon and Belfry Watch towers project Peal: golden ripple rings on the ground, about 900 units of radius, 25% larger by day. Allied units inside Peal are 'On Tempo' (+10% attack speed). Every 8 seconds (4 bars at 120 BPM, locked to the music's downbeat) all bells TOLL. A visible shock-ring sweeps through the Peal and does three things: units inside get Fortissimo for 2s (+25% damage); Hush-Shrouded enemies inside are revealed for 2s; and any ability cast during the 2s Fortissimo window gets its listed bonus. Two things take the Peal into the field: the Maestro's Metronome aura and the planted Tollwagon. Peal and Hush cancel each other where they overlap, except within each building's inner radius, so the map becomes a tug-of-war between sound and silence. For players who don't want rhythm play, a 'Conductor's Assist' toggle auto-delays spell casts to the next Toll. The bonus is deliberately modest so rhythm play is a skill expression, not a requirement.

**Visual identity.** Silhouettes are circles and flared bells, which read as loud and open. Helmets are domed bells, bodies are wide-bottomed bell skirts, weapons have flared horn mouths, and shoulders are thick and round. From the top-down camera a Carillon army reads as round bronze domes with fluttering pennants. Proportions are stocky and chunky with oversized hands, mallets and horns. Materials are polished bronze with painted highlights, green verdigris patina in the crevices, red brick, oak beams, leather aprons and glowing molten metal. Palette: polished bronze #C27C3A, bronze shadow #6B3E1E, verdigris #4FA58C, forge glow #FF8A2B, bone cream #F0DFB4, soot charcoal #2E2A28. Team color goes on the cloth tabard or sash, a small pennant flag on every unit's back (the most visible area from above), the band around the helmet crown, and the long banners hanging from building belfries. Emissives are warm orange forge-glow and gold sonic rings.

**Audio identity.** VOICES: loud, hoarse, cheerful shouters with brass-band working-class warmth. Everyone is half-deaf from the bells, so they mishear orders and shout. TTS output gets a short metallic ring tail. Sample lines (all original): Bellhand: 'Mallet's ready!' / 'EH? Build WHAT? ...Righto!' / 'Tink-tink, here we go.' / annoyed: 'I've got a bell in me ear AND you pokin' me head!'. Tollguard: 'DING DING, comin' through!' / 'I AM the armor!' / annoyed: 'Every time you click me I ring a little. Stop it.'. Blunderhorn: 'Point me at somethin' with ears!' / 'LOUD AND PROUD!' / annoyed: 'WHAT? ...WHAT? I can't hear you, I'm a horn guy.'. Chimesinger: 'In tune, and on time.' / annoyed: 'You are... very slightly flat.'. Hilde: 'Strike the bell! Wake the world!'. Ottavio: 'From the top!' / 'And-a ONE, two, three...' / annoyed: 'Do NOT tap the baton. I tap the baton.'. SFX: every action is struck metal. Selection is a handbell in D, building completion is a full peal, deaths are a cracked-bell 'clunk'. MUSIC instrumentation: brass band (cornets, euphonium, tubas), carillon and tubular bells, anvil percussion, field snare, timpani, pipe organ and a male work-song choir. The style is a marching, hummable 120 BPM in D major/mixolydian.

**Worker.** Bellhand: a squat apprentice in a leather apron and a dented bell-pot helmet, with an oversized tuning mallet and a small handbell on the belt. He mines Chordstone by tapping the Singing Vein in rhythm (the taps fall on the beat and join the soundscape), saws Tonewood with a two-handled saw, and builds and repairs with hammer clangs. Extra ability 'Tune Up': repairs buildings and the Tollwagon faster when done on the Toll.

**Units**

- Tollguard - melee frontline tank - a stout soldier wearing an entire bronze bell as body armor (the skirt ends at mid-thigh over stubby legs), with a round gong-shield and a bell-clapper mace. From above he reads as a big bronze dome with a team-color pennant - BELLMAIL (passive): each hit he takes rings him and stores Resonance. At 8 stacks his next swing releases a Clang shockwave (AoE damage plus a 0.5s stagger). With Fortissimo the shockwave radius doubles.
- Blunderhorn - ranged infantry - a lanky gunner (deliberately contrasting with the stout Tollguard) with cotton-stuffed ears and an ear-trumpet helmet. He shoulders an enormous flared brass horn-gun that fires visible gold sonic rings, and the flared bell-mouth is the unit's readable silhouette - BRASS BLAST: a short cone blast that knocks enemies back and interrupts channels. With Fortissimo it also deals bonus damage and breaks Shroud.
- Chimesinger - support caster - a robed singer in verdigris and cream with a tall high collar and a staff hung with swaying tubular chimes (the chimes sway procedurally) - MENDING CHIME (autocast): rings a soothing chord that heals allies in a small radius over 3s. With Fortissimo it also cleanses Silence and Hush debuffs, which is the Carillon's answer to the moths.
- Tollwagon - siege and mobile Peal source - a NON-BIPED steam-cart on four chunky wheels with puffing brass exhaust pipes and a giant bronze bell hanging from a gantry. It is fully procedural: wheels spin, the bell swings on each Toll and the pipes puff - PLANT & PEAL: anchors in place to become a portable Peal source. While planted its attack becomes the long-range Doom Toll, which deals heavy damage to buildings and double damage on the Toll.

**Heroes**

- Hilde Clangmore, the Bellwright Thane - melee strength hero, frontline tank and initiator. She is a broad-shouldered master bell-founder with a braided beard-like hair rope, a bell-headed warhammer and a small bell strapped to her back - (1) HAMMERFALL: leaps to a target point and slams a bronze ring that stuns for 1.5s and reveals Shrouded units (Fortissimo: 2.5s stun). (2) RINGING AEGIS: wraps an ally in a resonant bronze shield that absorbs damage; when it breaks it CLANGS and damages nearby enemies. (3) IRON TEMPO (aura): nearby allies attack faster, and the bonus doubles for 2s after each Toll. (4) ULTIMATE, THE GREAT TOLL: a colossal spectral bell drops from the sky onto the target area for massive damage and a stun. It then stays planted for 10s as a Peal source that rings every 2s, slowing enemies and revealing Shroud.
- Maestro Ottavio Tumult, the Grand Conductor - ranged caster and army buffer. He is a flamboyant conductor in a swallow-tail coat over a brass breastplate, with wild white hair, a monocle and a baton tipped with a glowing tuning crystal - (1) CRESCENDO: target allied units gain stacking attack speed for 6s, then the effect bursts into a heal scaled by the stacks. (2) DISCORD: fires a jagged line of clashing notes that damages and Silences enemies for 3s (Fortissimo: longer line). (3) METRONOME (passive aura): he carries a portable Peal, so nearby allies are always 'in Peal' and receive the Toll anywhere on the map. (4) ULTIMATE, GRAND FINALE: for 12s all allies in a large radius attack only on the beat, but every on-beat strike deals triple damage. The music swells to full orchestra and ends in a sonic shockwave that knocks everything back.

**Buildings**

- Great Carillon - town hall: trains Bellhands, takes resource drop-offs, provides 15 Voices, is the main Peal source and origin of the Toll, and upgrades through 3 tiers - a squat bronze-and-brick tower with a green-patina dome and an open belfry holding a huge bell that visibly swings on every Toll, with team-color banners hanging from the belfry arches.
- Bellhouse - supply (+10 Voices) - a cozy brick cottage with a small cupola bell and a rooftop chime-wheel that spins in the wind (procedural rotation); it gives a tiny 'ding' on each Toll.
- Bell Foundry - barracks: trains Tollguard and Blunderhorn, plus the Tollwagon once a Chime Spire exists - a stone forge with a glowing molten-bronze pour spout (emissive and flickering), a giant bell mold hanging on chains and two chimneys that puff smoke in rhythm.
- Chime Spire - arcane building: trains Chimesingers and researches upgrades (Resonant Bellmail, Wider Fortissimo Window, Brass Lungs) - a slender cream-stone spire wrapped in spiraling tubular chimes that slowly rotate, crowned by a glowing crystal tuning fork.
- Hall of Fanfares - hero altar: summons and revives heroes - an open bandstand pavilion ringed with giant brass horns pointing at the sky like organ pipes; the horns blast a fanfare when a hero is summoned.
- Belfry Watch - defensive tower: attacks with sonic rings, extends Peal and reveals Shrouded units on each Toll - a tall timber-and-bronze watchtower topped with a lookout bell (a kitbash of the Bellhouse base and the Great Carillon bell, so it needs no new model).

### Faction: The Velvet Hush

**Fantasy.** Graceful, eerie-but-adorable nocturnal moth-folk who drink sound, weave silence like silk and fight with whispers, echoes and lantern-light. The fantasy is being the ambush predator of the battlefield: you vanish into the quiet, strike unheard, then turn the noise of the battle against its makers.

**Unique mechanic.** HUSH & ECHOES. The Hollow Lantern, Lull Lanterns, the Chrysalith and Old Mother Murmur spread Hush: a soft violet dusk-mist on the ground, 25% larger at night. Inside Hush, Hushkin units that stand still for 2s become SHROUDED, invisible until they attack or a Toll reveals them. Enemy auras and Peal buffs are muted inside Hush, and the game's audio is low-pass filtered when the camera is over it, so players literally hear the silence (critical alerts are whitelisted). ECHOES: any unit that dies inside Hush or near a Hushkin hero, friend or foe, leaves a glowing sound-wisp that is auto-collected into a faction pool (max 10, raised to 20 by an Echo Well upgrade). Echospinners spend Echoes to REPLAY a translucent copy of that exact unit type, including an enemy Tollguard or Blunderhorn. Heroes spend Echoes to supercharge abilities. The noisier the enemy army, the more ammunition the Hush gets.

**Visual identity.** Silhouettes are crescents, teardrops and downward-pointing triangles, which read as quiet and closed. Folded wing-cloaks make long tapered triangle capes, feathery antenna plumes form a strong V on the head, crescent blades and hooked lantern poles complete the shape set, and the posture is tall, upright and floaty. From the top-down camera you read a V of antennae on top of a wide wing triangle. Proportions: big dark glossy eyes, huge fluffy fur collars, slim limbs that are still chunky enough to read (no spindly parts). Materials are velvet moth-fur with painted soft gradients, dark lacquered chitin, paper lanterns, silk cocoon-wrapping, driftwood and pale moon-stone. Palette: dusk violet #3B2D5E, velvet plum #6A4A82, moth-dust fawn #D9CBB0, ink black #17131F, moonglow teal #7FE8DA (emissive), pale lilac light #C9B8F0. Team color goes on the large EYE-SPOTS painted on the backs of the wing-cloaks (big, central and perfectly visible from above), the antenna tips, lantern tassels, and the silk streamers on buildings. Emissives are cool teal lantern glow and lilac motes, kept separate from the Carillon's warm orange.

**Audio identity.** VOICES: soft, breathy whispers, polite and slightly eerie, near-ASMR. They are always gently shushing, and the running comedic tell is that they get distracted by lights. TTS output gets a gentle low-pass and a reverse-reverb swell into each line. Sample lines (all original): Hushling: 'Shh... yes?' / 'Softly, softly.' / annoyed: 'Ooh, a lamp— no. No. Working.'. Moonsickle: 'We cut the quiet way.' / 'They won't hear the end.'. Lanternwing: 'Light finds them.' / annoyed: 'Please... stop tapping the glass.'. Echospinner: 'Every sound remembers.' / 'I kept a little of you.'. Old Mother Murmur: 'Hush now, little noise.' / annoyed: 'Dear, I can hear you clicking. I can hear everything.'. Vesper: 'You won't hear me coming. That's rather the point.' / annoyed: 'Click once more and I whisper your name to the dark.'. SFX: velvet wing-flutters, silk zips, glass tings, held breaths and reversed tones. Selection is a soft glass-harmonica note in D, and deaths are a sigh that dissolves into an Echo chime. MUSIC instrumentation: glass harmonica, celesta, music box, waterphone, bowed vibraphone, breathy female and children's choir humming, seed-pod shakers, a soft frame drum and cello drones. It runs at 60 BPM, half-time of the Carillon tempo, in D dorian, so both factions' music interlocks in battle.

**Worker.** Hushling: a small, round, fuzzy moth child with a huge fluffy collar, stubby antennae, oversized dark eyes, a lantern-on-a-stick and a woven basket. It mines Chordstone by humming a lullaby until the crystal 'falls asleep' and a shard drops off, peels long strips of Tonewood bark, and builds by spinning silk, so structures grow out of a cocoon. Extra ability 'Lamp Out': douses its lantern to become Shrouded instantly inside Hush, which helps it escape raids.

**Units**

- Moonsickle - melee ambusher - a tall, slender moth warrior in dark lacquered chitin with a wide wing-cloak bearing big team-color eye-spots and a huge crescent-moon sickle-glaive. From above it reads as a triangle cape plus a crescent - HUSHSTRIKE (passive): attacking out of Shroud deals +100% damage and Silences the target for 2s.
- Lanternwing - ranged support-damage - a robed moth carrying a long hooked pole with a glowing paper lantern; it attacks by releasing homing moonlight moths. The lantern glow reads clearly at night - MOONLURE: hangs a decoy lantern (200 HP) at a target point for 6s, and enemy units in range compulsively auto-attack the lantern instead of your units. This is the moth-to-flame joke turned around.
- Echospinner - support caster - an elder moth with enormous feathery antenna plumes and a distaff on its back that spins glowing threads of sound - REPLAY: spends 1 Echo to spin a translucent, rippling copy of a recently fallen unit type (friend or foe) that fights for 30s. The copy reuses the original model with an echo shader, so it costs no new art.
- Chrysalith - siege and mobile Hush source - a NON-BIPED giant chrysalis husk wrapped in silk with glowing teal veins and three dangling silk tendrils. It hovers and bobs and needs no rig (bob, slow spin, pulsing emissive, tendril vertex-wobble) - UNMAKE: channels on a building to drain its sound, dealing heavy damage over time and silencing it (no training, no Toll, no tower attacks) while it drains. It projects a small Hush around itself.

**Heroes**

- Old Mother Murmur, the Lantern Matriarch - ranged caster and controller. She is a stooped grand moth matron with an enormous lacy lunar-moth wing-shawl, big round spectacles and a tall staff hung with a cage-lantern full of glowing motes - (1) SWALLOW SOUND: enemies in a target area are Silenced for 3s, and each one silenced yields an Echo. (2) MOTH MOTES: releases a glowing moth swarm that orbits a target ally, damaging nearby enemies and blinding them (miss chance); spending an Echo doubles its duration. (3) VELVET DUSK (aura): nearby allies regenerate and become Shrouded after 1s instead of 2s, and she projects Hush around herself. (4) ULTIMATE, THE LONG HUSH: drops a dome of perfect silence for 8s. Inside it the music and SFX cut to near-silence, enemies cannot cast, their auras are off and the Toll does nothing. All damage enemies take inside is stored, and when the dome lifts the stored noise erupts as a Thunderous Echo that deals 50% of it again in one huge burst.
- Vesper Sablewing, the Echo Duelist - melee agility assassin. She is a sleek black-and-silver moth duelist with twin crescent blades, a tattered wing-cape with giant team-color eye-spots and a porcelain half-mask - (1) VELVET STEP: blinks behind a target enemy and Silences it for 1.5s. (2) AFTERIMAGE (passive): each attack echoes, repeating 0.75s later as a ghostly second hit for 40% damage. (3) EYESPOT FLASH: flares her wing eye-spots so enemies in a cone are terrified and flee for 2s. (4) ULTIMATE, THOUSAND ECHOES: she becomes untargetable and ricochets between up to 8 enemies, and each strike echoes twice. Every kill during it grants an Echo.

**Buildings**

- Hollow Lantern - town hall: trains Hushlings, takes resource drop-offs, provides 15 Voices, is the main Hush source, stores Echoes and upgrades through 3 tiers - an ancient hollow, twisted tree with a giant glowing paper-lantern cocoon hanging inside its arch, with team-color silk streamers and moths circling (a procedural particle orbit).
- Silk Cradle - supply (+10 Voices) - a driftwood frame with clusters of softly glowing cocoon pods that sway gently; the pod mesh is reused, scaled up, as the Chrysalith.
- Duskloom - barracks: trains Moonsickles and Lanternwings - a giant wooden loom weaving a sheet of dusk-violet silk, with a shuttle that slides back and forth (procedural) and moon-stone weights.
- Echo Well - caster and siege building: trains Echospinners and Chrysaliths, raises the Echo cap and researches upgrades (Deeper Shroud, Lingering Echoes, Silent Wings) - a mossy moon-stone well ring with violet sound-wisps spiraling up out of it, under a bent tree from which chrysalides hang.
- Moonshrine - hero altar: summons and revives heroes - a pale crescent-moon stone arch flanked by two moth statues with spread wings, with floating lilac motes and a hanging bell-less wind-silk.
- Lull Lantern - defensive tower: projects Hush, fires moonlight moths and slows the attack speed of enemies near it - a tall crooked driftwood pole with stacked glowing paper lanterns (a kitbash of the Lanternwing lantern and Silk Cradle driftwood, so it needs no new model).

### Later factions

- The Bellowmire Chorus - frog-folk bog clans who throat-sing and drum on their own inflating vocal sacs (a simple scale-bone procedural puff on a humanoid rig). Mechanic 'Call and Response': units that attack the same target in sequence chain-amplify one another, so tight focus-fire builds a croaking crescendo. It is foreshadowed in the vertical slice by the Old Thunderthroat creep boss.
- The Tuned - crystal-and-stone automatons awakened by giant Chordstone tuning forks. They hover, spin and float, which suits procedural animation. Mechanic 'Frequency': the player retunes the whole army between Low, Mid and High pitch, trading armor, speed and range, and matching an enemy unit's frequency shatters its armor through resonance.

### Creeps

- Thrumstone Golems - humanoid stone golems grown out of Chordstone, with tuning-fork horns sprouting from their shoulders and crystal glowing through cracks in slate-blue rock. They hum menacingly beside Singing Veins and expansion mines. Thrumlings (small) and the Thrumstone Colossus (large) are the SAME model scaled 0.7x / 1.5x. Ability HARMONIC SLAM: a ground pound that stuns for 1s. Drops: Tuning Fork of Swiftness (+attack speed) and Chordstone Pendant (+mana).
- Hollerbacks - big-chested, upright ape brutes in rust and orange fur with gold-ringed knuckles. They pound their chests like war drums and live in troupe camps in the Tonewood forest. They are designed upright to fit the humanoid rig, with no knuckle-walking. Ability WAR DRUM: a chest-beat that raises nearby creeps' attack speed. The Hollerback Chieftain is the same model scaled 1.4x with a stolen gong strapped to its back (kitbash). Drops: Earmuffs of Valor (+armor, immune to Silence, which is a big deal in this matchup) and Drumskin Boots (+move speed).
- Off-Key Brigands - patchwork bandit minstrels in mustard yellow and faded red with dented lutes, broken bagpipes and stolen handbells. They camp by the roadside and are human proportions on the shared rig. Ability SOUR NOTE: a dissonant chord that curses a target, reducing mana regen and making its attacks miss. Drops: Kazoo of Rallying (consumable: +move speed burst to nearby allies) and Rosin of Healing (consumable heal).
- STRETCH / optional boss - Old Thunderthroat: a giant bullfrog-man bandleader with an inflating throat sac who guards the central high-yield Chordstone vein and foreshadows the Bellowmire Chorus faction. If the model budget is tight, the central guard becomes a Thrumstone Colossus plus Hollerback Chieftain instead. NEUTRAL SHOP: the Tinker's Caravan, a traveling instrument peddler's wagon at the river crossing that sells potions, Earmuffs and scrolls.

### Map / tileset
MAP: Chimewood Hollow, a misty 2-player river valley, mirrored on the diagonal, on the border between the Carillon's highland moors (NW) and the Hush's twilight forest (SE). Each player has a main base with one Singing Vein and one natural expansion vein guarded by Thrumstone Golems. Hollerback and Brigand camps sit along the lanes. A central high-yield vein is guarded by a boss camp, the Tinker's Caravan shop sits at the river ford, and two Choir Stone circles give vision when a unit stands inside and softly play the shared leitmotif. TERRAIN TEXTURES (cheap painted tiles): sun-gold meadow grass, ochre dirt road, mossy cobblestone with brass drainage grates (Carillon side), violet moonmoss and pale lichen (Hush side), cracked riverbed, shallow 'singing' river with reed beds, and slate cliffs with painted top-light. DOODADS: Echo Trees (Tonewood, with hollow flute-hole trunks and amber/red/gold autumn canopies; variants come from scale, hue and rotation), Singing Veins (tuning-fork crystal clusters glowing teal-gold), Choir Stones (ancient organ-pipe standing stones), half-buried cracked bell husks from older wars, wind chimes on fences, paper lanterns hung in trees, glowing mushrooms, and a broken Carillon survey cart. DAY: golden late-afternoon light, long soft shadows, warm haze and drifting pollen, with Peal radius +25%. NIGHT: deep teal-indigo, bioluminescent crystals, fireflies and moth particles, lantern glow, with Hush radius +25%. Ambient sound beds: whistling trees by day, crickets and soft crystal hum by night. Godot notes: one terrain splat shader with 6-8 layers, Peal and Hush drawn as an influence-map texture blended in the terrain shader (gold ripples vs violet mist), and the day/night cycle via a DirectionalLight3D color/angle tween plus emissive boost.

### Art direction
TARGET LOOK: early-2000s heroic stylized fantasy RTS (never write 'Warcraft' or 'Blizzard' in prompts). Chunky, exaggerated, saturated and hand-painted, readable from a high 3/4 camera at about 60-90px unit height. PROPORTIONS: heads about 1/4 of body height, oversized hands, feet, shoulders and weapons, short sturdy legs, thick limbs. Nothing thinner than a finger at game scale. Heroes are 1.35x the scale of line units and carry one extra silhouette feature (a crest, cape or giant weapon). SHAPE LANGUAGE: Carillon is circles, domes, flared bells and horn mouths (round and open means loud). Hush is crescents, teardrops, downward triangles and V-shaped feathery plumes (soft and closed means quiet). Creeps get neutral blocky shapes (slab golems, barrel-chested apes, patchwork bandits). PALETTES: Carillon bronze #C27C3A, bronze shadow #6B3E1E, verdigris #4FA58C, forge #FF8A2B, cream #F0DFB4, soot #2E2A28. Hush dusk violet #3B2D5E, plum #6A4A82, fawn #D9CBB0, ink #17131F, moonglow #7FE8DA, lilac #C9B8F0. Neutral and environment: meadow gold #C9B44A, moss #5E8C3A, slate #5A6474, river #3FA7B5. Faction glows never share a hue: the Carillon are warm orange and gold, the Hush cool teal and lilac. TEAM COLOR: a flat, untextured area on top-facing surfaces (Carillon back pennant, tabard and helmet band; Hush wing eye-spots and antenna tips), masked in the texture so the shader tints it. LIGHTING AND MATERIALS: painted diffuse textures with baked gradients (lighter on top, darker at the feet), painted highlights on metal instead of PBR reflections, low roughness variation, one warm key light, a cool fill and a strong rim light. Emissive only on lanterns, forges and crystals. CONCEPT PROMPT TEMPLATE: 'stylized hand-painted 3D game character, [description], chunky exaggerated proportions, oversized hands and weapon, thick bold silhouette, saturated colors, painted textures with baked soft lighting, heroic fantasy RTS unit, full body, T-pose, front view, orthographic, plain light-grey background, no cast shadow'. Make a matching 'turnaround sheet (front, side, back)' version for image-to-3D. Buildings: 'stylized hand-painted fantasy RTS building, chunky proportions, slightly tilted and squashed, [description], isometric 3/4 view, isolated, plain background'. AVOID: photorealism, PBR grime, grimdark, thin spindly parts (antennae must be thick feathery plumes), loose cloth hanging between the legs, transparent parts, floating disconnected pieces, fine micro-detail and noise, text and logos, symmetry-breaking clutter on top surfaces. Also avoid any Warcraft-associated motifs: green-skinned tusked warriors, blue-and-gold lion-heraldry knights, fel-green glow, elven crescent-moon priestesses in that specific style, and the WC3 UI frame look. UI: a brass-and-velvet split frame, round bell-shaped portrait frame for the Carillon, crescent-and-silk frame for the Hush. Icons are painted at 64px with a bold central shape and faction-color backgrounds.

### Music direction
The core idea is that the score is the clock. All in-game music is written in D at 120 BPM (the Carillon) or 60 BPM half-time (the Hush), and the gameplay Toll fires every 4 bars, exactly on the downbeat, with a bell hit stitched into the music. Sync uses the AudioStreamPlayer playback position and AudioServer latency compensation, and only audio, not gameplay, depends on it. LEITMOTIF: 'The First Chord', a 5-note rising phrase (D-A-E-F#-A). The Carillon play it as a brass fanfare, the Hush as a whispered hummed choir, and the main menu plays both in counterpoint, which tells the story of the two sides of one song. ADAPTIVE LAYERS: each faction track has Build (light), Tension (enemy spotted) and Battle (full percussion) stems that crossfade on bar lines, and Battle adds drum layers as the number of nearby units grows. Dissonance upkeep subtly detunes the music and adds a wobbly kazoo layer. The Long Hush ultimate ducks everything to near-silence with a faint tinnitus sine, then cues a massive orchestral hit when the Thunderous Echo erupts. The Grand Finale ultimate brings in a full-orchestra swell. COMBAT AS MUSIC: hit, clang and spell SFX are pitch-snapped to the current chord of the playing stem and nudged up to 60ms onto a 16th-note grid, so big battles sound like a percussion section. This is cheap to do with pitch_scale and a small scheduling buffer. INSTRUMENTATION: Carillon tracks use a brass band (cornets, euphonium, tubas), carillon and tubular bells, anvils, field snare, timpani, pipe organ and a male work-song choir, in an upbeat, heroic, hummable march style. Hush tracks use glass harmonica, celesta, music box, waterphone, bowed vibraphone, a breathy female and children's choir humming, seed-pod shakers, soft frame drum and cello drones, in an eerie, tender, lullaby-like style. Neutral and creep areas get folk fiddle and hurdy-gurdy with off-key bandit tunes. STINGERS: victory is the full brass plus choir playing the leitmotif; defeat is one cracked bell and a moth's whisper; hero level-up is a handbell or glass ting rising a fifth. SAMPLE AI-MUSIC PROMPTS: 'heroic fantasy brass band march, carillon bells, anvils, snare, timpani, pipe organ, 120 BPM, D mixolydian, uplifting, game soundtrack loop, instrumental'. 'ethereal night lullaby, glass harmonica, celesta, breathy choir humming, bowed vibraphone, soft frame drum, 60 BPM, D dorian, mysterious and gentle, game soundtrack loop, instrumental'. Generate stems, or generate separately per layer at a fixed BPM and key, and trim to exact 4-bar multiples for seamless loops.

## Vibecraft: Maize & Bone

*Pitch angle: folklore*

**Logline.** Heroes kneaded from corn dough face a vain underworld court of disease-lords in a cosmic ballgame, fought across a jungle crossroads. It plays like Warcraft III, with heroes, creeps and upkeep, and the world comes from the K'iche' Maya Popol Vuh.

### World
The Makers tried three times to create people, and each attempt failed. The first creatures could only squawk. The Mud Folk melted in the rain. The Woodenfolk had no hearts, and in the end their own cooking pots, griddles and grinding stones rose up and beat them. On the fourth try the Hearth Grandmother ground yellow and white maize and kneaded the Maizeborn. They are warm, sun-loving people of dough who sprout again when they fall. They farm terraced fields (milpas) in a lush limestone jungle full of cenotes, giant ceiba trees and four colored roads. They have always liked a good ballgame. Nine floors beneath their fields lies the Downcourt, an underworld run like a petty, gloating tax office by a council of disease-lords. They settle everything with a ballgame, cheat whenever they can, throw lavish parties for the dead and keep careful records of everyone who owes them.

The Downcourt has sent its owls with writs again. The living are summoned to trial, the Black Road has cracked open, and the Lords want the Maizeborn's sun to use as their new ball. The tone is heroic and sunny, mixed with grotesque comedy. The Downcourt are funny and vain but truly dangerous: they don't kill you quickly, they make you sick and then collect. The Maizeborn are farmers who became champions, and their heroes win by trickery rather than armor. The whole map is the ballcourt, and the war is as much a contest of wits as of armies.

### Why it isn't generic
There are no humans-vs-orcs, castles or steel. Everything is stone-age jungle material: obsidian, quilted cotton, jade, rubber and bark paper. The light side are people made of corn, and the dark side is a party-loving underworld bureaucracy, not plague undead. Games have barely used the Popol Vuh. Smite borrows individual Maya gods, and as far as a quick search showed, no RTS has used the world of the Hero Twins. The myth already gives us a conflict built around a ballgame, comic villains named for diseases, and failed-creation creeps (mud people, and wooden people beaten up by their own kitchenware). Every mechanic comes from the myth: Replanting is maize rebirth, Ailments come from the disease-lords, the Magistrate's ultimate is the Trial of the Six Houses, and the map is built on the Four Roads. The two factions contrast clearly at RTS scale: the Maizeborn are bright, round shapes with fans; the Downcourt are dark sticks and spikes around a round belly. We deliberately avoid the nearest look-alikes: Warcraft's jungle trolls and Zandalari (no tusks, tikis, voodoo or raptors), WC3 Undead (no frost, plague cauldrons or stitched brutes), Day-of-the-Dead sugar skulls, Aztec Sun Stone kitsch, and Gord/Witcher grimdark. Every name is original: quick searches found no game using Maizeborn, Downcourt, Cobguard, Bone Bailiff, Skull Roller or 'Maize & Bone'.

### Resources
JADE (the gold equivalent) is mined from Jade Seams, green-glowing limestone cliffs at the edge of cenotes; workers carry out chunky jade blocks. TIMBER (the lumber equivalent) comes from felling broadleaf jungle trees, and cutting them opens new paths. Giant ceibas are sacred and impassable. SUPPLY: the Maizeborn grow 'Maize' on Milpa Terraces (+8 each), and the Downcourt seat their army at the Lords' Banquet Pit (+8 'Seats' each). Halls give +10, the cap is 80 supply, and units cost 1 to 4 supply, so armies stay at roughly 20 to 40 units. UPKEEP works like WC3 with two flavors. Maizeborn 'Lean Season': more mouths and fewer cobs at market. Downcourt 'Court Fees': the Lords skim a cut, and a bigger army means more clerks. Supply 0-40 has no upkeep, 41-60 cuts jade income by 30%, and 61-80 cuts it by 60%. Faction twists: the Downcourt also earns jade from Death Tax kills and Confiscated Kernels, and the Maizeborn save resources by Replanting. Items are bought with jade at the neutral Cacao Trader; cacao beans are the trader's flavor currency.

### Faction: The Maizeborn

**Fantasy.** Sun-warmed people kneaded from yellow and white maize dough by the Hearth Grandmother: farmers, ballplayers and tricksters who outwit death instead of out-armoring it. They win through sustain, tempo and trickery. Their dead sprout back, and their heroes swap places, bounce and outfox the enemy. Playing them feels like being the hero of a folk tale: cheerful, scrappy and fearless.

**Unique mechanic.** REPLANTING. When a non-hero Maizeborn unit dies, it drops a Golden Kernel, a glowing sprout that is easy to see from the top-down camera and lasts 25 s. A Husker can pick it up and carry it to the Great Granary like a resource. There the unit is 'Re-kneaded' for 40% of its cost and 50% of its train time (it still needs supply). The Hearth Grandmother's skills re-knead kernels on the spot. After every fight this creates a short race: get the kernels home before Downcourt Owl Couriers Confiscate them for jade. The faction is sustain-heavy, and the Downcourt has natural counters in Pus (reduced healing), the Death Tax and Confiscate. Secondary flavor: Milpa Terraces visibly grow from green sprouts to golden ears over the match (vertex-shader growth), which signals the game clock.

**Visual identity.** SILHOUETTE: round, bottom-heavy and blooming. Barrel torsos in puffy quilted cotton, big round shields, and tall fan-shaped crests of husk and feathers that open upward, so they read from the 3/4 top-down camera as bright fans. Weapons are oversized and simple: clubs set with obsidian teeth, atlatls, a rubber ball the size of a head. MATERIALS: dough-tan skin with a subtle kernel speckle, woven husk, quilted cotton, polished jade, painted stucco, chunky carved wood, terracotta clay. PALETTE: Maize Gold #F2B632, Husk Cream #F1E2B0, Maya Blue #4DA6D8, Jade Teal #2BB59E, Terracotta #CF6A3A, Cacao Brown #5B3A24 (outlines and wood). TEAM COLOR: magenta-keyed zones on surfaces that face the camera: shield faces, crest-fan tips, shoulder sashes, Grandmother's shawl, roof banners and the Granary's roof cloth.

**Audio identity.** VOICES: warm, earthy, upbeat farmers turned champions, mixed ages, fond of corn and dough puns. Sample original lines. SFX: wooden club thocks, clay shattering, rubber-ball boings, buzzing hornet swarms, husk rustles, sprouting chimes for kernels. MUSIC INSTRUMENTATION: wooden slit-drum and turtle-shell percussion, gourd rattles, clay ocarinas and ceramic flutes, conch-horn calls, and bright heroic brass and choir over a full orchestra.

**Worker.** Husker: a stocky, dough-gold farmer in a husk hood, with a tumpline basket on his back and a stone-headed digging axe. **Buildings**

- Great Granary - town hall: trains Huskers, receives resources, re-kneads Kernels, and has tier upgrades (Granary > Sun Granary > Golden Granary) - a round thatched granary on a stepped stucco platform, ringed with giant clay storage jars and topped by an oversized golden corn-ear finial that slowly turns.
- Milpa Terrace - supply (+8 Maize) - a small terraced field of maize, bean vines and squash with a scarecrow on a pole; stalks sway in the wind and turn from green to gold over the match.
- Drum Lodge - barracks: trains Cobguard and Dart Hurler and researches infantry upgrades - a long thatched lodge with painted posts and a huge carved slit-drum out front that pulses with a 'thump' whenever a unit is trained.

- Sun Stair - hero altar: summons and revives heroes - a small, steep stepped pyramid crowned with a big rotating sun disc, with copal incense smoke drifting from the top.

### Faction: The Downcourt

**Fantasy.** The Lords of the Underworld, nine floors down: a vain, petty, party-loving bureaucracy of disease-lords who run death like a tax office, settle everything by ballgame and cheat at every chance. They are grotesque comedy villains who are genuinely dangerous: instead of killing you fast, they make you sick and then collect. Playing them feels like being a gleeful, scheming villain who also has paperwork.

**Unique mechanic.** AILMENTS & THE DEATH TAX. Each Downcourt damage-dealer inflicts one Ailment, shown as a shaped pip above the victim. WASTING (Bone Bailiff; bone-white crack): -2 armor. PUS (Pus Pitcher; green drip): -50% healing received. FEVER (Snatcher and the Fever Hostess; ember swirl): damage over time. An enemy carrying all three becomes RIPE: a copper bell rings, a skull pip appears, and the target takes 20% more damage. If it dies while Ripe, the Downcourt collects a DEATH TAX of jade equal to 20% of the victim's cost (for heroes, 50 + 10 per level), and the body leaves no Golden Kernel. The mechanic rewards mixed armies and focus fire over raw numbers, and directly counters Maizeborn sustain. Owl Couriers can also Confiscate dropped Kernels and items for jade.

**Visual identity.** SILHOUETTE: 'sticks, spikes and a ball'. Spindly limbs, big round pot-bellies, tall vertical bark-paper banners and spiky obsidian crests, with things that dangle and sag: eyeball collars, copper bells, bone tubes. They read as dark vertical strokes against the jungle, the opposite of the Maizeborn's bright round fans. Limbs are wrapped in thick black cloth bands so they survive image-to-3D. The look draws on Maya death-god imagery (eyeball collars, %-like cheek marks, round black bellies, bells). It is NOT Day-of-the-Dead sugar skulls and NOT generic plague undead. MATERIALS: dull ivory bone, glossy black obsidian, smoky ragged cloth, bark paper, oxidized copper bells, sickly glows. PALETTE: Obsidian #1D1A23, Bone #E8E0CA, Dusk Indigo #3D3470, Ember Orange #F2792B, Pus Chartreuse #A6CF3B, Copper #B5713A. TEAM COLOR: magenta-keyed zones on the tall back banners (they read as flags from above), on belly stripes, and on banner poles and awnings of buildings.

**Audio identity.** VOICES: pompous, nasal court officials, wheezing giggles, sing-song sarcasm and owl hoots, all with a slight down-a-well reverb. Sample original lines. ### Art direction
SHAPE LANGUAGE: chunky, exaggerated, toy-like heroic proportions. Units are about 4 heads tall; hands, feet and weapons are 1.5x oversized; warriors' shoulders are 2x hip width. Use big, simple, bevelled primary forms with no detail smaller than about 1/20 of the silhouette. Faction contrast is CIRCLES & FANS (Maizeborn) versus STICKS, SPIKES & A BALL (Downcourt). Put the important read on top-facing surfaces (crests, shoulders, banners, roofs), because the camera looks down. SURFACE: hand-painted diffuse with a baked soft top-light gradient (lighter on top), painted ambient occlusion in creases, thick color blocks and no photo textures or PBR noise. LIGHTING in concepts: warm key from the upper left, cool sky fill and a soft rim. MATERIALS: Maizeborn use quilted cotton, woven husk, polished jade, painted stucco, chunky carved wood, terracotta and feathers rendered as big solid shapes. Downcourt use dull bone, glossy obsidian, smoky ragged cloth, bark paper, copper bells and sickly glows. PATTERNS: invented geometric motifs only (step-frets, kernels, spirals, dots); never real Maya glyphs or readable text. TEAM COLOR: paint team zones as flat, unshaded pure magenta #FF00FF; no other material may use pink or magenta. CHARACTER PROMPT TEMPLATE: 'stylized hand-painted 3D game character, early-2000s fantasy RTS style, chunky heroic exaggerated proportions, [description], full body, A-pose, front view, limbs clearly separated from body, plain light grey background, even lighting, no cast shadows, clean readable silhouette'. BUILDING TEMPLATE: 'stylized hand-painted fantasy RTS building, 3/4 top-down view, single structure on a small square ground base, oversized roof/crown, chunky exaggerated proportions, plain background'. ICON TEMPLATE: 'painted bust portrait, dramatic rim light, saturated colors, centered, bold shapes'. STANDING NEGATIVE PROMPT: no Warcraft/Blizzard references, no tusks, no trolls or orcs, no blue skin, no tiki masks, no voodoo, no dinosaurs or raptors, no Aztec Sun Stone, no sugar skulls or Day of the Dead, no conquistadors or steel armor, no gore or sacrifice imagery, no photorealism, no text, no thin dangling strands, no transparent parts. Lock one approved style-bible sheet per faction and use it as an image reference for every later prompt.

### Music direction
The core sound is a WC3-scale heroic orchestra (big brass, choir, war drums) on a rhythmic bed inspired by Mesoamerican instruments: wooden slit-drum, turtle shell, gourd rattles, rain-sticks. Melodies are carried by clay flutes, ocarinas and conch calls. It is fantasy-inspired and makes no claim to authentic reconstruction. MAIN THEME 'Under the Maize Sun': a rising anthem with a conch-call motif. MAIZEBORN cues: major or mixolydian, 90-110 BPM, 'sunrise over terraces', brass fanfares with ocarina countermelodies and bright choir. DOWNCOURT cues: minor or phrygian, 70-90 BPM, a sly comic-villain court procession (bassoons, tuba, plucked basses, detuned copper bells, clay death-whistle shrieks, a mocking choir) with grotesque waltz and march energy. AMBIENT: by day, layered jungle birds and cicadas with sparse flute; by night, frogs, insects, owl calls and low drones. COMBAT: layered intensity stems (percussion only, then percussion plus brass, then full) crossfaded by battle size. STINGERS: hero level-up (conch plus choir hit), Ripe bell, Replant sprout chime, Old Ballcourt 'ring scored' crowd roar, and victory and defeat themes per faction. For AI generation, keep one key and tempo family per faction so stems loop cleanly. Avoid marimba-led tropical or Caribbean clichés, 'Aztec techno', and anything that imitates WC3's specific themes.

### Producibility notes
MODEL BUDGET = 30. That covers 14 faction characters (Husker, Cobguard, Dart Hurler, Ballcourt Bruiser, Hive Lobber, Sunshot, Granny Metate; Owl Courier, Bone Bailiff, Pus Pitcher, Snatcher, Skull Roller, Nine-Bones, Six-Fever), 10 buildings (5 per faction), 3 creeps and 3 props: the Jade Seam, one jungle tree used at several scales and tints (the ceiba is a scaled, retextured variant) and a limestone rock cluster. FREE REUSE: the Doughling is the Cobguard mesh with a raw-dough shader and no shield. The Old Ballcourt is the Ballcourt mesh with a ruin and moss material. Crockery creeps, hive gourds, the rubber ball and pus pots are textured primitives. Building tiers are shown by attaching procedural props (banners, glow). The Cacao Trader is an optional 31st model or a kit-bash of the Drum Lodge with awnings. RIGGING: 16 of the 17 animated characters (all units, heroes and creeps except the Skull Roller) are humanoid bipeds and share one retargeted animation library (idle, walk, attack, cast, work, death, victory), plus a 'hover-flap' set for the Snatcher and the Macaw Pretender, whose arm-wings flap as arms while hover is a root offset plus bob. The Skull Roller is the only non-biped and is fully procedural (roll, squash-and-stretch, jaw clack). Woodenfolk use deliberately stiff animation. Props are separate meshes attached to bones (blowgun, squash, grinding slab, ball, gavel, fans, back banners) so image-to-3D does not fuse them into the body. BUILDINGS are static meshes with procedural or shader motion: spinning Granary finial, swaying and growing milpa through vertex wind, drum pulse, chewing Maw jaw, rattling blades, billboard bats and smoke particles, glowing owl eyes. TEAM COLOR comes from magenta hue-keying in a Godot shader. Ailment pips, Golden Kernels and selection rings are UI/VFX overlays, not textures, so they stay readable at small scale. VOICE: 6-8 TTS lines per unit (select, move, attack, annoyed), each under 2 s, using two or three base voices per faction with pitch variants; Downcourt voices get a consistent down-a-well reverb. RISKS AND MITIGATIONS: (1) Thin Downcourt limbs and tall Maizeborn crests may break in image-to-3D. Wrap limbs in thick cloth bands, keep crests as thick solid planes, and prompt 'limbs separated'. (2) Wing membranes can confuse auto-rigging. Attach them only along the arm, like sleeve-capes, never to the torso. (3) Style drift across 30 assets. Approve a style-bible sheet first and condition every prompt on it. (4) IP drift toward WoW jungle trolls, WC3 Undead, Day of the Dead or Aztec kitsch. Keep the standing negative prompts and review concepts against them. (5) Cultural sensitivity: the Popol Vuh is a sacred narrative of the living K'iche' Maya people. Present the game as 'inspired by', invent every name, use no real deity names or real glyphs and no sacrifice gore, credit the source in the game, and budget a sensitivity read. Both factions come from the same myth, so no real people are cast as villains. (6) Mechanic complexity for the AI opponent. Give AI Huskers a simple 'fetch kernel' job, and have the Downcourt AI prioritize Ripe targets and Confiscate. Ship the Ailment numbers behind a tuning table.

## Vibecraft: Lantern & Stone

*Pitch angle: fidelity*

**Logline.** The sun has cracked and its shards lie in the hills as glowing Sunstone. Lamp-lit frontier towns bottle that daylight to hold back the long blue nights, while the huge moss-trolls of the old folk tales, who turn to stone at dawn and wake hungry at dusk, set out to smash every lantern in the land.

### World
Long ago something took a bite out of the sun. Scholars blame a comet. The Mossbacks swear it was Gloomgut. The shards rained down as Sunstone, warm amber crystal that still holds daylight. Since then the days are short and golden and the nights are long, blue and cold, a season people call the Gloaming. The Lanternmarch is a frontier kingdom of brass, glass and cobbled roads. It mines Sunstone to bottle daylight, and it pushes its territory out into the dark moors one lantern post at a time, with a curfew bell, a ledger and a lot of earnest marching. The Mossbacks are the hill-trolls of old folklore: hulking, mossy, sentimental and always hungry. They petrify at sunrise and wake ravenous at dusk. To them every new lantern is a place where a Mossback can never wake up again. Worse, the Marchers have started planting lanterns on the rolling hills, and those hills are the Mossbacks' sleeping elders.

The tone is heroic storybook with slapstick: big battles, booming hero entrances and real stakes, punctured by charming, dumb and pompous unit banter. Both sides think they are the reasonable ones. On the edges of the map, older troubles stir. Orchard spirits resent both the lights and the trampling, and from a drowned cathedral a creeping wax plague has started stealing flames. Those threats set up the later factions.

### Why it isn't generic
It keeps the familiar Warcraft III matchup of stalwart civilization against savage horde, but neither side is a stock humans-vs-orcs pair.
(1) The conflict is built into the mechanics: light against night. Marcher territory is literal light (Lanternlight), and Mossback stats physically change with day and night (Stoneform/Nightwild). Every fight asks the same question: is it lit here?
(2) The sources are not Tolkien or Blizzard. The Marchers draw on Victorian lamplighters, lighthouse keepers and glassblowers: industrial bottled sunlight, not holy light. There are no paladins, priests, horses or griffons. The Mossbacks draw on Scandinavian folktale trolls (the John Bauer and Brian Froud style of huge noses, moss manes and stone-at-dawn): grey-green boulder bodies, no green skin, tusks, mohawks, wolves or spiked black armor.
(3) Readability doubles as identity. Marchers are tall verticals carrying warm amber points of light. Mossbacks are wide lumpy boulders with paired moon-cyan eyes at night. From the top-down camera at night you can tell the armies apart by glow color alone.
(4) The world is part of the joke and the drama. Mossback town halls are sleeping elders that open their eyes at night, the horde's grunts literally get stiff in the morning, and a hero claims he bit the sun.
(5) The creeps (floating sun-splinters, toad poachers, moth-filled empty armor) and the economy names (Sunstone, Hearths, Snores) all come from the same broken-sun premise.

### Resources
GOLD analog: SUNSTONE. Glowing amber shards of the broken sun, mined from Sunstone Seams (cracked rock outcrops veined with light) that hold a fixed amount and gradually dim as they are mined out. Marchers say Sunstone fuels their lanterns. Mossbacks just like shiny rocks and hoard it in their elders' bellies.

LUMBER analog: TIMBER. Harvested from gloamwood pines and silver birches. Wickhands chop them; Grubbers bear-hug them out by the roots. Treelines double as walls and chokepoints, as in WC3.

FOOD/SUPPLY: Marchers count HEARTHS (Cottage Row +8, Beacon Hall +10). Mossbacks count SNORES (Snore Pit +8, Sleeping Elder +10; every warrior needs a warm pile to sleep in). Cap is 60. Costs: worker 1, infantry and casters 2, siege 4, heroes 5, which gives armies of about 20 to 35 units.

UPKEEP, named STRAIN:
- 0-24 supply: no strain.
- 25-40 supply: 'Stretched', -30% Sunstone per trip.
- 41-60 supply: 'Starving', -60% Sunstone per trip.
Marchers call strain 'the Garrison Tithe', and a taxman voice line announces each tier. Mossbacks call it 'the Grumbles': workers visibly stop to snack. This keeps the WC3 hero-and-small-army economy.

ITEMS come from creep drops and a neutral shop: Bottled Noon (temporary light pool), Moth-in-a-Jar (moths fly to the nearest enemy light and reveal it), Toad Oil (heal), Lucky Skipping Stone (+attack speed), Moth-King's Circlet (boss drop, night vision and detection).

### Faction: The Lanternmarch

**Fantasy.** A disciplined frontier kingdom of lamplighters, glassblowers and night-watch militia. They extend civilization by literally lighting it, planting chains of brass lanterns across the moors. They are earnest, a bit pompous, obsessed with curfews and paperwork, and quietly brave: the people who walk into the dark holding up a lamp. This fills the classic WC3 human/alliance slot (sturdy infantry, riflemen, casters, siege, a heroic marshal and a wizard), but the magic is alchemical bottled sunlight, not divine light.

**Unique mechanic.** LANTERNLIGHT (territory as light). Beacon Hall projects a large amber radius of light (about 14 m) and every other Marcher building projects a small one (about 6 m). Workers build cheap, fragile Lanternposts (20 Timber, 120 HP). A post must be placed inside existing light, so the Marchers grow their territory as visible chains of glowing pools on the terrain.
Inside Lanternlight:
- Marcher units get +2 armor and double HP regeneration.
- Enemy invisible units are revealed.
- Mossbacks are forced into Stoneform even at night (slower, but tougher).
- Watch Lamp towers can only be built inside light.
A snuffed light source goes dark for 15 s.
Result: the Marchers fight best by pushing their light outward, and the Mossbacks fight best by smashing it. Forcing Stoneform is double-edged, because stone Mossbacks are slow but very hard to kill.

**Visual identity.** Silhouette language: tall verticals, rectangles and circles. Every unit has a crest, pole or plume rising above its head, round mirror-shields, lantern cages and dish mirrors. Every unit carries one point of warm amber light, so at night an army reads as a moving constellation of warm dots, each with a soft pool of light on the ground.
Materials: polished brass with painted highlights, cobalt-lacquered steel, starched linen, leaded glass painted opaque with emissive masks, oak, cream limestone, blue slate roofs.
Palette: Midnight Cobalt #1E3A64, Brass #C8962E, Lantern Amber glow #FFC861, Linen Cream #F1E6CC, Oak #6B4423, Verdigris accent #4F9E8C.
Team color: large flat zones on tabards, helm plumes, shield faces, roof pennants and door banners. Lantern glow is always faction amber and never team-colored.

**Audio identity.** Voice personality: crisp, earnest, slightly pompous British-Isles watchmen and craftsfolk. They make lamp and curfew puns, complain about overtime and treat every order as a formal procedure. Heroes are bigger: a gruff, matronly drill-marshal and a doddering, delighted professor.

- Glassgunner: 'Hold still, I'm focusing.' / 'Lovely day for a sunburn.'
- Lumenwright: 'Let there be... adequate lighting.' / 'Bottled at noon, opened with care.'

SFX: lantern clinks, glass chimes, brass ratchets, a curfew handbell when a hero levels up.

**Worker.** Wickhand: a chubby lamplighter in a cobalt coat, flat cap and big leather gloves, carrying a lantern-pole over one shoulder and a brass tool satchel. ### Faction: The Mossback Horde

**Fantasy.** The hulking hill-trolls of old folk tales: mossy, lumpy, huge-nosed, sentimental and always hungry. They sleep as stone through the day and pour out of the hills at dusk to raid. Clan matriarchs lead them and moon-mad shamans advise them. They love naps, shiny pebbles, skipping stones and the dark, and they hate mornings. They want the world kept dark so their elders can keep sleeping in peace. This fills the classic WC3 savage-horde slot (tough grunts, rock throwers, shamans, a battering siege unit, a brawler chieftain and a mystic), but the horde comes from Scandinavian folklore rather than greenskins.

**Unique mechanic.** STONEFORM / NIGHTWILD (the day/night cycle rewrites their stats).
- Day, or anywhere inside enemy Lanternlight: Stoneform. Skin greys to granite through a shader blend. +4 armor, immune to knockback, -20% move speed, -15% attack speed. Strong at defending, weak at raiding.
- Night, outside light: Nightwild. Eyes open and glow moon-cyan, moss lichen runes light up. +20% move speed, +15% attack speed, +2 vision radius.
- Dusk and dawn are 3 s petrify/wake transitions with a voice line.
The Mossbacks' counter-tools are snuffing lights (Clobberer, Gloomgut) and making their own darkness (Mosswife's Moonshade, Gloomstone fields, the Eclipse ultimate). The strategic rhythm: turtle by day, raid by night.

**Visual identity.** Silhouette language: wide, low, lumpy pebble and boulder masses with hunched backs, long dangling arms, enormous noses, ears and hands, and shaggy moss manes. Asymmetric and horizontal, the opposite of the Marchers' verticals. At night every Mossback shows a pair of moon-cyan eye dots, so they read as boulders that are watching you.
Materials: slate-grey skin with granite patches, thick moss and lichen, birch bark, rope, bone, antler, river stones, and smashed Marcher brass worn as trophies.
Palette: Slate #5E6A73, Moss #6E8C3A, Lichen #C9D45A, Peat #4B3526, Bone #E8DDC4, Moon-glow cyan #7FE3FF (eyes and runes at night).
Team color: big painted handprints and stripe war paint across shoulders and backs, hide banners on totem poles and buildings, rag streamers tied to weapons.

**Audio identity.** Voice personality: deep, gravelly, slow, lovably dim and childlike. They are food- and nap-obsessed, sincere about violence, and grumpy in the morning. Made with TTS pitched down 3 to 5 semitones and layered with a soft stone-grinding texture.
Sample lines (all original):
- Grubber: 'Dig dig. Crunchy.' / 'Rock for you, rock for me.' / 'Is it nap time? ...Hrrm. Not nap time.'
- Clobberer: 'SMASH the shiny!' / 'Me big. You small. Good plan.' / annoyed: 'Poke again, you become gravel.'
- Any unit at dawn: 'Nnngh... getting... crunchy...'
- Any unit at dusk: 'MOON'S UP! TEETH OUT!'
- Stoneskipper: 'Seven skips! New record!'
- Mosswife: 'Hush now, the dark is coming.'
- Boulderbelly: 'Rollin'! ...Which way is down?'
- Ragga: 'Ragga doesn't throw tantrums. Ragga throws PEOPLE.'
- Gloomgut: 'I bit the sun once. Tasted like lemons.'
SFX: rock clacks, deep snores, gulps, stone cracking during petrification.
Music instrumentation: huge frame drums and log drums, clacking-stone percussion, a low male choir humming drones, hurdy-gurdy, a folk fiddle with sympathetic strings, bone rattles, long wooden lur-style horn calls and bowed double bass. Night variants grow louder and faster, the reverse of the Marchers.

**Worker.** Grubber: a small young Mossback (still more moss than stone) with a wicker basket on its back and a huge wooden spoon-shovel. Mines Sunstone, uproots Timber with big bear-hugs and builds by piling stones. Ability: Rock Nap, which curls it into an invulnerable boulder for up to 8 s (it cannot act); this is the horde's answer to worker raids.

**Units**

- Clobberer - frontline melee bruiser - hulking, hunched slate-skinned troll with a mossy mane, giant nose and ears, a tree-trunk club with a boulder lashed to the end, and a smashed Marcher lantern worn as a helmet. From above: a wide boulder with team-color handprints across the back - Snuff: attacks against light sources (Lanternposts, Watch Lamps, Bottled Dawn pools, building lights) deal +50% damage and extinguish that Lanternlight for 15 s.
- Stoneskipper - ranged - lanky, long-armed younger Mossback with a satchel of flat river stones, throwing side-arm like a kid at a pond - Skipping Stone: hurls a flat stone that skips along the ground, hitting up to 4 enemies in a line, with 20% less damage on each bounce.
- Mosswife - caster/support - tiny, ancient, hunched crone under a thick moss shawl modelled as a solid mass, with an owl-skull staff hung with bone chimes; her lichen runes glow moon-cyan at night - Moonshade: calls down a 12 s pool of true night at a target area. Inside it, Mossbacks go Nightwild, enemy Lanternlight is suppressed and enemy vision shrinks. It is the direct counter to Bottled Dawn. Has an autocast Moss Poultice heal as a minor secondary.
- Boulderbelly - siege/battering - an enormous elder Mossback permanently curled into a mossy boulder-ball, with only a face, stubby arms and feet poking out. It moves by rolling (procedural spin and squash, no rig) - Bowl Over: rolls in a straight line at high speed, knocking units aside and dealing heavy damage to the first building it hits, then is dazed for 2 s.

**Heroes**

- Ragga Stonebelly, the Hill-Mother - strength melee grappler/bruiser - colossal matronly chieftain with a braided moss mane, bone-bead necklaces and a rune-carved standing stone (menhir) she swings as a club - (1) Fling: grabs a small or medium enemy and hurls it at a target point, damaging and stunning (1 s) everything where it lands. (2) Menhir Wall: slams her stone into the ground, raising an impassable stone wall segment for 6 s and knocking back adjacent enemies. (3) Thick as Rock (passive): flat damage reduction on every hit, doubled while in Stoneform. (4) ULTIMATE The Hills Wake: the ground heaves and two giant Elder Mossbacks rise to fight for 30 s (these are scaled-up Clobberers with a full stone-and-moss shader).

(2) Gulp the Light: swallows a target light source (Lanternpost, Bottled Dawn pool, Prism or a Lumenwright's lantern), extinguishing or dispelling it and healing Gloomgut. (3) Lullaby of Stones (aura): allied Mossbacks in Stoneform regenerate HP twice as fast, which makes holding ground by day viable. (4) ULTIMATE Eclipse: the sky goes dark over a huge area for 20 s, making it full night regardless of time or lanterns; Mossbacks go Nightwild, enemy Lanternlight drops to 25% and enemy vision is halved.

**Buildings**

- Sleeping Elder - town hall: trains Grubbers, resource drop-off, upgrades to Elder Stirs and then Elder Wakes - a colossal petrified elder Mossback curled up asleep, with a bark-and-hide hut village built on its back; at night its eyes open and glow moon-cyan (emissive toggle), and snoring breath puffs from its nose.
- Snore Pit - supply (+8 Snores) - a dug-out turf pit under a hide awning, with a heap of sleeping stone-trolls sculpted into the mesh and drifting 'Zzz' particles at night.
- Brawl Ring - barracks: trains Clobberers and Stoneskippers; melee and ranged upgrades - a ring of stubby standing stones around a trampled mud floor, with log benches and a gong made from a squashed Marcher shield.
- Moss Hollow - hero altar (summon and revive heroes), Mosswife lodge and caster research - a huge hollowed tree stump overgrown with moss, glowing lichen runes, and bone chimes and owl skulls hanging from its roots.
- Boulder Chute - siege works: Boulderbellies; siege and armor upgrades - an earthen ramp with a log-railed rolling chute and a pile of practice boulders; as an idle animation, a boulder procedurally rolls down the chute.
- Gloomstone - counter-territory node and defense tower: radiates a Gloom field that suppresses enemy Lanternlight around it and lobs rocks at enemies; it can be placed anywhere - a single leaning menhir with glowing moon-runes. Reuses the standing-stone prop mesh with a rune decal, so it costs no new AI mesh.

### Later factions

- The Bramble Moot (nature spirits): orchard and hedgerow spirits (scarecrow wardens, gourd-headed lantern sprites, bee-maidens and walking hay-ricks) whose buildings grow through four seasons (sow, bloom, harvest, fallow) instead of paying upkeep. They defend the old farmland that both the light and the night keep trampling. The source is harvest and farmland folklore, not moon-forest elves.
- The Wax Choir (plague): a creeping candle-wax blight from a drowned cathedral. Victims become singing waxwork effigies with wick-heads, and the faction spreads 'Drip' (cooling wax terrain) and steals other factions' light to burn as its own. Its dead melt into pools that can be re-poured into new units: undead-like, but built as the dark side of the setting's light theme.

### Creeps

- Sunsplinters (procedural, no AI mesh) - floating clusters of fallen-sun crystal with a blinking amber core, slowly bobbing and rotating around Sunstone Seams. Two sizes: Splinter and Splintercluster. Ability Glint: a blinding flash that gives nearby enemies a miss chance. Drop mana runes and Bottled Noon consumables, which either faction can use to make a temporary light pool. Built from procedurally generated prism meshes with an emissive shader.
- Bogwallop Toadfolk (1 biped mesh, 2 variants by scale, tint and prop) - pot-bellied, wide-mouthed toad-men poachers in reed armor and fishing-net cloaks, camped in the peat bog. - Rustwardens (1 biped mesh, 2 variants including a boss) - empty suits of armor from a forgotten war, animated by nests of glimmer-moths that puff from the visor when struck. ### Map / tileset
MAP: 'Brindlemoor Crossing', a small 2-player map (about 128x128 cells, WC3-small scale) set on misty northern moorland at the edge of the Gloaming.

LAYOUT:
- Marchers start in the southwest on a cobbled hill road lined with old waystones and roadside lanterns.
- Mossbacks start in the northeast in a mossy boulder field ringed by standing stones; some of the large hills there are visibly sleeping elders with closed eyes.
- Center: the Noonscar, a cracked crater with a rich Sunstone Seam guarded by Sunsplinters and the Moth-Crowned Warden boss.
- Two natural expansions per side, guarded by Sunsplinters.
- The western peat bog holds Toadfolk camps; the eastern ruined battlefield holds Rustwarden camps.
- Birch woods and dark gloamwood pines form the treelines and chokepoints.

TERRAIN TEXTURES (hand-painted, tileable): heather (dusty mauve #9C6F92), moor grass (ochre-green #8EA04B), peat mud (#3F2E22), cobblestone road (#9A8F80), chalk footpath (#D8CFB8), lichen rock cliff (#7A8278), crystal crust around seams (warm cracks with emissive amber veins), and bog water (murky teal, a simple flow shader).

DOODADS: silver birches, gloamwood pines, lichen boulders, standing stones, peat stacks, dry-stone walls and wooden stiles, broken roadside lanterns, mushroom fairy-rings, toad-carved idols, half-buried rusty armor, an abandoned Marcher supply cart, bones, glowing moss tufts.

DAY/NIGHT:
- Cycle: 6-minute day and 4-minute night, with 20 s dusk and dawn, shown by a sun/moon dial on the HUD.
- Day: a long, low golden-hour sun (key #FFE2A8), saturated colors, long cool-blue shadows; Mossbacks are grey stone.
- Dusk: a magenta-orange sky gradient while elder eyes crack open across the Mossback base.
- Night: deep blue-violet ambient (#2B2F5E), moonlight key (#8FA8FF). Marcher amber light pools, moon-cyan Mossback eyes and runes, Sunstone veins and bog wisps carry the scene.
- Night is never pitch black; scene brightness stays at roughly 35% or more for readability.

### Art direction
OVERALL STYLE: chunky, storybook-heroic stylized 3D with hand-painted textures. Saturated but harmonized color and soft baked lighting painted into the albedo. Low-frequency forms that read at 40-80 px on screen.

PROPORTIONS:
- Heads about 1/4 of body height.
- Hands, feet and weapons oversized about 1.5x; broad shoulders; planted stances.
- Heroes are 1.4-1.6x unit scale, each with one signature glowing element (Brasswick's helm lamp, Kilnbright's molten pipe, Ragga's runed menhir, Gloomgut's moth cloak).

SHAPE LANGUAGE:
- Marchers: verticals, rectangles and circles (crests, poles, shields, lanterns, dishes).
- Mossbacks: wide pebble and boulder masses, asymmetric lumps, big noses, ears and hands, horizontal silhouettes.
- Neutrals: round and squat (toads), angular and hollow (Rustwardens), faceted and floating (Sunsplinters).

TEXTURING:
- Hand-painted albedo with soft baked ambient occlusion, a warm top-light gradient, and painted edge highlights on metal and stone.
- Big color blocks; no photographic textures, noise grime or normal-map micro-detail.
- Glows always come from separate emissive masks.
- Team-color zones are painted pure magenta #FF00FF in concepts and textures so an import script can key them into a team-color mask.

LIGHTING REFERENCE:
- Day: warm key #FFE2A8 from upper-left, cool sky fill #8FB8E8.
- Night: moonlight #8FA8FF plus amber pools #FFC861 and moon-cyan accents #7FE3FF.

PROMPT SKELETONS:
- Character: '<subject>, stylized hand-painted 3D game character, chunky exaggerated heroic proportions, oversized hands and weapon, clean readable silhouette, large simple shapes, painted textures with soft baked lighting, saturated colors, full body, A-pose, arms held away from body, front view, plain neutral grey background, no text'.
- Building: '<subject>, stylized hand-painted fantasy strategy-game building, chunky storybook architecture, oversized roof and trim, slightly crooked, three-quarter top-down view, square footprint, isolated on grey background'.
- Icon: 'painted portrait bust of <subject>, bold shapes, dramatic rim light, square composition, <faction palette> background'.

AVOID:
- Photorealism and scanned PBR detail.
- Thin dangling parts: chains, ropes, straps, capes, loose hair, tails, separated fingers.
- Transparent glass geometry; paint glass opaque and add emissive.
- Spikes-and-skulls 'evil' clichés; green skin, tusks, mohawks or blue skin on the Mossbacks.
- Holy or angelic iconography, horses or griffons on the Marchers.
- Any studio or game names in prompts (no 'Blizzard', 'Warcraft', 'WoW style'); describe the qualities instead.

### Music direction
Heroic orchestral-folk hybrid at about 80-110 BPM, with big memorable horn melodies and a clear day/night split. Main theme: a soaring horn and strings melody over a tolling 'lantern bell' ostinato, answered by stone-drum hits and low choir hums. The two factions trade the melody, as WC3's themes did, without quoting it.

Lanternmarch:
- Day tracks: brass chorale, marching snare, handbells and glockenspiel, warm strings, tin-whistle counter-melody; proud and slightly pompous.
- Night tracks: sparse music box, glass harmonica and solo cello; brave and lonely.

Mossback Horde:
- Day tracks: sleepy, plodding hurdy-gurdy drones, bowed bass and distant lur horns, as if the hills are dreaming.
- Night tracks: pounding frame and log drums, clacking stones, a hummed low male choir and a fast folk fiddle; savage and joyful.

Also needed: a creep-camp ambush stinger, a boss fight loop, hero level-up and item-pickup flourishes, and victory and defeat stingers for each faction (about 8-10 cues in total).

AI music prompt hygiene: describe instrumentation, tempo and mood only. Never name Warcraft, Blizzard or their composers. Keep vocals wordless (hums, chants) to avoid lyric issues. Generate loopable 2-3 minute stems with clean starts and ends so tracks can crossfade at dusk and dawn.

### Producibility notes
MESH BUDGET (AI image-to-3D):
- Lanternmarch, 12 meshes: Wickhand, Wardpike, Glassgunner, Lumenwright, Noonglass Engine, Brasswick, Kilnbright, Beacon Hall, Cottage Row, Muster Yard, Glassworks, Lumen Spire.
- Mossbacks, 12 meshes: Grubber, Clobberer, Stoneskipper, Mosswife, Boulderbelly, Ragga, Gloomgut, Sleeping Elder, Snore Pit, Brawl Ring, Moss Hollow, Boulder Chute.
- Creeps, 2 meshes: Bogwallop Toadfolk, Rustwarden.
- Props, 3 meshes: Sunstone Seam, gloamwood/birch tree (tint and scale variants), standing stone/boulder.
- Core total: 29. Stretch item: Trundle the Peddler-Snail shop (30).

ZERO-MESH REUSE (built from primitives, procedural generation or existing meshes):
- Lanternpost and Watch Lamp: primitives plus a lantern prop.
- Gloomstone: the standing-stone prop with a rune decal.
- Sunsplinters: procedural crystal prisms.
- Elder Mossbacks (ultimate summons): Clobberer at 2x with the stone shader.
- Moth-Crowned Warden: Rustwarden at 1.7x plus a crown.
- Croaker: a Toadfolk variant.
- Prism and Bell Jar: primitives.
- Pikes, poles, walls and fences: primitives.

RIGGING:
- Every infantry unit, worker, hero and creep is a humanoid biped, so one shared humanoid skeleton and one retargeted animation library cover them all: idle, walk, attack-swing, attack-throw, cast, work, death, taunt.
- Mossbacks reuse the same clips with slower playback and an additive hunch pose. Stoneform also slows animation to 0.8x.
- The only non-bipeds are deliberately procedural:
  - Noonglass Engine: wheel spin, dish tilt, recoil. Split the wheels off the mesh, or replace them with painted cylinder primitives.
  - Boulderbelly: roll plus squash-and-stretch.
  - Sunsplinters and the snail: bob and rotate.
- There are no quadrupeds, mounts, flyers or slitherers anywhere.

RIGGING RISKS AND MITIGATIONS:
- Mossbacks' long arms, huge noses and hunch can confuse auto-riggers. Generate them upright in A-pose with arms clear of the body and add the hunch in animation.
- Mosswife's shawl must be a solid mass hugging the body.
- Brasswick's lantern-flail is a hand-attached prop with a simple pendulum script, not a modelled chain.
- Hold weapons away from the torso, or generate them as separate primitive props attached to the hand bone.

SIGNATURE SHADERS AND SYSTEMS (cheap, high impact):
(1) One Mossback material uniform, stone_amount, blends to a triplanar granite texture with desaturation and toggles eye and rune emission. This drives day/night for the entire faction with no extra assets.
(2) Lanternlight is a world-space light-field texture (circle SDFs from the list of light sources) that the terrain shader reads to paint amber pools. Only the nearest few sources get real OmniLights, which keeps Godot 4 Forward+ fast with dozens of lanterns.
(3) Day/night runs through a WorldEnvironment gradient plus DirectionalLight rotation and color.
(4) Team color is keyed from the magenta mask zones by an import script into a mask channel, then tinted in the shader.
(5) Lantern glow is always a separate emissive mask, so glass and brass never depend on image-to-3D reflections, which bake badly. Paint them as stylized opaque surfaces.

AUDIO:
- About 14 voiced units at about 10 lines each, roughly 140 TTS lines (select, move, attack, 3-4 annoyed lines, dawn/dusk lines).
- Mossback voices: pitch-shift plus a gravel layer.
- SFX are mostly glass, brass, stone and moth flutters, which are easy text-to-audio prompts.

GAMEPLAY RISKS:
- The day/night asymmetry needs tuning and an AI opponent that understands the cycle: Mossback AI raids at dusk and turtles by day; Marcher AI chains lanterns by day and snuffs out raids at night.
- Night readability: keep ambient brightness at roughly 35% or more and always draw team-color selection discs.
- Stoneform must be telegraphed clearly with the shader, an icon on the unit frame and voice barks.

IP NOTES:
- Quick name checks found no game hits for Lanternmarch, Noonglass, Wickhand or Rustwarden.
- Two names were dropped after the checks: 'Grumm', because World of Warcraft has Grummles, and 'Shardling', because WoW has a Raging Shardling.
- 'Glimmer-' names were avoided because of the near-match 'Songs of Glimmerwick'.
- The search budget ran out before Mossback, Boulderbelly, Gloomgut, Bogwallop, Sunsplinter, Brasswick and Kilnbright could be checked; they still need a proper trademark search before anything is public.
- Never use the word 'troll' in the UI, to avoid WC3 troll associations, and never put Blizzard terms in generation prompts.

- Folktale trolls are still trolls, a WC3 race, and turning to stone at dawn is well known from Tolkien. 'Troll' must be kept out of the UI and marketing, as the pitch already notes
- Closest of the four to the human-vs-orc template. It is still clearly not Freecraft, but it is the least surprising of the pitches
- With a 6-minute day and a 4-minute night, Mossbacks spend about 60% of the time in their weaker form. Forced Stoneform inside Lanternlight risks turtling stalemates and needs careful tuning and AI that understands the cycle
- Several names (Mossback, Boulderbelly, Gloomgut, Bogwallop, Brasswick, Kilnbright) were not trademark-checked
- The upkeep thresholds (0-24 / 25-40 / 41-60, cap 60) are tighter than WC3's and need tuning so armies still reach 20-35 units

**Art director — keep:**
- Lanternlight territory: cheap Lanternposts built from primitives, placed in chains inside existing light. Visible, readable, and costs no AI mesh.
- One stone_amount material uniform drives the whole Mossback day/night transformation (granite blend plus eye and rune emission) with no extra assets.
- Night readability through glow color: an amber constellation for the Marchers against paired moon-cyan eyes for the Mossbacks.
- A light-field SDF texture painted in the terrain shader, with real OmniLights only on the nearest few sources. A solid Godot 4 performance pattern.
- The most production-safe plan: every character is a biped, the only non-bipeds are procedural (cart, rolling boulder, floating prisms), and there are no flyers or quadrupeds.
- The Sleeping Elder town hall is a petrified giant with a village on its back whose eyes open at night. A memorable building with no animation cost.

**Art director — weaknesses:**
- By its own description it fills the WC3 Alliance and Horde slots one to one: footman, rifleman, priest, siege, paladin and archmage analogs against grunt, headhunter, shaman, catapult and two chieftain-mystic analogs. That is the closest of the four concepts to the generic feel the user rejected.
- IP-adjacent signals stack up: the faction is called 'Mossback Horde' and is made of trolls, the humans are blue and gold (the Lordaeron/Alliance palette), 'Magister' is a WoW blood elf title, and there is 'Hold the Light' phrasing.
- Many names were never checked (Mossback, Boulderbelly, Gloomgut, Bogwallop, Sunsplinter, Brasswick, Kilnbright).
- Victorian lamplighters are fresher than knights, but still read as 'the human faction' from a distance.
- Stone-at-dawn trolls are well-trodden (Tolkien, Discworld), so the horde's novelty is limited.

**Producer — keep:**
- Stoneform/Nightwild driven by one material uniform (stone_amount blending to triplanar granite plus eye/rune emission toggles), which re-skins a whole faction for day and night with zero extra assets
- Lanternlight drawn as a world-space field of circle SDFs in the terrain shader, with real OmniLights only on the nearest few sources, so dozens of lanterns stay fast in Godot Forward+
- Mesh savings without new models: Lanternpost and Watch Lamp from primitives plus a lantern prop, the Gloomstone reusing the standing-stone prop, Elder Mossbacks as scaled Clobberers, the boss as a scaled Rustwarden, and Sunsplinter creeps as procedural prisms
- Every animated character is a humanoid biped on one retargeted library; the only non-bipeds roll on wheels or as a ball and are deliberately procedural, with no quadrupeds, mounts, flyers or slitherers anywhere
- Glass is painted opaque with separate emissive masks, so nothing depends on transparency or reflections from image-to-3D
- Instant night readability: warm amber dots for the Marchers against paired moon-cyan eyes for the Mossbacks, with tall vertical shapes against wide, low boulder shapes
- Day/night (which WC3 already has) is the faction's core mechanic instead of a new audio-sync system, so it is deterministic and easy to build

**Producer — weaknesses:**
- The least novel structure: it maps the WC3 slots almost one to one (footman, rifleman, priest, siege, a tank marshal and an archmage against grunt, headhunter, shaman, siege, chieftain and shaman), which risks reading as a reskin; give each unit a sharper identity
- Mossbacks are trolls in all but name; keep the folklore look (huge noses, moss, no tusks) strict to avoid WC3 troll associations
- Several names were never trademark-checked (Mossback, Boulderbelly, Gloomgut, Bogwallop, Sunsplinter, Brasswick, Kilnbright)
- Night readability and the day/night asymmetry need tuning, and the AI must understand the cycle (raid at dusk, chain lanterns by day)
- Long noses, long arms and the hunch can confuse auto-riggers; generate them upright in A-pose and add the hunch in animation, as the concept plans
- The supply cap of 60 and upkeep starting at 25 are tighter than WC3 and should be checked against the 20-40 unit target

## Vibecraft: Hush & Holler

*Pitch angle: wildcard*

**Logline.** A country-sized titan sleeps as it drifts through a sea of clouds. On its back, ram-horned lullaby knights who must keep it asleep go to war with a brass-band army of rooster-folk who must wake it up. It is a Warcraft III-style hero RTS where the map is a living creature and every battle is silence against noise.

### World
Mumbleback is a titan the size of a country: part tortoise, part whale, part mountain. It has slept for a thousand years while drifting through an endless pink-and-gold Cloud Sea. Its hide is the ground. Its fur grows in forests of amber Bristle stalks. Where its dreams press up through the skin they harden into glowing Dreamglass. Two peoples climbed onto it at the end of the First Night. The Lullfold are ram-horned shepherds whose sacred duty is to keep it asleep, because they believe a waking Mumbleback would simply roll over and shake the world off. The Reveille are rooster-folk heralds whose sacred duty is to wake it at the Last Dawn.

For a thousand years both duties were only theory. Now a violet storm-wall, the Grinding Squall, has appeared dead ahead. The Reveille say this IS the Last Dawn: wake the Titan so it can swim around the storm. The Lullfold say Mumbleback has slept through a hundred storms and that waking it now will kill everyone. Both have a point, and both have armies. The tone is a bedtime story told with war drums: storybook warmth, real heroics, constant comedy in the unit responses, and real scale in the setting. The Titan's horned head looms on the skybox horizon, the ground sometimes twitches, and the whole world dims and brightens with each slow breath.

### Why it isn't generic
This is not medieval humans against orcs. There are no humans, orcs, elves, dwarves or undead, no castle-and-farmland tileset, and no grimdark war. (1) The map is a living creature. Trees are its hair, gold mines are crystallized dreams breaking through its skin, rocks are warts and shed scales, day/night is its breathing, and upkeep is literally the Titan getting itchy from too many boots. (2) The conflict is a charming question with two sympathetic sides: let the god sleep, or wake it? That gives the game built-in humor and heart. (3) Each faction is defined by SOUND, quiet versus loud, and that one idea drives visuals, voice acting, music and mechanics: Drowse/Quilt against Noise/Crescendo. They play differently (slow-and-smother against tempo-and-swarm), look opposite (soft cream circles against brass-red vertical spikes) and sound opposite (yawning lullabies against marching brass). (4) Every unit can be pitched in one line and remembered: a hero who sleepwalks into battle with her eyes closed, a bomber that is a hot-air balloon shaped like a nightcap dropping sandbags of sleep, a siege engine that is a giant wind-up alarm clock, a worker hen called the Henchman. The core loop is still pure WC3: heroes to level 10 with 3 abilities plus an ultimate, creep camps with item drops, a gold analog and a lumber analog, supply with upkeep tiers, 20-40 unit armies, tiered town halls, towers, a neutral shop and a healing fountain.

### Resources
GOLD analog, DREAMGLASS: iridescent cyan-to-pink crystals that form where the Titan's dreams push up through its hide. It is mined from Dream Blisters, big crystal geodes cracking out of the skin (about 12,500 per blister), and workers carry glowing shards back to base. LUMBER analog, BRISTLE: the Titan's hair grows in forests of giant amber stalks with tufted tips, each tuft the size of a pine tree. Shearlings snip it and Henchmen saw it, and like WC3 trees it does not regrow within a match (it is very slow fur). FOOD analog: the Lullfold use 'Pillows' from Snugs and the Hall, and the Reveille use 'Perches' from Perches and the Hall. The cap is 80. Typical costs: workers 1, line infantry 2, casters 2, Balloon or Alarm Engine 4, heroes 5. UPKEEP analog, THE ITCH: the more boots on Mumbleback's back, the itchier it gets, and its twitching skin shakes Dreamglass loose before workers can carry it home. 0-40 supply is Calm (100% Dreamglass per trip). 41-60 is Itchy (70%; the UI shows an itch icon and the ground under your base shivers slightly). 61-80 is Scratchy (40%, with an occasional camera-shake twitch and a distant annoyed rumble). This keeps armies at WC3's 20-40 unit scale and gives the rule a funny reason to exist in the world. Bristle is never taxed. Neutral economy: a Lost & Found shop and a Warm Pore healing vent (see creeps and tileset).

### Faction: The Lullfold: ram-horned keepers of the Titan's sleep

**Fantasy.** Stout, stubborn, deeply cozy sheep-folk who have sung Mumbleback to sleep for a thousand years. They are knights of bedtime. They wear quilted armor and carry pillow tower-shields stuffed with iron filings, shepherd's-crook halberds, music boxes and moth-lanterns. They play defensively, with heavy sustain and crowd control: they make the enemy too drowsy to fight, then flatten them with very heavy rams. Think 'a fortress that tucks you in, then headbutts you.' Personality: polite, sleepy, unshakeable, secretly the most dangerous people on the Titan when woken up.

**Unique mechanic.** QUILT & DROWSE. (1) Quilt: Lullfold buildings must be placed on Quilt. Quilt is a patchwork ground decal that spreads from the Featherbed Hall and each Snug, like a cozy blight, and costs only a terrain splat. Friendly units on Quilt regenerate +2 HP/s ('tucked in'), and enemies standing on Quilt gain Drowse twice as fast, so attacking a Lullfold base is like wading into a feather bed. (2) Drowse: most Lullfold attacks and spells apply Drowse stacks, shown as floating lavender 'z' billboards above the target and readable at any zoom. Each stack slows attack and move speed by 6%. At 5 stacks a unit Dozes for 2.5s and cannot act. Any single hit over 40 damage wakes it, so the player has to time burst damage instead of just chain-disabling. Heroes cap at 4 stacks and only fully doze from ultimates. Counterplay: Reveille Noise strips stacks. During the map's 'Deep Sleep' phase (night), each Drowse application adds +1 extra stack.

**Visual identity.** Silhouette: round and bottom-heavy. Torsos are cloud-lump wool, big spiral horns read as two circles from top-down, legs are stubby, and shields and pillows are oversized. Shape language is circles, spirals, domes and pillows. Materials: wool sculpted as big clumps (never strands), quilted canvas, felt, tarnished pewter bells, beeswax candles, pale birch wood, stitched leather. Palette: Fleece Cream #F4ECD8, Dusk Lavender #9A86C9, Midnight Indigo #2E3566, Candle Amber #F2B550, Pewter #8C8F9C, Sage Felt #A3B98E. Drowse VFX glow #C9B8FF. Team color goes on the NIGHTCAP, the largest and highest top-down shape on every unit, so an army is a sea of colored nightcaps. It also goes on the quilt patch on each shield face, on banner pennants, and on roof patches of buildings.

**Audio identity.** Voice personality: drowsy, slow, polite and slightly slurred, with sheep vowels ('baa-rely awake') and yawns mid-sentence. They become alarmingly crisp when attacking. Original sample lines. Pillowguard select: 'Mmh... is it morning?' / 'I was only resting my eyes.' Move: 'Slippers on.' / 'Shuffling over.' Attack: 'Nighty-night!' / 'Lights out, friend.' Annoyed (repeated clicks): 'I counted sheep once. Then I realized I AM the sheep. Who counts us?' / 'Poke me again and I'm putting on the SECOND nightcap.' Tallyslinger: 'One... two... you.' Hushmother: 'Hush now, dearie.' Bellwether: 'Follow the bell.' / 'Ding. Dong. Bedtime.' Death: a long yawn trailing into a tiny snore. SFX: soft cotton thumps, pewter bell chimes, music-box plinks, pillow-whump impacts. Music instrumentation: celesta, music box, harp, glockenspiel, bowed psaltery, warm low strings, solo cello and a wordless hummed choir, mostly as a 3/4 lullaby-waltz.

**Worker.** Shearling: a small lamb in an oversized team-color nightcap and dungarees, carrying giant tailor's shears and a felt sack. It snips Bristle stalks for lumber, chips Dreamglass loose with the shear handles, and builds by stitching buildings together with a huge needle (the build animation is sewing). It can repair ('Tuck In'). Lines: 'Snip-snip.' / 'Stitching, stitching...' / 'Shear the hill? ...Okay.'

**Units**

- Pillowguard - frontline tank infantry - a barrel-round ram in a quilted gambeson with a pewter half-helm over a team-color nightcap. He carries a door-sized pillow tower-shield stuffed with iron filings and a crook-mace. - Fluff Up: puffs his fleece to 1.4x size for 8s, gaining +6 armor and -40% move speed; enemies that melee him gain 1 Drowse per hit.
- Tallyslinger - ranged DPS - a lean ewe in a moth-patterned hooded cloak with a felt pebble pouch and a forked birch sling. She counts out loud while she fires. - Tally: every 3rd consecutive hit on the same target applies 2 Drowse and plays a rising numbered chime (counting sheep, as a weapon).
- Hushmother - support caster - an elderly hunched ewe in a shawl and round spectacles, holding a music box mounted on a crook staff whose wind-up key turns procedurally. - Lullaby (4s channel): area effect that adds 1 Drowse/s to enemies and heals allies 8 HP/s. Her basic autocast spell is Warm Milk, a single-target heal.
- Nightcap Balloon - air bomber and siege. No rig: a static mesh animated with procedural bob, sway and basket swing. It is a patchwork hot-air balloon shaped like a giant team-color nightcap with a dangling tassel, carrying a wicker cradle-basket of two sleepy lamb bombardiers. - Sandbag Drop: lobs a sack of dream-sand that deals area damage (x3 against buildings) and applies 2 Drowse. It cannot attack air.

**Heroes**

- The Bellwether - Strength hero, frontline leader. A towering ram knight whose horns curl like wagon wheels, in quilted plate, with a huge bronze bell hung on his chest (procedural pendulum swing) and a crook-halberd. Abilities: 1) Toll of Rest: rings the bell for a 300-radius blast dealing 60/110/160 damage and 2/3/4 Drowse. 2) Pillow Fort: raises a ring of giant pillows (one cheap instanced mesh) around a target point for 6s, blocking pathing to trap enemies or shelter allies. 3) Flock Together (passive aura): allies gain +1 armor for each other allied unit within 400 range (max +4/+6/+8). 4) ULTIMATE Lights Out: night falls in a 900 radius for 10s. Enemy non-heroes doze instantly for up to 5s (damage cannot wake them for the first 1.5s), enemy heroes gain 4 Drowse, and Lullfold units deal +35% damage to drowsing or dozing targets.
- The Somnambulist - Agility/caster hybrid, harasser. A lanky ewe sleepwalking in a long nightgown and tasseled nightcap, eyes closed and one arm outstretched, hovering a hoof's width above the ground. She holds a candlestick in one hand and fences with a giant knitting needle. Abilities: 1) Sleepwalk: for 8s she phases through units, ignores slows and is immune to Noise; each enemy she drifts through gains 1 Drowse, and the walk ends in a yawn that deals 60/100/140 area damage. 2) Dream Puppet: takes control of a DOZING enemy non-hero, or a creep up to level 5, for 20/30/40s. 3) Night Light: plants a candle ward for 60s that reveals a 600 radius and heals nearby allies 3 HP/s. 4) ULTIMATE Counting the Infinite Flock: a stampede of 12 glowing spectral dream-sheep (the Shearling mesh reused with a lavender ghost shader) charges in a line, knocking enemies aside for 220 damage and applying maximum Drowse.

**Buildings**

The whole building gently 'breathes' through a procedural scale pulse, and it has team-color quilt patches.

- Cradle of the First Lamb - hero altar and revive - a giant carved wooden cradle on a stone dais under a hanging mobile of moons and stars that slowly turns procedurally.

### Faction: The Reveille: brass-band rooster-folk sworn to wake the Titan

**Fantasy.** Swaggering, sleepless, magnificently loud rooster-folk and their hens: a marching-band army in lacquered brass, plumed shako helmets and steel spurs. Their prophecy says Mumbleback must wake at the Last Dawn to swim clear of the storm-wall ahead, and they have decided the Last Dawn is TODAY. Playstyle: an aggressive tempo faction with fast units and burst damage that grows the more of them fight together. Think 'a parade that turns into a cavalry charge.' Personality: proud, theatrical, relentlessly optimistic, exhausted but would rather die than admit it.

**Unique mechanic.** NOISE & CRESCENDO. Every Reveille unit emits Noise, shown as a pulsing gold ring decal under it. Crescendo: each unit gains +1 stack per 3s in combat, plus +1 extra for each other Reveille unit within 500 range, up to 10 stacks. Each stack gives +3% attack speed and +2% move speed. Stacks drain out of combat, because going quiet makes them weak. At 10 stacks a unit reaches 'Fortissimo', and its next attack or ability is empowered with a bigger area of effect and knockback. Noise also removes 1 Drowse/s from allies inside the ring, and units at 5+ Crescendo cannot Doze. Design intent: the mechanic rewards committing in big groups and staying in the fight, and the Lullfold punish it with slows that drag fights out and split groups apart. Map-level twist: Reveille buildings are 'too loud to sleep near', so Quilt cannot spread within 300 of them. That makes the ground a visible tug-of-war between the two sides, and in Crescendo-building terms the Reveille build it 50% faster during the map's 'Stirring' phase (day).

**Visual identity.** Silhouette: tall, vertical and spiky, with fan tails, plumed shako helmets, sharp beaks, bugle bells and lances. Waists are narrow, chests are big, and feet are oversized brass-capped plantigrade boots (never thin bird legs). From the camera they read as a field of upright gold-and-red spikes against the Lullfold's soft cream blobs. Shape language is triangles, verticals and fans. Materials: painted lacquered brass (stylized, not PBR chrome), red-lacquer wood, glossy feathers layered like armor plates, drum leather and cast-iron clockwork. Palette: Brass Gold #E0A526, Comb Red #D6312B, Sunrise Orange #F28A30, Feather Ivory #FFF3DC, Lacquer Black #2A1F1C, Verdigris accent #3FA39A. Crescendo VFX gold #FFD45C. Team color goes on the tall SHAKO PLUME, the highest point seen from top-down, and on the chest sash or tabard, drumheads, building pennants and the weathervane flag. Combs and wattles stay natural red on every team.

**Audio identity.** Voice personality: LOUD, brash and theatrical, like a drill sergeant crossed with a vaudeville bandleader. Every line is half-shouted, and some end in a strangled mini-crow. Deliberately avoid any famous cartoon-rooster accent. Original sample lines. Spurknight select: 'Bright and EARLY!' / 'Spurs polished, sir!' Move: 'Strutting!' / 'Hup-two, hup-TWO!' Attack: 'Rise and SHINE!' / 'WAKEY WAKEY!' Annoyed: 'I've been up since four. Every day. Forever. I'm fine. I'm FINE.' / 'Is that a snooze button? Do NOT press the snooze button.' Banger: 'Light 'em up!' / 'Short fuse, shorter temper!' Drum Major: 'Keep the BEAT!' Morning Marshal: 'The sun answers to ME.' Racketeer: 'Let's make some noise, see?' Death: a strangled half-crow and a deflating brass 'bwomp'. SFX: bugle stabs, snare rolls, firecracker pops, alarm-clock ticking, cymbal crashes, steam whistles. Music instrumentation: marching band (cornets, trombones, sousaphone), snare and bass drum, piccolo, glockenspiel and steam-calliope flourishes, in 2/4 and 6/8 marches.

**Worker.** Henchman: a plump hen in a tin hard hat with a candle stub on top, rolled sleeves and a jingling toolbelt. She drills Dreamglass with a hand-crank drill, saws Bristle with a two-hand bucksaw, and builds by hammering in a frantic drum rhythm. Lines: 'Shift starts at dawn!' / 'Cluck it, I'll do it.' / 'Henchman reporting. What? It's a perfectly good word.'

**Units**

- Spurknight - fast melee shock trooper - a tall rooster in a red-lacquer cuirass and plumed team-color shako, with a short sabre and brass-buckled boots fitted with huge steel spurs. - Spur Kick: leaps up to 400 range onto a target spurs-first for a 2s stun and +2 Crescendo; the Fortissimo version also knocks back adjacent enemies.
- Banger - ranged artillery infantry - a stocky hen in welding goggles and a leather apron, with a bandolier of firecrackers and a bamboo launcher tube on her shoulder. - String of Pops: fires a ground-targeted line of 6 crackers that pop in sequence for small splash damage and interrupt channels (the hard counter to Lullaby).
- Drum Major - support and aura - a very tall rooster in a white band coat with gold frogging and a huge bass drum strapped to his chest, twirling a mace-baton. Passive Marching Beat: +10% ally move speed. - Wake-Up Roll: a 3s drum roll that removes all Drowse and Doze from allies in a 600 radius and gives each +3 Crescendo.
- Alarm Engine - siege. No rig: a static mesh with procedural wheels, ticking clock hands, a turning wind-up key and an oscillating bell hammer. It is a giant brass wind-up alarm clock on cart wheels with twin bells, wound by a Henchman riding on the back. - Ring Ring: after a 2s windup with loud ticking, releases a 360-degree shockwave that deals heavy damage to buildings, knocks back units and strips all Quilt in a 350 radius for 30s.

**Heroes**

- The Morning Marshal - Strength hero, vanguard. An enormous golden rooster general in brass parade armor and epaulettes, with a towering team-color plume and a cape of layered tail feathers. His weapon is a spiked morningstar flail whose head is a brass rising sun. Abilities: 1) Sunrise Smash: leaps and slams for 75/125/175 damage in a 300 radius, and enemies hit are dazzled with a 40% miss chance for 3s. 2) Wake-Up Call: a colossal crow that removes Drowse and Doze from all allies within 800 and deafens (silences) enemies within 400 for 2/3/4s. 3) Strut (passive aura): allies within 600 gain +10/20/30% attack speed while moving and for 3s after moving. 4) ULTIMATE Make It Morning: drags a miniature sunrise over a 1000 radius for 12s. ### Later factions

- The Engorged Court: bloated tick-and-louse aristocrats in powdered wigs and lace who burrow into Mumbleback to drink it dry. They have a tunnel network between buildings and a life-drain economy, and their units fatten (and slow) as they feed. The faction grows naturally out of the slice's Tick Brute creeps.
- The Dreamt: beings from Mumbleback's own dreams that leaked out through the Dream Blisters, such as paper-cutout knights, wind-up toy soldiers and kite-dragons. ### Creeps

- Dustbunnies (level 1-2 camps of 3-5): hopping puffballs of shed dander, from fist-sized to pony-sized, with two button eyes. NO RIG: a sphere mesh with a fuzzy-clump shader and procedural squash-and-stretch hops. When one dies it lets out a Sneeze Cloud that briefly blinds attackers. Drops: small Dreamglass bundles and consumables such as the Cocoa Mug (heal) and the Homeward Pillow (teleport to base).
- Tick Brutes (level 3-5 camps of 2-3, with a larger tinted Tick Matron as camp leader): bipedal bloated parasites with a hard red-brown carapace on their backs, little hooked arms, a pale swollen belly and a drinking proboscis. - The Under-Bed Bogey (level 8 boss guarding the central Grand Dream Blister): a hulking, shaggy, purple-black closet-monster biped with long knuckle-dragging arms, glowing yellow eyes and a stolen quilt for a cape. - Neutral structures (not creeps, listed for completeness): the Lost & Found, a junk shop inside a giant discarded boot that sells charged items and is the shop analog; and the Warm Pore, a steaming skin vent that restores HP and mana nearby and is the fountain analog.

### Map / tileset
The map is Mumbleback's Shoulder, a 2-player map of about 128x128 cells. Terrain textures, all cheap tiling images: Hide (leathery olive-grey skin with soft pores and creases, #7C8366); Moss Mantle (thick bright moss carpets, #6FA046); Lichen Scale (cliff and plateau tiles made of huge overlapping scale plates spotted with orange and teal lichen); Dander Dust (pale beige paths, #CDBB98); Dew Pools (shallow pale-teal water in skin folds); and Quilt (the Lullfold ground decal, a patchwork of lavender, cream and team color with visible stitching). Doodads and props: Bristle forests (amber hair stalks like bamboo with tufted tips, #D9B26A, swaying gently to the Titan's breath wind), Wartstones (smooth lichen-crusted dome rocks), Shed Scales (flat slab rocks), Dream Blisters (the gold mine analog: an iridescent crystal geode bursting out of a raised ring of skin, #7FE3F0 to #E79BEA, glowing at night), Pore Vents (puff steam in rhythm with the breathing), and odd ancient things the Titan picked up over millennia (a rusted ship's anchor, the bleached ribs of some smaller titan, a fallen weather-bell). Skybox: an endless pink-gold cloud sea. Mumbleback's colossal horned head and one sleepy eye rise on one horizon, and the violet Grinding Squall storm-wall flickers with lightning on the other. This is a painted 2D skybox, so it costs no models. Day/night is replaced by the BREATH CYCLE. 'Stirring' (day, about 4 min) has a warm apricot key light #FFC98A, long shadows and golden dust motes, and Reveille Crescendo builds 50% faster. 'Deep Sleep' (night, about 3 min) has lavender-indigo moonlight #6E6AC9, glowing Dreamglass and z-shaped motes drifting up out of the pores, and Lullfold Drowse is stronger. Each transition is marked by one enormous slow breath sound and a global dim or brighten. Layout: the Lullfold start in the warm Nape Hollow (SW) and the Reveille on the Shoulder-Blade Ridge (NE). Each base has a main Dream Blister plus a natural expansion guarded by Tick Brutes, and each side has two Dustbunny camps for early hero leveling. The center holds the Grand Dream Blister guarded by the Bogey. The Lost & Found sits at one crossroads and the Warm Pore at the other. Two ramps climb the scale ridges, and Bristle forests form chokepoints that can be cut open over time.

### Art direction
SHAPE LANGUAGE: Warcraft-era chunky stylization done in our own way. Big heads (about 1:3 head-to-body for units, 1:3.5 for heroes); huge hands, hooves, feet and weapons (weapons about 1.3x realistic size); compact torsos and thick limbs. No part may be thinner than about 4% of model height, because thin parts vanish at RTS zoom and break image-to-3D. The Lullfold are circles and spirals (wool lumps, horn curls, domes, pillows). The Reveille are triangles and verticals (plumes, beaks, fan tails, spires, bugle bells). Every model must pass a 64px squint test from the gameplay camera (about 55 degree pitch, 3/4 top-down): faction, unit role and team color should be identifiable from silhouette and top surface alone. RENDERING: hand-painted albedo with baked AO and painted highlight strokes, low-frequency detail only, saturated but never neon. Wool and feathers are SCULPTED CLUMPS and layered plates, never strands or realistic fur. Metal is painted (brass is a warm gradient with one cream highlight stroke), not chrome PBR. LIGHTING: warm apricot key, cool lavender fill and rim, and a soft 2-3 step toon ramp shader in Godot. Add a thin inverted-hull outline (1-1.5px) on units only, for readability against busy terrain. TEAM COLOR: generate every unit and building with its team-color zones (nightcaps, plumes, sashes, pennants, roof patches) painted flat in a key magenta #FF00C8 that appears nowhere else, then replace that hue with the player color in a Godot shader. IMAGE PROMPT SKELETON for concepts that feed image-to-3D: 'stylized hand-painted 3D game character, chunky heroic proportions, [faction] [unit description], full body, A-pose with arms clearly separated from body, front view, plain light-grey background, even flat lighting, bold readable silhouette, [sculpted wool clumps / layered feather armor plates], team-color areas in flat magenta, no text, no logo'. Buildings: 'stylized hand-painted 3D RTS building, chunky exaggerated proportions, [description], 3/4 view from above, isolated on grey, simple bold shapes, large readable details'. ICONS AND UI: painted bust portraits on circular badges (lavender velvet for the Lullfold, sunburst gold for the Reveille). The Lullfold UI is a stitched quilt panel with wooden toggle-buttons; the Reveille UI is red lacquer and riveted brass. AVOID: photoreal fur, the claymation/plasticine look (keep away from any famous stop-motion sheep), cartoon-rooster caricature from classic animation, kawaii/chibi anime eyes, thin bird legs, spindly weapons, noisy micro-detail, grimdark or gore, and generic medieval humans, orcs, elves or undead. Specifically avoid green tusked brutes, lion or fist heraldry, Warcraft-like UI frames, Warcraft's gold-and-stone icon borders or font styles, and any 'polymorph into sheep' visual gag.

### Music direction
One shared original leitmotif, 'Mumbleback's Lullaby' (a simple 6-note rising-and-falling phrase), arranged two ways so the soundtrack itself argues. LULLFOLD THEMES: 3/4 lullaby-waltz at 60-72 bpm with celesta, music box, harp, glockenspiel, bowed psaltery, warm low strings, solo cello and a wordless hummed choir; cozy but noble, like a medieval nursery hymn. REVEILLE THEMES: 2/4 and 6/8 marches at 112-128 bpm with cornets, trombones, sousaphone, snare and bass drum, piccolo, glockenspiel and steam-calliope flourishes; swaggering and comic-heroic. MAIN, BATTLE and MENU: full heroic orchestra in the early-2000s fantasy-RTS idiom (big brass, low taiko-style drums, choir 'aahs'), always with a very low double-pulse 'heartbeat' at about 50 bpm underneath, representing the sleeping Titan. The menu is the Titan breathing: slow low-string swells and a distant whale-like groan under a solo celesta statement of the leitmotif. STINGERS: Deep Sleep falls with a music box winding down; Stirring begins with a lone bugle call answered by the brass section; a hero levels up with a short leitmotif fragment in the faction's instruments; Lullfold victory is the choir resolving the lullaby; Reveille victory is a full-band fanfare ending in a triumphant crow. ADAPTIVE: per faction, generate calm, tension and battle variants at matched tempo and key so they can crossfade on combat detection, plus breath-phase ambience beds (soft wind, creaking hide, distant rumbles). AI MUSIC PROMPT TIPS: name the instrumentation, tempo, meter and mood explicitly; specify 'instrumental, no lyrics' and 'loopable, 90-150 seconds'; generate several takes and pick the ones whose leitmotif matches by ear.

### Producibility notes
MODEL BUDGET: 28 AI models, with 2 optional. Rigged bipeds (14): the 12 faction characters (Shearling, Pillowguard, Tallyslinger, Hushmother, Bellwether, Somnambulist, Henchman, Spurknight, Banger, Drum Major, Morning Marshal, Racketeer) plus Tick Brute and Bogey. Procedurally animated constructs (2): Nightcap Balloon and Alarm Engine. Static buildings (10): Hall, Supply, Barracks, Advanced and Tower for each faction. Props (2): Dream Blister and the Lost & Found boot shop. The 2 altars can be AI models (bringing the total to 30) or kitbashed from primitives (a cradle shape, a disc plus ray planes). ZERO-MODEL assets: Bristle trees (procedural curved tapered cylinders plus tuft cards with a painted texture and vertex sway), Wartstones and Shed Scales (noise-displaced spheres and slabs), Dustbunnies (a sphere with a clump shader and squash-stretch), Bedsheet Haunts (a cloth plane with a flutter shader), Pillow Fort pillows (a squashed rounded box, instanced), spectral dream-sheep (the Shearling with a ghost shader), Quilt and Noise rings (decals), and the skybox titan (2D painting). RIGGING: humanoid auto-rigging covers everything with legs. (a) Sheep: generate in A-pose with arms clearly separated from the torso; put big wool volume on the torso only and tight short clumps on the limbs so skin weights don't bleed; horns, bells and shields are rigid attachments. (b) Roosters: digitigrade bird legs are the main auto-rig risk, so the design puts every rooster in big plantigrade brass-capped boots and gives them human arms with feathered gauntlets (no functional wings). Tail fans attach rigidly to the hips and plumes to the head bone, with Godot's SpringBoneSimulator3D for jiggle. (c) The Somnambulist's hover and the Balloon's and Alarm Engine's motion are code, not animation. Retarget one shared humanoid animation library (idle, walk, run, attack x2, cast, death, work) through Godot's SkeletonProfileHumanoid, then add personality procedurally: a rooster head-bob bone modifier, a sheep yawn idle layer, and a waddle multiplier. PROCEDURAL PARTS: image-to-3D fuses everything into one mesh, so moving parts (spinning wheel, weathervane, gear, bell, music-box figurine, clock hands, balloon basket) should be separate primitives or separately generated small meshes attached in Godot. Where that isn't possible, use vertex-shader wobble or whole-object bob and scale 'breathing'. TEAM COLOR: the magenta key-color method keeps one texture per model and lets the shader recolor it; check every generation for magenta bleeding into non-team areas. READABILITY: Drowse z-billboards, Crescendo gold rings and Quilt ground make the core mechanics visible from the camera with no extra models. RISKS: (1) Tone. Anthropomorphic sheep and roosters can read as a kids' farm game. Counter this with heroic proportions, real armor, weighty VFX and audio, an epic orchestral score and the looming Titan, and keep the humor in the voice lines rather than the visuals. (2) Lookalikes. Steer clear of famous stop-motion sheep, classic cartoon roosters, the bird faction of a popular woodland-animal board game and a well-known lamb-cult game, and never reference WC3's polymorph sheep. (3) Crowd-control fatigue. Drowse can feel oppressive, which is why big hits wake dozers, heroes are capped and Reveille has three dispels; playtest early. (4) Image-to-3D mush on buildings. Keep building concepts to 3-5 bold forms with large readable details. (5) NAME CHECK NOT DONE. This run hit its web-search limit, so no name was checked online. Before locking the names, do a quick trademark and store search on Hush & Holler, Mumbleback, Lullfold, Reveille, Dreamglass, Spurknight, Pillowguard and Bellwether. Reveille and Dreamglass are the most likely to collide; fallbacks are 'The Dawnbrass' and 'Slumberquartz'. Many ability names are ordinary words (Lights Out, Wake-Up Call, Encore), which carries low risk, and none copy WC3 spell names.

## Judges' verdicts

**WC3 fan** picked **fidelity**. With wc3_feel and readability_gameplay weighted highest, Lantern & Stone (fidelity) wins clearly. Weighted total, with wc3_feel and readability at double weight: fidelity 59, wildcard 52, resonance 51.5, folklore 48.5.

As a WC3 player, it is the only pitch where I instantly know how to play both sides. The Marchers fill the sturdy infantry / rifle / caster / siege / marshal / wizard slots. The Mossbacks fill grunt / thrower / shaman / battering siege / chieftain / mystic. Every unit is still a fresh fantasy: lamplighters with bottled noon, sun-biting shamans, a curled-up elder you roll into walls. Its signature system is the strongest in the set because it is simple and visible: is it lit here? Lanternlight chains, Stoneform against Nightwild, and the paired counters Bottled Dawn/Moonshade and Second Sunrise/Eclipse all build on WC3's day/night cycle without adding hidden state. Readability is the best of the four: amber dots against moon-cyan eyes at night, and tall verticals against wide boulders by day. One shader uniform drives the whole Mossback transformation. It is also the most producible: all characters are bipeds, the only non-bipeds are procedural, and Lanternposts, towers and the Gloomstone cost no new meshes.

Resonance and the wildcard are more original. Resonance's rhythm-locked Toll and muffled audio would feel like a gimmick to competitive players, and its stealth plus aura-muting plus Echo layers hide too much. The wildcard's world-as-titan is brilliant, but sheep and roosters pull it toward a kids' farm game and the polymorph-sheep joke. Folklore is the freshest setting, but it carries real cultural-sensitivity risk, a jungle palette close to WoW trolls, and a thin, dark Downcourt silhouette that is hard to read.

Required fixes for the winner:
1. Rename 'The Mossback Horde', since Horde is the core Warcraft faction term. Something like 'Mossback Clans' or 'Hillfolk Moot' works.
2. Never use 'troll' in the UI or marketing.
3. Trademark-check the unchecked names.
4. Tune the 6-minute/4-minute day/night split and forced Stoneform so the game does not turn into a turtling stalemate.

Grafts worth stealing from the other pitches:
- Resonance: music detuning as an audio cue for upkeep, the shared leitmotif played in two arrangements, and back-pennant team-color placement.
- Wildcard: billboard-style status stacks to make Stoneform state readable, and a looming skybox landmark.
- Folklore: a neutral center objective like the Old Ballcourt, and kitchenware-style comedic creep camps.

**Art director** picked **resonance**. I weighted originality and IP safety double. On that basis Resonance and Wildcard tie at the top: both are original at 9 and both score 7 on IP safety, with different risk profiles. Resonance wins the tie on WC3 feel and on how cohesive the art can be with AI-generated assets.

Why Resonance:
- Its humanoid races still read as heroic fantasy.
- Bell-armored soldiers and moth duelists stay closer to the chunky epic tone the user asked for than livestock knights do.
- The bronze/verdigris against velvet/teal split is a tight, generation-friendly art bible with strong grayscale silhouettes (domes plus pennants against a V of plumes over a wing triangle).
- It is the only concept with a documented name-collision pass.
- Its signature systems tie art, audio and mechanics together, and Echo Replay costs no extra art.

Its IP problems are real but fixable:
- The Hush lean toward Night Elves. Fix: drop the moon-crescent naming and imagery (Moonsickle, Moonshrine), make the town hall something other than a hollow tree, and change the stand-still Shroud trigger so it doesn't echo Shadowmeld.
- Rename 'Old Mother Murmur', since Murmur is a sound-themed WoW boss.
- Rename 'Hammerfall', a WoW Horde town.
- Make Hilde less dwarf-coded.

Scope recommendations for production:
- Treat the music-synced pitch and quantize features as stretch polish.
- Keep the Toll on a deterministic gameplay timer.

The other two concepts:
- Folklore is fresh, but it borrows heavily from a living people's sacred narrative, uses 'the Maw' and sits near Zandalari visuals.
- Fidelity has the best WC3 feel and producibility, but by design it maps one to one onto the Alliance and Horde rosters. It also uses 'Horde', trolls and blue-and-gold humans, so it scores lowest on originality and IP.

Ideas worth grafting into the winner:
- From Wildcard: team color on the highest top-down shape, z-style status billboards with a wake-on-big-hit rule, and zero-model procedural trees and rocks.
- From Fidelity: primitive-built territory nodes, the single-uniform material transform shader, and a light-field terrain texture with only the nearest few real lights.
- From Folklore: magenta-keyed team color with no other pink allowed, creeps made from crockery primitives, and the post-fight pickup race.

**Producer** picked **fidelity**. I weighted producibility highest. Lantern & Stone is the safest concept to turn into a playable vertical slice with roughly 29 AI meshes. Every animated character is a humanoid biped on one shared, retargeted animation library. Its only non-bipeds are a wheeled cart and a rolling boulder, both procedural, with no flyers, quadrupeds or air layer. Its signature systems are cheap and deterministic. One stone_amount shader uniform re-skins the whole Mossback faction for day and night, and Lanternlight is a field texture read by the terrain shader, with real lights only on the nearest few sources. Many extra assets cost no new meshes: lantern posts, the Gloomstone, the Elder summons, the boss and the Sunsplinter creeps. It also has the strongest WC3 feel, because day/night, creeps, a shop, upkeep and two heroes per side map cleanly. Its readability at night is the best of the four: warm amber dots against cyan eyes, tall vertical shapes against wide boulders. Its weaknesses are fixable at the design stage. Give each unit a sharper identity so it reads as less of a WC3 slot clone, finish the trademark checks, and keep the troll look strictly to the folklore version.

Resonance is the most original and well thought through on art reuse. However, it needs the most rigged bipeds (15), has the riskiest rig shapes (moth wing-cloaks, the bell skirt) and carries the heaviest systems: the Toll is timed to the music, SFX are pitch-snapped, and the AI music must hold an exact tempo. Maize & Bone has comparable asset scope but carries cultural-sensitivity risk, spindly Downcourt limbs that image-to-3D handles poorly, and two flyers with arm-wings. Hush & Holler is very readable and has excellent zero-model environment ideas. Against it: an air unit, no name checks, close look-alikes and a farm-game tone.

Ideas to bring into the winner from the others:
- From Resonance: Echo-style replays made with a shader on existing meshes, and towers kitbashed from existing parts.
- From Hush & Holler: floating billboard stack indicators and the rule that a big hit breaks crowd control.
- From Maize & Bone: Mud Folk creeps that melt and reform through a vertex shader, crockery creeps built from primitives, and a standing negative prompt with a style-bible sheet per faction.
- From Maize & Bone and Hush & Holler: team color painted in a magenta key and swapped in a shader.

Weighted total with producibility counted twice: Fidelity 49, Resonance 44, Folklore 43, Wildcard 42.
